1. Introduction

This  study  investigates  the  role  of  generative  artificial  intelligence  (AI)  chatbots,  particularly
ChatGPT, in enhancing programming education for university students, specifically in big data
analytics. The  research  addresses  the  growing  need  for  innovative  educational  practices,  espe-
cially in developed East Asian countries like South Korea, where declining university enrollment
presents new challenges. Using a sample size of N = 343 students, this mixed-methods research
employed controlled experiments and surveys to compare student performance in programming
tasks across three groups: those using ChatGPT, those using Stack Overflow, and a control group
without  external  assistance.  Results  showed  that  students  using  ChatGPT  significantly  out-
performed those relying on Stack Overflow or no assistance, particularly in hands-on coding tasks.
This  research  contributes  to  the  ongoing  discourse  on  AI  in  education  by  providing  empirical
evidence of generative AI’s effectiveness in improving learning outcomes and engagement, while
also  highlighting  the  challenges  associated  with  integrating  AI  into  educational  settings.  The
findings emphasize the potential of ChatGPT to personalize learning experiences, improve per-
formance, and offer real-time support, underscoring the need for a balanced curriculum design
that incorporates AI while maintaining academic integrity and human oversight.

The integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly ChatGPT by OpenAI, has emerged as a pivotal development in
various domains, including higher education. As noted by Dowling and Lucey (2023), Eke (2023), and Lim, Gunasekara, et al. (2023),
these AI systems are significantly impacting diverse areas through their data-driven content generation capabilities. ChatGPT utilizes
deep learning to generate natural language text, having been trained on a vast corpus of online text data. This ability to generate
human-like text makes it a valuable tool for numerous writing tasks. It should be noted that AI technology provides a more person-
alized user experience (Virvou, 2023), and as natural language processing technology advances, strategies for leveraging generative AI
chatbots are becoming increasingly sophisticated (Dwivedi et al., 2023).

The International Journal of Management Education (IJME) recently highlighted the impact of new generative AI on improving
educational and business training opportunities. For example, Lim, Azmat, and Voola (2023) examined the paradoxical impact of
generative AI on education, specifically whether it heralds a new era of educational transformation or poses significant risks that could
disrupt the current educational paradigm. The study extends paradox theory to the context of educational technology, demonstrating

Exploratory studies on the utilization and application of generative AI are promising. However, questions remain about how this
interest translates into practical outcomes. Specifically, the impact of generative AI on higher education, which is currently facing
significant challenges, is not well understood. This raises the need to move beyond general interest in the benefits and acceptance of
ChatGPT to observe the concrete changes that occur when it is integrated into actual educational processes.

The ongoing decrease in university students in developed East Asian countries, such as South Korea, signifies a transformative shift
in global higher education, emphasizing sustainability (Hyun Yoo, 2023). This decline may lead to a shrinking education market,
reduced  investment  efficiency  in  management,  and  difficulties  in  recruiting  teaching  staff,  raising  new  concerns  about  a  cyclical
decline  in  educational  quality  (Kim  &  Ilon,  2020).  This  shift  underscores  the  need  for  sustainable  approaches  to  changing  de-
mographics  and  educational  demands (Wang &  Fukuda,  2019). Higher  education institutions  must move  away  from  focusing on
quantity to fostering diverse and dynamic learning environments (Goralski & Tan, 2020). The declining student population in South
Korea foreshadows a rise in international students, impacting nations with aging populations, including Japan, the UK, and France
(Koh, 2023). However, these efforts are often hindered by significant barriers such as language and cultural differences, which can
limit students’ opportunities to actively receive help from instructors in programming and data analysis education (Hong, 2017).

The utilization of generative artificial intelligence applications like ChatGPT and AI-based translation services, such as DeepL and
Google Translator, is already evident in the field of education (Daun & Brings, 2023; Loos et al., 2023; Rawas, 2023; Zhu et al., 2023).
The increasing interest in the potential of generative AI in education and related sectors can be attributed to the shifting usage patterns
among both international and domestic students (Wang et al., 2023). Rather than relying solely on AI applications for assignment
completion or class attendance, these students are now turning to platforms such as ChatGPT’s code assistant features and GitHub’s
Copilot  for  information  retrieval  or  programming  assistance.  However,  there  remains  a  dearth  of  comprehension  regarding  how
generative AI can enhance educational administration, curriculum development, pedagogy, and student learning specifically within
the realm of data science education.

The  importance  of  practical  exercises  in  programming  education  cannot  be  overlooked.  It  is  well-known  that  students  have
traditionally utilized online knowledge repositories to solve coding practice assignments. Beyond textbooks and open-source projects,
these repositories are organized in a question-and-answer format and evaluated based on user feedback. Despite the existence of such
valuable resources, the actual benefit of generative AI in this context needs careful examination. Previous studies on the educational
utility  of  generative  AI  have  warned  that  tools  like  ChatGPT  might  lead  to  negative  outcomes  due  to  occasionally  incorrect  or
incomplete answers, the lack of a real programming environment, and a possible negative impact on the development of critical
thinking skills (Yilmaz & Yilmaz, 2023). Although not limited to programming assignments, concerns regarding student cheating and
the originality of their work are also important issues that need careful consideration (Ratten & Jones, 2023). Knowledge repositories
like StackOverflow have the potential to mitigate these risks by encouraging practices such as proper citation of sources. In line with
Ratten and Jones (2023), Lim, Azmat, and Voola (2023) also acknowledged that generative AI does not solely possess positive at-
tributes in the realm of education. More assertively, Duong, Vu, et al. (2024) reported that the use of ChatGPT can be a stress-inducing
factor, leading to reduced student life satisfaction. In sum, there is a lack of substantial and long-term comparative data on the effects of
ChatGPT in programming education. Consequently, questions arise regarding its value as a viable technological solution for addressing
real-world issues in this field.

This study seeks to bridge the research gap concerning the influence of artificial intelligence on machine learning and big data
programming education at the university level, while also examining its potential to enhance pedagogical methods. In this study, we
aim  to  uncover  the  prospective  advantages  of  artificial intelligence  in  facilitating  personalized  learning  experiences  for  students.
Furthermore, this study emphasizes the necessity for educators to adopt a more interactive and student-centered approach to cur-
riculum design, transitioning from traditional, one-way lectures to a more dynamic and engaging learning environment. In addition,
this research acknowledges the imperative to devise strategies for effectively interpreting and responding to AI-generated suggestions,
explanations, and modifications of student-written code, while also tackling the challenges posed by hallucination issues in generative
AI. The findings of this study will contribute to the development of specific guidelines that can inform educational practices and
mitigate these unique challenges within the field.

2. Background

2.1. Higher eudcation and challenges in South Korea

South Korea has experienced rapid economic growth in recent decades. Following the devastation of the Korean War in the 1950s,
the country initially relied heavily on agricultural, forestry, and mining sectors due to a lack of industrial infrastructure. However,
South  Korea  swiftly  embarked  on  a  path  of  industrialization  and  achieved  remarkable  progress.  Notably,  it  made  significant  ad-
vancements in telecommunications, internet services, and entertainment industries. This development propelled South Korea into the
ranks of developed nations by 2020.

Meanwhile, the most significant challenges that could hinder South Korea’s long-term success are its declining and aging popu-
lation.  South  Korea  heavily  relies  on imported  raw  materials  for  its  economy,  particularly  in  processing  these  materials  to  make
products. This necessitates the utilization of advanced technologies to enhance their value, thus highlighting the importance of a
consistent supply of highly educated workers in the industry. However, South Korea is currently grappling with a rapidly decreasing
population. The effectiveness of educational methods employed during the baby boom era is diminishing, resulting in a decline in the
quality of education services. Given the increasing significance of higher education, enhancing individual student performance has
become an imperative matter.

According to a 2023 analysis conducted by the South Korean Ministry of Education and the Korea Education Development Institute,
not only has the number of higher education institutions teaching students decreased, but the number of students enrolled in education
has been on the decline since 2011 (KOSIS, 2023). Since 2021, the number of international students has increased for three consecutive
years and now exceeds 180,000 (Son, 2023). The number of students enrolled in higher education institutions decreased by 17.1%
from 2014 to 2023. The number of international students rose from approximately 50,000 in 2014 to around 130,000 in 2023. During
this time, tuition fees at South Korean universities were suspended for political reasons, so the influx of international students became
the primary means by which universities overcame financial difficulties. This situation is anticipated to deteriorate.

According to the latest statistics from South Korea’s Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) and the Korea Institute of
Advanced Industrial Technology (KIAT), the ratio of industrial technology workers to the total number of workers was 34.1% in 2018,
up 1.7% from the year before the survey (KIAT, 2019). Among the industries driving new business growth in Korea, ICT-related sectors
such as software and electronics have driven this increase, despite a decreasing labor force, with a 72.3% shortage in 2015 and a 73.3%
shortage  in  2017.  The  software  industry  contributed  21.8%  and  the  electronics  industry  contributed  18.8%.  Because  of  Korea’s
increasing reliance on the ICT industry, it is unable to provide companies with sufficient workers with advanced degrees. This, coupled
with the worsening financial situation of universities and the deteriorating quality of higher education, is a challenge that will be
difficult to surmount soon.

2.2. AI in education

The use of AI in education is rapidly increasing, enhancing learning experiences and personalizing educational content. This rise in
AI adoption has spurred  significant research  efforts aimed at improving  education through AI. According  to Seldon and Abidoye
(2018), AI effectively enhances learning experiences and customizes educational materials to meet individual student needs. This
phenomenon has led to increased scholarly investigations focused on leveraging AI to enhance educational methodologies (Chichekian
& Benteux, 2022). The development of tools like the Generative Artificial Intelligence Acceptance Scale, as described by Yilmaz et al.
(2023), underscores the importance of measuring students’  acceptance of AI technologies in educational contexts. Augmented in-
telligence, exemplified by tools like ChatGPT, has shown promising results in programming education, providing benefits such as quick
responses, assistance with debugging, and enhancing thinking skills (Yilmaz & Yilmaz, 2023). AI and social media can enhance ac-
ademic performance and mental well-being through personalized learning experiences and emotional support, although the synergy of
these technologies requires further exploration (Shahzad et al., 2024). Furthermore, the use of ChatGPT has been shown to improve
computational thinking, programming self-efficacy, and student motivation, demonstrating the transformative potential of AI in ed-
ucation (Yilmaz &  Yilmaz, 2023). In management education, generative AI tools like ChatGPT can enhance teaching and learning
processes (Ratten & Jones, 2023). The adaptation of the Artificial Intelligence Literacy Scale highlights the importance of AI literacy,
enabling better interaction with AI technologies and understanding their impacts (Polatgil & Güler, 2023). Responsible Management
Education (RME) integrates sustainability and social responsibility into business curricula, leveraging AI and gamification to promote
innovative learning experiences (Lim, Azmat, & Voola, 2023).

The aforementioned phenomenon has prompted a notable increase in scholarly investigations (e.g., Chichekian & Benteux, 2022)
focused on leveraging AI to improve educational methodologies. Through the utilization of various computing technologies, including
intelligent education, innovative virtual learning, and data analysis and prediction, artificial intelligence (AI) has the potential to
improve the quality and effectiveness of educational processes (L. Chen, Xie, et al., 2020; Kahraman et al., 2010).

AI has emerged as a promising tool for enhancing the value and performance of learning (X. Chen, Xie, et al., 2020). By integrating
information technologies such as intelligent learning planners, virtual instructors, and data analysis and prediction algorithms, AI has
the potential to revolutionize education. Various AI-assisted educational services, including ActiveMath, MATHia, and Why2Atlas,
have been developed as interactive learning environments that not only monitor student progress but also facilitate goal management,
enhance teaching tools, and promote global communication and feedback between instructors and students (Chassignol et al., 2018).
Additionally, AI-driven applications like Grammarly, Ecree, and PaperRater have proven beneficial for foreign students by assisting
them with administrative tasks (Johnson et al., 2010).

Research has demonstrated the efficacy of learner-centered AI applications such as Deep Tutor and Auto Tutor in tailoring content
to the specific abilities and needs of university students (Rus et al., 2013). By providing customized and personalized learning ex-
periences, these AI tools have the potential to enhance educational outcomes and potentially contribute to improved academic per-
formance (Mikropoulos & Natsis, 2011; Sharma et al., 2019). Moreover, the advent of online learning has facilitated the development
of AI-powered teaching tools that can transcend geographical boundaries, enabling international students to benefit from language
translation technologies like Google Translate. A study conducted by Tsai (Tsai, 2019) on Chinese college students learning English
revealed that Google Translate assisted foreign learners in utilizing more advanced vocabulary and reducing spelling and grammar
errors. Additionally,  employing virtual reality,  3D technologies, and  gamification techniques in instruction has not only  elevated
teaching standards but also increased efficiency in catering to the needs of international students (Lee & Hammer, 2011).

Numerous  scholarly  investigations  have  substantiated  the  capacity  of  AI  to  augment  the  educational  experiences  of  students.
Chassignol et al. (Chassignol et al., 2018) have conducted a study on the utilization of AI in various educational applications, including
Knewton, Cerego, Immersive Reader, and CALL. These applications employ machine learning algorithms to offer immediate recom-
mendations and customize educational resources to cater to the specific requirements of learners across different educational stages,
spanning from preschool to graduate school. In a similar vein, Pokriv˙c´akov´a (Pokrivcakova, 2019) has emphasized the efficacy of
chatbots that leverage AI algorithms to tailor learning materials to the specific needs and capabilities of students. Furthermore, the
utilization of AI technologies such as Pearson’s Write-to-Learn and Turnitin has been implemented to enhance academic integrity
through the provision of support for editing and writing activities, in addition to supporting tailored learning experiences. Never-
theless, Crowe et al. (Crowe et al., 2017) express concern regarding the potential unintended consequences of these tools, as they may
unwittingly promote unethical behaviors by guiding students towards websites or platforms that facilitate the process of creating
papers, so potentially undermining the principles of academic integrity.

The integration of AI powered translators and writing tools, voice recognition and dictation tools, as well as language learning
tools, can potentially enhance the language acquisition journey for non-native English-speaking international students, especially
when pursuing education in English-speaking countries. An illustrative instance is the combination of Google Translate, Grammarly
and even ChatGPT, an application designed to aid in writing tasks, which can facilitate the process of composing academic content for
international students, faculty members, and researchers within an academic environment (ONeill & Russell, 2019; Pratama, 2021).
Additionally, other resources like ProWritingAid, Quillbot, and Ginger offer valuable assistance for English learners who are specif-
ically focused on developing their academic writing skills for educational purposes (Erkan, 2022; Nurmayanti & Suryadi, 2023; Tran &
Nguyen, 2021). Numerous studies have provided evidence to support the notion that the incorporation of AI discussion chatbots in
educational settings has yielded notable benefits in terms of enhancing students’ English communication proficiency. This positive
outcome can be attributed to the chatbots’  ability to provide more precise language feedback, as well as their capacity to create
additional opportunities for interactive engagement (Junaidi, 2020; Kim et al., 2019).

AI technologies present both significant opportunities and challenges in educational settings. On one hand, these technologies can
enhance learning experiences and provide innovative tools for both students and educators. On the other hand, they pose risks such as
potential negative impacts on critical thinking skills and fostering dependency (Yilmaz & Yilmaz, 2023). This paradox highlights the
necessity for a balanced and thoughtful integration of AI in education (Lim, Azmat, & Voola, 2023). Moreover, the compulsive use of AI
tools, including ChatGPT, has been linked to adverse effects such as loneliness and psychological distress, underscoring the importance
of promoting healthy technology use practices (Duong, Ngo, et al., 2024). To maximize the benefits of AI in educational environments,
it is essential to understand the interaction between effort expectancy and performance expectancy, ensuring these tools are used
effectively (Duong et al., 2023). Duong, Ngo, et al. (2024) conducted a study using a survey of 2602 ChatGPT users in Vietnam, to
investigate the adverse effects of compulsive ChatGPT use on mental well-being, revealing that compulsive use increases technostress
and  decreases  life  satisfaction,  with  technology  anxiety  moderating  these  effects,  thereby  extending  the  Stressor-Strain-Outcome
model and highlighting the need for strategies to mitigate these negative consequences.

In sum, the integration of AI in education offers substantial benefits, including personalized learning experiences and improved
educational outcomes. However, it also presents challenges such as potential dependency and negative impacts on critical thinking
skills. The dual nature of AI necessitates a balanced approach to its adoption, ensuring that its use promotes positive educational and
psychological  outcomes.  Promoting  healthy  technology  use  practices  is  crucial  to  mitigating  the  adverse  effects  associated  with
compulsive use, such as loneliness and psychological distress. Understanding the nuanced interplay between effort expectancy and
performance expectancy can help in optimizing the effectiveness of AI tools in educational settings. Thus, a thoughtful and measured
integration of AI technologies is essential to fully harness their potential while minimizing their drawbacks. Further research is needed
to explore these dynamics and develop strategies that maximize the benefits while mitigating the risks associated with AI in education.

2.3. Training programming skills with AI assistance

There has been a research stream that investigates the possible benefits of using AI systems in schools (L. Chen, Xie, et al., 2020).
One of the main perks is that learning goes faster and is more accurate. AI systems can quickly look through huge amounts of data and
find complex patterns and links that students might find hard to see due to the lack of experiences and hints. AI systems can also help
make personalized learning paths (Chen et al., 2023; Hsu et al., 2023). More specifically, by looking at how and why students learn, AI
systems can give each student personalized help and advice that fits their specific needs. This makes it easier for them to reach their
educational goals. AI systems can also make online learning easier, which means that students who live in remote places can get the
same access to education as other students (Akgun & Greenhow, 2021, pp. 1–10; Chichekian & Benteux, 2022). This encourages justice
and inclusion by letting everyone get a chance of better education.

Concurrently,  there  are  continuous  deliberations  concerning  the  obstacles  that  necessitate  resolution  for  the  effective  imple-

in higher education, all call for a comprehensive examination of the potential challenges that may arise with the active integration of
AI as an educational tool.

Zhang and Aslan (Zhang & Aslan, 2021) conducted an extensive investigation on the application of AI in education and its potential
advantages. Their study highlighted several benefits, such as increased student engagement and motivation, enhanced testing pro-
cedures with immediate feedback, as well as improved efficiency and cost-effectiveness of the educational process. Similarly, Pedro
et al. (Pedro et al., 2019) discussed the merits of integrating AI into education in their scholarly article. The authors concurred that
leveraging AI technology in educational settings could automate mundane tasks, allocate more time to challenging cases, and equip
individuals with new skills necessary for effectively collaborating with AI systems. Pedro et al. (Pedro et al., 2019) discussed the
challenges and policy implications associated with the implementation of AI in educational settings and the preparation of students for
real-world situations. The authors identified several key issues that require attention, including ensuring equitable and inclusive access
for all individuals. According to Pedro et al. (Pedro et al., 2019), concerns regarding AI in education encompass unequal access to
educational systems resulting from machine learning algorithms, inadequate guidance provided to specific student groups, excessive
retention of personal data, liability concerns, and the potential impact of AI automation on teachers’ roles. Safeguarding personally
identifiable information and privacy settings poses a significant challenge, particularly when dealing with young students who may not
possess the capacity to provide informed consent.

Programming with the help of artificial intelligence provides a certain level of freedom in ethical concerns regarding the content, as
AI  can  serve  as  a  limited  guide  by  combining  publicly  available  algorithms,  code  snippets,  documentation,  and  development
knowledge to provide explanations or showcase exemplary or sample code. While using AI in coding education may not completely
solve or eliminate all issues related to the use of AI in educational settings, considering the practical environment and conditions of
current generative AI, it is believed that it can provide substantial assistance in university students’ education while also offering a rich
information environment for information system researchers.

2.4. Related theories

Previous studies have consistently shown that facilitating conditions significantly aid the integration of generative AI in learning
processes. Yilmaz et al. (2023) utilized the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) model to develop a scale
measuring  students’  acceptance  of  generative  AI  applications.  The  UTAUT  model  significantly  contributed  to  these  findings  by
providing a comprehensive framework to assess the key factors affecting technology acceptance, such as performance expectancy,
effort  expectancy,  facilitating  conditions,  and  social  influence,  which  were  all  crucial  in  understanding  students’  acceptance  of
generative AI in educational contexts (Yilmaz et al., 2023). Yilmaz and Yilmaz (2023) examined this phenomenon by analyzing student
perspectives on using ChatGPT in programming learning. Their research highlighted several advantages, such as quick and effective
responses to questions and enhanced debugging support, demonstrating how facilitating conditions like 24/7 availability of AI tools
can positively impact learning experiences. The concept of augmented intelligence, which combines human and artificial intelligence
to enhance decision-making processes, is evident here as ChatGPT supports and improves students’ programming skills. These findings
underscore the importance of augmented intelligence in education, where human decision-making is enhanced through AI support,
ultimately contributing to improved learning outcomes and increased confidence in programming skills.

Previous studies have consistently shown that facilitating conditions significantly aid the integration of generative AI in learning
processes. Shahzad et al. (2024) examined the impact of AI and social media on students’ academic performance and mental well-being
through the lens of Bandura’s Social Learning Theory (SLT). Their research revealed that AI positively influences academic perfor-
mance by offering personalized learning experiences and real-time feedback, while AI tools like chatbots enhance mental well-being by
providing emotional support and reducing stress. The concept of augmented intelligence, which blends human and AI capabilities, is
evident as these technologies support and enhance student learning and well-being in smart learning environments (Shahzad et al.,
2024).

Ratten and Jones (2023) explored the implications of using ChatGPT in management education, focusing on how generative AI can
enhance  teaching  and  learning  processes.  They  argue  that AI  tools  like  ChatGPT  offer  significant  benefits  by  providing  real-time
feedback and personalized learning paths, thereby enhancing the overall learning experience. However, the study also highlights
challenges such as the potential for cheating and the need for educators to design more creative and context-specific assessments to
ensure academic integrity. The core theory revolves around digital transformation and educational technology integration, empha-
sizing the need for management educators to adapt their methodologies to incorporate these new technologies while maintaining
educational standards and integrity. Furthermore, the study extends the literature on management education by proposing a new
framework that incorporates generative AI technologies, aiming to foster creativity, critical thinking, and ethical usage among stu-
dents.  It  offers  practical  guidelines  for  management  educators,  such  as  using  real-life  examples  and  scenarios  in  assessments  to
contextualize learning and make it harder for AI to provide generic responses.

necessary for navigating the AI-integrated digital landscape. The validated Turkish AILS provides a crucial tool for educators, poli-
cymakers, and researchers to assess and enhance AI literacy among Turkish-speaking individuals, aiding in the evaluation of AI ed-
ucation programs and identifying areas for further training. This adaptation fills a critical gap in the literature, offering a robust
instrument for future research and practical applications in AI education.

Lim,  Azmat,  and  Voola  (2023) explore  the  paradoxical  impact  of  generative  AI  on  education,  questioning  whether  it  heralds
educational transformation or poses significant disruptive risks. The study employs paradox theory to understand the contradictory yet
interrelated elements of generative AI in education. The key findings highlight several paradoxes: First, generative AI serves as both a
’friend’ and a ’foe’. As a friend, it facilitates knowledge acquisition and supports educational tasks such as writing essays and coding.
However,  as  a  foe,  it  raises  concerns  about  originality  and  plagiarism,  potentially  undermining  traditional  assessment  methods.
Second, generative AI is both ’capable’ and ’dependent’. While capable of generating detailed responses across subjects, it is heavily
dependent on the quality and specificity of input prompts and the data on which it was trained. Third, generative AI is both ’accessible’
and ’restrictive’. It democratizes access to educational resources, potentially reducing educational inequities, yet premium versions
and limited access can exacerbate existing inequalities among students. Finally, bans on AI tools like ChatGPT often lead to increased
interest and usage among students, a phenomenon paralleling the Streisand effect and psychological reactance. The study extends
paradox theory to the context of educational technology, demonstrating how generative AI embodies conflicting characteristics that
educators must navigate.

Previous studies have highlighted the technology acceptance of generative AI models like ChatGPT during the learning process,

The field of programming is characterized by its complexity, requiring individuals to acquire a comprehensive understanding of

3. Methodology

3.1. Introduction

This section outlines the methodology employed in our empirical study, which aims to investigate the impact of generative AI,
specifically ChatGPT, on the learning outcomes of programming students. The study is designed to compare the efficacy of traditional
knowledge repositories, such as StackOverflow, with generative AI in supporting programming education. To achieve this, we con-
ducted a series of controlled experiments involving three distinct groups of students: those receiving support from ChatGPT, those
using StackOverflow, and a control group with no external assistance. The primary objective was to evaluate whether the use of
ChatGPT could significantly enhance learning efficiency and performance compared to conventional methods.

The study participants were first- and second-year university students majoring in business administration at a university in Seoul,
South Korea. The participants were divided into three groups based on the type of support they received: ChatGPT, StackOverflow, and
no assistance. Each group underwent a learning process involving a series of tasks designed to teach them programming skills using the
R package dplyr. Data collection involved both quantitative and qualitative methods. Quantitative data were gathered from the quiz
scores and analyzed using statistical techniques to compare the performance across different groups. Additionally, interviews were
administered to collect qualitative data on the students’ experiences and perceptions of the learning support they received. To provide
a comprehensive analysis, we employed Item Response Theory (IRT) to evaluate the validity and reliability of the quiz items. This
approach helped us measure the discrimination and difficulty parameters of each quiz item, ensuring that our assessment accurately
reflected the students’ learning progress.

3.2. Tasks

To assess the academic performance of the students, they were instructed to complete 10 quizzes, each corresponding to a specific
learning module. These quizzes were evaluated by checking their responses. The participants were assigned the task of acquiring
proficiency in using the R programming package known as dplyr, which is specifically designed for data manipulation. This learning
process spanned a period of two weeks. After the completion of each module, quizzes were administered to record the grades achieved
by the students.

The initial step of Reading Semantics aims to provide a theoretical understanding of fundamental concepts. Students learn about
data and key functions, utilizing the data package nycflights13 which contains information on flight departures and arrivals in New
York airports. Additionally, the characteristics and significance of dplyr functions are explained, although the direct usage of these
functions is not demonstrated. Instead, emphasis is placed on illustrating how inputs and outputs are affected by the functionalities
performed by these functions with text descriptions. In the phase of Writing Semantics, the primary objective is to facilitate the
conversion of lucid and meticulous natural language explanations of language constructs into a syntactic form that can be compiled
and executed as intended. In this process, students engage in the analysis of input and output tables presented in Excel as illustrative
instances. They deliberate upon the necessary steps involved in converting input data into desired output, while also exploring the
means to achieve this objective through the utilization of dplyr’s function names. Reading Templates consist of source code and
comments explaining how they work. They provide brief glimpses into the functioning principles of the key functions within dplyr and
their resulting outcomes by showcasing various examples. Additionally, they also explain basic arithmetic functions included in R and
provide descriptions of data.frame objects. Finally, in the Writing Templates, students are required to perform data manipulation
tasks using the nycflights13 dataset and utilize RStudio provided by posit.cloud to write Rscript files. Table 3 provides a brief illus-
tration of the learning process for combining select(.), filter(.), and mutate(.) functions in R.

The experimental participants consisted of first- and second-year university students, specifically business administration majors at
a university in Seoul, South Korea. They were divided into two groups based on their class attendance period: one group attended

groups is presented in Table 5.

The course being offered in this study was titled "Business Data Analytics," which was an elective course for students pursuing their
respective majors. The instructional format included online lectures and offline labs. It is worth noting that the average grades across
the different student groups were relatively consistent. The class consists of multiple groups, each comprising a similar number of
students (N = 110, 109 and 124 respectively). There were 206 male students (60%) and 137 female students. Most of the participants
were majoring in management (76%), as the data was collected from courses that were major-specific within the business school.
Additionally, 15% of the students were majoring in engineering, and 31% were majoring in natural sciences or fine arts. Among the
343 students, 101 were international students, making up 29% of the total. The average GPA of the students varied by class but ranged
between 3.4 and 3.7 on a 4.5 scale. The average programming skill level of students who participated in 2022 ranged between 0.18 and
0.21, where 1 indicated experience and 0 indicated none. This figure rose to 0.71 in 2023, likely due to a newly introduced Python
coding course in the business administration department.

It is important to highlight that the data collected from Group A during October 2022 was not pertinent to ChatGPT due to its
temporal context. Conversely, the data obtained from Group B in April 2023 holds relevance to ChatGPT as it was collected when
ChatGPT had gained widespread recognition. Moreover, during this period, students had the opportunity to leverage ChatGPT as one
of the lectures specifically addressed its usage within the lecture (see Fig. 1).

learning. To gain a deeper understanding of this phenomenon, we conducted supplementary interviews with a sample of 24 students.
These interviews aimed to investigate the differences between using ChatGPT as a collaborative tool, combined with the knowledge
repository of Stack Overflow, versus relying solely on traditional classroom-based learning methods. In this study, the evaluation of
learning methods has involved considering factors such as ease of use, usefulness, and satisfaction, drawing from concepts in the
technology acceptance literature (Phan & Daim, 2011; Saadi et al., 2017; Venkatesh et al., 2003). To comprehensively evaluate the
process of selecting learning methods in our study, we administered a survey alongside the interviews. This allowed us to gather
detailed information on alternative judgments and subsequently analyze this data using the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) method.
AHP facilitates the partitioning of a decision problem into a hierarchical structure consisting of numerous qualities or criteria. This
hierarchical structure is designed such that the higher-level parts encompass more specific elements, hence increasing the amount of
detail  and  granularity  (Saaty,  1990,  2008;  Zahedi,  1986).  AHP  has  demonstrated  its  effectiveness  in  addressing  a  wide  range  of
intricate decision-making challenges (Zahedi, 1986). Numerous previous studies have employed AHP for the goal of ranking various
entities. Colace et al. (Colace et al., 2006) applied the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) method while studying an e-learning platform.
Chen Hsieh and Do (Chen et al., 2015) introduce a novel framework for assessing teaching performance in higher education. This
framework integrates the fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and fuzzy comprehensive evaluation methods to establish a teaching
performance index system.

The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) is a versatile methodology that can be applied to address a range of practical problems.
Examples  include  the  selection  of  suitable  building  sites,  the  development  of  evaluation  criteria  for  performance  appraisals,  the
formulation of public policies, and the decision-making process for launching new products in the market. AHP employs a ratio scale to
measure relative importance or preferences, enabling the derivation of quantitative results. The execution of AHP involves several
steps. Firstly, the problem’s goal is defined. Subsequently, a hierarchical structure is constructed, encompassing criteria and alter-
natives for evaluation. Pairwise comparison matrices are then created using reciprocal ratios. These matrices serve as the basis for
calculating relative importance and assessing coherence and consistency through the computation of a consistency index (CI). If the CI
falls below an acceptable threshold, further discussions with respondents are necessary to address any inconsistencies. Finally, ei-
genvalues derived from these calculations are synthesized to determine the relative importance among alternative options.

The initial inquiry pertains to the extent to which ease of use aids learners in their learning process. We conducted interviews in
which participants were requested to compare and assess the relative significance of traditional lectures (Base), support through Stack
Overflow, and utilization of ChatGPT on a scale ranging from 1 to 9, with regards to ease of use. In the analysis, the comparative
outcomes were expressed as standardized eigenvalues that range from 0 to 1. The consistency index (CI) was computed based on the
eigenvalues provided by  each respondent,  and if it  was below 0.1, the responses were considered consistent. To calculate the  CI
promptly as participants provided their answers, we developed an R-shiny app and re-evaluated any areas where response consistency
was compromised. Additionally, we inquired about the reasons underlying these assessments. From the perspective of ease of use,
lectures  were  perceived  as  having  the  highest  importance  in  facilitating  learning  (0.598),  while  Stack  Overflow  was  regarded  as
presenting the greatest challenge (0.067).

In the evaluation of the usefulness aspect, a similar approach was applied as before, where respondents were asked to rate the
relative importance on a scale of 1–9 and provide reasons for their ratings. The average lambda values, which indicate response
consistency,  were  below  0.1,  showing  consistent  responses.  The  results  revealed  that  ChatGPT  (0.565),  Base  (0.360),  and  Stack
Overflow (0.075) were ranked in that order.

4.3. Reasons of releative preference to ChatGPT in coding learning

The interview results with 24 students, conducted to gather data for AHP analysis, consistently highlighted the enjoyment and
companion-like  feeling  of  using  ChatGPT,  as  well  as  its  kindness,  as  significant  advantages.  It  was  noted  that  ChatGPT  offers  a
noticeably different experience compared to traditional means of accessing information or simply relying on web search engine results.
The notion of considering ChatGPT as a close peer to overcome the challenges of learning together is particularly intriguing.

As a business analytics major, not really coming from a computer science background, I’ve gotta say, getting good at programming is
pretty tough. It’s like, I know it’s important, but it’s definitely a challenge to pick up all these coding skills when you’re not in a hardcore
tech major, you know? Talking to my professor definitely helps, but they’re usually so swamped, it’s hard to get a lot of time with them.
That’s where ChatGPT comes in super handy. It’s always there to give me detailed answers. Even when I’m totally lost on some topics, I
just keep asking questions, and studying feels less overwhelming. It’s like having this 24/7 helpdesk that makes everything clearer for me.

It can be challenging to determine what to study when handling data and understanding the procedures involved, especially when
it’s a topic that students are not familiar with. Initially, understanding what ChatGPT is saying and knowing what questions to ask can
be difficult, and searching on Stack Overflow may also pose challenges in terms of interpreting the results and comprehending complex
information. However, having lecture slides in PowerPoint format with explanations from the professor can be helpful due to their
clear examples.

Xie et al. (2019) recommended a structured approach to programming learning, segmenting it into Reading Semantics, Writing
Semantics, Reading Templates, and Writing Templates. They advocated for a gradual progression through these stages. In teaching
coding with R for data manipulation, we adopted this learning sequence. We also facilitated access to Stack Overflow and ChatGPT for
learner support. Our students appreciated traditional lecture-based methods for Reading Semantics and Writing Semantics. However,
they found generative AI support immensely beneficial for hands-on coding in the Writing Templates phase.

Whenever I need examples, ChatGPT is right on it. And the best part? If I need something tweaked, it doesn’t just tell me – it shows me the
code and the results. I don’t have to test each example myself. It’s been awesome for understanding how the code really works, especially
with  different  functions.  Like,  when  I  was  messing  around  with  str_match_all(.)  in  stringr  and  got  stuck  with  regular  expressions,
ChatGPT came through with spot-on examples and easy-to-get explanations. Seriously, it’s made studying a whole lot easier for me.

The stuff my professor shows in class? Super clear and really helpful. But, when I try coding on my own, it’s mistake city. It’s pretty
embarrassing, honestly. Asking others what I’m doing wrong is tough, and even with some Stack Overflow help, I end up Googling like
crazy to figure out my errors. I didn’t want to bug my friends with non-stop questions, and finding help late at night? Almost impossible.
Emailing professors is tricky too, ’cause sometimes I don’t even know what to ask. But ChatGPT? It’s a game-changer. It gets me, even
when my questions are kind of vague. I drop in some code or a half-baked explanation, and it just rolls with it, giving me exactly the kind
of conversation I need.

A common impression we gathered during the interviews is that students were fascinated by the utility provided by ChatGPT. They
also emphasized the ongoing need for lectures and communication with professors. While Stack Overflow offers many useful tips,
having  interactive  features  that provide  more examples  and  explanations  for  each  problem  proved  to  be  more helpful.  Having  a
companion who engages in coding alongside interactive explanations, rather than just searching and reading from a knowledge re-
pository, was greatly appreciated.

5. Conclusion

5.1. Summary

This study explores the impact of generative AI, specifically ChatGPT, on enhancing the self-learning capabilities of university
students in programming, particularly in the context of big data analytics education. The research examines how AI tools like ChatGPT
can revolutionize programming education by providing personalized and interactive learning experiences. Using a mixed-methods
approach, the study compares the effectiveness of traditional learning methods, Stack Overflow, and ChatGPT in supporting pro-
gramming education. The results indicate that students who used ChatGPT showed significantly better academic performance than
those who relied on Stack Overflow or had no external assistance. The study also delves into the ethical implications of AI in education
and  emphasizes  the importance  of  a  balanced,  student-centered curriculum design  that thoughtfully integrates  AI tools. Through
controlled experiments and interviews, the study finds that generative AI not only aids in learning efficiency but also enhances student
engagement by providing immediate, context-specific feedback. However, the research also highlights the need for human oversight to
mitigate potential risks such as dependency on AI and the spread of misinformation due to AI-generated errors.

5.2. Contribution

The study conducted by Wang et al. (2023) presents a comprehensive investigation into the impact of artificial intelligence on
international student education. The researchers thoroughly explore various applications of AI in this context and highlight significant
considerations such as privacy, cultural differences, language skills, and ethical implications. Similar to their findings, our research
also revealed that the capabilities of generative AI provide significant assistance to learners. However, unlike Wang et al. (2023), we
compared long-term learning processes and quantitatively assessed the areas in which learners benefited based on decision models,
while also striving to gain a more detailed understanding through interviews. The findings of this study underscore the necessity of
integrating AI tools like ChatGPT into the curriculum design of programming courses. Such integration should not be superficial but
should involve a thorough re-evaluation of teaching methodologies to ensure that AI complements, rather than replaces, traditional
instructional methods. This approach will require educators to focus on developing students’ critical thinking and problem-solving
skills, ensuring that they can effectively interpret and utilize AI-generated suggestions.

The impact of utilizing an AI chatbot in the learning process was observed to be significant. This was evident through its ability to
provide a wide range of coding examples, thereby enhancing students’  understanding. Additionally, the interactive nature of the
chatbot encouraged students to ask questions actively, leading to increased interest and enjoyment in the learning experience. The
integration of artificial intelligence in educational settings goes beyond this specific instance and includes personalized learning ap-
proaches,  intelligent  tutoring  systems,  and  improved  monitoring  of  the  learning  process  and  interactions  between  learners  and
teachers (Kamalov et al., 2023). Our empirical research findings demonstrate the considerable value of generative artificial intelli-
gence based on LLM for educational or training purposes. We believe that it holds promising potential in meeting the demand for
high-quality higher education amidst anticipated population decline.

This study not only revalidates prior research findings but also empirically demonstrates the practical utility of generative AI in
enhancing academic performance. Yilmaz et al. (2023) employed technology acceptance theories to construct a scale assessing stu-
dents’ acceptance of generative AI, emphasizing constructs such as performance expectancy and facilitating conditions. In contrast to
their  focus  on  acceptance  intention,  our  research  extends  the  discourse  to  encompass  the  tangible  impacts  of  AI  utilization  in
educational contexts. Moreover, Ratten and Jones (2023) explored the advantages and challenges associated with the integration of
ChatGPT in management education, proposing a framework that balances AI integration with the preservation of academic integrity.
Our study advances the work of Ratten and Jones (2023) by providing empirical evidence of the specific domains in which AI can offer
substantive benefits.

The findings of this study have been practically applied during workshops conducted with two partner companies, with whom one
of  the  authors  has  established  collaborative  relationships  (pseduonymously  referred  to  as  Needs  Lab  and  Reputation  Me).  These
workshops  focused  on  fine-turing  the  instruction-tuned  Google  Gemma  2B  model  to  generate  better-fitted  user  encouragement
messages based on collected reputation data. To enhance the productivity of this coding process, participants utilized Microsoft Visual
Studio Code with Github Copilot alongside ChatGPT-4o Canvas (beta version) provided  by OpenAI. As  a result,  participants suc-
cessfully adapted code examples from Hugging Face repositories and efficiently completed step-by-step tasks.

5.3. Limitation and future research

It is crucial to recognize the limitations of this study. The data analysis findings were derived from a specific context, namely, the
teaching of R for data analysis to business school students. Given the homogeneity of these students in terms of skills and learning
backgrounds, it can be argued that the internal validity and reliability of the results are relatively robust. However, caution should be
exercised when attempting to generalize these findings to other programming learning tasks or students with diverse backgrounds.
Further evidence is required to support such generalizations.

Another important limitation is the sample size and diversity. While the study involved N = 343 students, a larger and more diverse
sample would enhance the reliability and generalizability of the findings. By employing random sampling and including participants
from different disciplines and educational backgrounds, future research could strengthen the quantitative analysis and increase the
credibility of the results.

This study did not explore the potential combination of more traditional learning methods with generative AI. Methods such as
flipped learning, collaborative learning, and project-based learning could further enhance the possibilities of generative AI. However,
as we experimented with the potential of generative AI as a supplementary learning tool in a traditional lecture-based setting, we did
not provide clues about such outcomes. Further research is needed to address these limitations.

From the perspective of empirical research, the methodology and analytical approach employed in this study suggest avenues for
further refinement. We utilized direct measurements of learners’ academic performance as the primary indicator, presenting statistical
outcomes that quantify the extent of improvement. However, this approach inherently overlooks the potential for more diverse and
rigorous analyses, such as those enabled by tracking individual student performance over time. For instance, employing difference-in-
differences methodologies could facilitate a more nuanced examination of effects within a quasi-experimental framework, allowing for
detailed scrutiny of changes pre- and post-intervention. Nonetheless, such statistical approaches come with their own set of limitations
and challenges. Additionally, it is imperative to engage in qualitative research to gain a deeper understanding of the implications of AI
utilization in management education, thereby capturing the broader, more nuanced impacts that may not be readily apparent through
quantitative measures alone.
