1. Introduction

This study aims to discuss the effects of ChatGPT on the management of students’ cognitive load and learning
outcomes.  Participants  used  ChatGPT  for  answering  particular  questions,  checking  information,  and  solving
complex problems involving equations. Results show that ChatGPT lowers intrinsic and extrinsic cognitive load
with  very  detailed  responses  and  efficient  search  for  information,  even  though  it  has  difficulties  in  entering
mathematical notation. Students consider that ChatGPT is a tool able to enhance understanding, engage, and
promote critical thinking. These findings underline that the level of AI assistance must be balanced against in-
dependent learning and critical evaluation.

As we catch up on technological advancements within the market,
we are experiencing the most critical shift, especially in the landscape of
educational settings. The most recent advancement that has changed not
only the technological landscape but also educational practices is the
launch of the generative artificial intelligence (GAI) in 2022, especially
ChatGPT.  This  change  is  considered  massive  because  the  technology
gained millions of users in just five days (Alier et al., 2024). Artificial
Intelligence (AI), particularly the generative AI (GAI) is an advanced and
innovative  computerized  tool  that  simulates  information  in  a  way
similar to how humans process it. This emergence offers educators an
advantage  by  enabling  the  creation  of  resourceful  and  advanced
learning  environments  while  maintaining  the  quality  assurance  and
standards set by higher education (Eager & Brunton, 2023).

The learners commonly resort to ChatGPT whenever they are stuck
while attempting to solve mathematical word problems, as it requires
cognitive aspects (Latif et al., 2023). AI interventions have significant
potential  in  mathematics  education,  particularly  for  improving  peda-
gogy and managing cognitive load. The field of mathematics education
can benefit  greatly from AI interventions by enhancing teaching stra-
tegies  and  supporting  cognitive  load  management.  Cognitive  Load
Theory (CLT) provides a significant framework for addressing how AI
interventions  affect  cognitive  load  evolution  and  ensuring  academic
performance in mathematics education.

Hence,  artificial  intelligence  is  a  very  meaningful  subject  for  the

world to ponder upon. Like any other field, AI can have a very profound
impact on higher education (Bates et al., 2020; Demartini &  Benussi,
2017). AI will  drastically and  fundamentally change the field  of edu-
cation, especially in reducing the cognitive load of the students, which
would not impede the learning process. Using cognitive load theory in
combination with AI tools may lead to effective and optimal learning
environments.

Cognitive load represents information put or used to make mental
effort in our working memory while we engage in learning activities at a
given time. CLT is a design theory for pedagogy that highlights the way
working  memory  processes  and  stores  information  about  the  limited
capacity that brings about overloading and affects the learning outcome.
It  distinguishes  between  intrinsic,  extraneous,  and  germane  cognitive
load (Sweller, 2020).

CLT  is  built  on  an  instructional  framework  concentrating  on  the
human cognitive system and focuses on the fact that the mental capacity
held in our working memory is limited (Aditomo, 2020; Albers et al.,
2023; Kirschner & Hendrick, 2020; Roussel et al., 2017; Russo & Hop-
kins,  2017).  The  fact  that  we  could  circumvent  this  was  through  the
retrieval of significant concepts from the long-term memory and orga-
nizing numerous pieces of information into a single cognitive schema
(Baddeley, 2020; Kirschner & Hendrick, 2020).

According  to Van  Merri¨enboer  and Sweller  (2005),  CLT  is  catego-
rized  into  three  distinct  classifications:  intrinsic,  extraneous,  and
germane. The level of difficulty of the work that students must perform
is known as the intrinsic cognitive load. The task’s complexity level may

vary depending on the student’s performance; the higher the difficulty,
the  higher  the  intrinsic  load,  which  may  result  in  the  student’s  low
performance. The extraneous cognitive load refers to mental activities
and elements that do not directly contribute to the learning. That extra
burden comes from the improper design of the learning material. For
example, when related information is poorly linked or organized, the
extra burden would be an added load of unnecessary work since it re-
quires  conducting  irrelevant  searches  to  find  information  required.
Germane  load  is  the  particular  load  used  for  learning,  such  as
constructive  activities  that  help  build  mental  frameworks.  This  phe-
nomenon is caused by the working of cognitive processes and knowledge
acquisition that helps with learning. Instructional design should focus on
increasing germane cognitive load and not extraneous cognitive load. As
mentioned earlier, intrinsic cognitive load is due to the complexity of the
task at hand. However, extraneous and germane cognitive loads origi-
nate  in  instructional  design  and  how  something  is  supposed  to  be
learned.  Being  that  intrinsic  load  is  not  vulnerable  to  instructional
design; the reduction of extraneous and germane load should be maxi-
mized as much as possible. More relevant mental effort equals a better
chance of learning. Therefore, knowledge learning means a shift from
useless cognitive burden to relevant cognitive burden (Sweller, 2020;
Paas & Van Merri¨enboer, 2020).

Some research has also supported the need to reduce cognitive load
by providing pedagogical support and assistance while using AI tools.
Flexible support, tailored evaluation, and engaging educational experi-
ences can thus be provided to learners (Kacmaz, 2023). For example, if
AI platforms can help teachers control the task in a way that the level of
difficulty and complexity is at the optimal stage according to the per-
formance of the students (Bahari, 2023), they can avoid frustration and
boredom of students (Plass & Kalyuga, 2019).

Besides  these,  AI  can  also  support  the  germane  cognitive  load  by
providing scaffolding strategies and encouraging the students to engage
in  various  activities  that  promote  profound  learning  and  comprehen-
sion.  It  also  provides  active  and  meaningful  learning  experiences  by
improving problem-solving skills, acquiring schema, and understanding
complex  mathematical  concepts  (van  Nooijen  et  al.,  2024).  These
technological  enhancements, such  as algorithmic  learning and  virtual
reality, allow the teacher to create an academic environment that is able
to stimulate the critical thinking of the students to attain the required
learning  experiences  and  enhance  performance  academically  (Wardat
et al., 2024).

These  technological  developments  notwithstanding,  mathematics
educators  should  explore  these  boons  and  challenges  of  AI-driven  in-
terventions on the dynamics of cognitive load. Hu (2024) has recom-
mended  that  we  should  consider  how  demographic  parameters,
pedagogical  approaches,  and  new  technological  advances  affect  the
management of cognitive load. More importantly, an area where much
consideration  should  be  given  in  this  regard  is  the  ethical  aspects  of
information  privacy,  computation  bias,  and  equal  access  to  AI-driven
resources (Burr & Leslie, 2023).

Recent studies (e.g., Beˇsli´c et al., 2024; Li, Gould, & Zaki, 2024; Yi
et al., 2024; Yim et al., 2024) have highlighted the potential of AI in-
terventions  and  utilizations  in  improving  learning  efficiency  and
accessibility.  These  studies  show  a  generally  optimistic  view  of  how
artificial intelligence might help students by automating regular tasks
and providing quick, thorough answers to challenging questions. How-
ever, these studies mostly concentrate on general results and ignore the
complex effects of artificial intelligence on the three aspects of cognitive
load—intrinsic,  extraneous,  and  germane—within  the  mathematics
teaching and learning process.

This technical development can be illustrated by generative artificial
intelligence systems like ChatGPT, which create instant responses, so-
lutions,  or  explanations,  therefore  making  problem-solving  quite
accessible. Although this convenience simplifies the process of solving
problems, it poses questions about whether such interactions actually
promote deep comprehension or transferable abilities. Simply entering

problems into an artificial intelligence prompt box does not ensure that
students understand the underlying ideas to be able to solve like situa-
tions  on  their  own.  This  restriction  implies  that  artificial  intelligence
may  not  be  able  to  sufficiently  manage  internal  mental  workload  or
eliminate  external  burden  without  compromising  general  cognitive
operations.

Hence,  this  study  seeks  to  address  this  gap  by  exploring  the  phe-
nomenon  of  using  Generative  Artificial  Intelligence  (GAI)  in  mathe-
matical problem-solving. It specifically seeks to investigate how students
use  GAI  technologies,  including  ChatGPT,  to  solve  minimally  chal-
lenging mathematical word problems and evaluate the effects of these
interactions for cognitive load and skill transferability. Examining these
aspects will help to clarify how artificial intelligence affects cognitive
load  and  its  possible  function  in  improving  mathematical  problem-
solving techniques.

1.1. Formal perspectives in the use of generative AI for problem-solving

Jean Lave’s (2016) ideas on learning show that education is not just
about  individual  thinking  but  is  also  shaped  by  the  community  and
environment around us. Learning happens best when it is connected to
real-life situations and social interactions. A key idea in this theory is
"communities of practice," where people learn together by sharing and
building knowledge as a group. AI tools like ChatGPT can support this
kind of learning by acting as interactive partners. ChatGPT can create a
space where students and the machine work together, solve problems,
and check answers in a collaborative environment. This teamwork be-
tween  the  two  helps  students  feel  more  connected  and  less  isolated.
Additionally,  ChatGPT  can  contextualize  difficult  ideas  depending  on
how one creates a command to reduce difficulty of the ideas or even link
them to a broader concept, making it a powerful tool for creating in-
clusive and engaging learning communities.

Lave’s (2016) theory also highlights how socioeconomic status may
influence access to learning tools. Those wealthier students may have
the privilege to access advanced and more features that deliver diverse
services  from  AI  technologies  that  can  significantly  influence  educa-
tional outcomes. For example, “ChatGPT 3.5 plus version”, provides a
lot of services particularly on run time and data storage in the system
compared to the “free version”  that is available to the greater public.
Thereby  enhancing  their  opportunities  for  independent  learning  and
success  in  mathematics.  Realistic  problem  context  creation  that  may
help  ease  the  level  of  difficulty  for  problems  situate  mathematical
learning within practical applications, such as solving minimally com-
plex  mathematics  problems  (Jo  &  Park,  2023;  Orrù  et  al.,  2023),
budgeting  or  engineering  scenarios  (George  &  George,  2023;  Wang,
Wang,  &  Su,  2024).  This  functionality  may  not  be  available  to  both
versions leaving the “free version” at a disadvantage. Moreover, AI tools
such as ChatGPT serve as virtual communities of practice. This enables
students  to  engage  in  practical  and  relatable  problems,  thereby  can
strategize a problem-solving strategy for a simpler task. Through these
interactions,  AI  enhances  the  social  aspect  of  learning  by  simulating
collaborative problem-solving and fostering a sense of ownership and
engagement.

Aligned with the findings of Leiss et al. (2019), AI tools also address
linguistic  barriers  in  mathematical  problem-solving  by  simplifying
dense  or  ambiguous  language.  When  students  used  properly  the
reframing  characteristics  of  the  AI,  it  can  help  simplify  complex  text
structures and emphasizing critical mathematical relationships, in this
since AI reduces extraneous cognitive load and facilitates comprehen-
sion.  Furthermore,  AI  supports  domain-specific  reading  strategies
(McCarthy  &  Yan,  2024;  Su  et  al.,  2024),  assisting  students  in  inter-
preting symbolic language and specialized vocabulary. It also provides
incremental support that is tailored to align with students’ skill levels,
bridge the connections between verbal descriptions, numerical data, and
symbolic  representations,  and  employ  interactive  dialogue  to  model
expert  thinking.  These  functionalities  of  the  AI  allow  students  to

2. Methodology

This  study  utilized  a  descriptive-phenomenological  approach  to
describe an individuals’  actual experiences, as shown in Fig. 1. It was
employed  to  examine  and  report  the  lived  experiences  of  individuals
(Christensen  et  al.,  2017).  Descriptive-phenomenological  research
enabled researchers to conduct an empirical investigation into the ex-
periences  of  students  using  the  “ChatGPT-3.5”  free  version  to  solve
mathematics problems. This version of the GAI received up-to-date in-
formation, accessed, and retrieved data from the internet, whereas other
open-source  AI  tools,  compared  to  ChatGPT,  struggled  to  achieve  at
most 15% accuracy (Chen et al., 2023). A phenomenological study is
regarded  as  an  evidence-based  artifact  and  extends  beyond  available
information to provide dense, insightful, and generative conclusions.

Before starting the study, we sought a formal request letter from the
institution’s head for permission. We also obtained an Ethics Approval
Exemption, as the research seamlessly integrated into the instructional
process  without  requiring  any  additional  interventions  or  curriculum
changes.  No  sensitive  personal  data  were  collected,  and  participation
did  not  impact  students’  grades  or  standing  in  the  course.  Upon
receiving permission, we notified all thirty-eight (38) participants, who
subsequently  chose  to  participate  in  the  research,  with  their  consent.
The participants were already familiar with AI and had been using it as
assistive technology in their studies for other academic courses. How-
ever, they only used the AI on their mobile phones, as this was the most

convenient and readily available resource. One limitation of using the AI
on a phone was the difficulty of writing equations, particularly when
verifying  additional queries  about mathematical equations.  The input
was  constrained  due  to  the  minimal  number  of  symbols  and  limited

study presumed ethical behavior from participants, including providing
truthful responses during interviews and appropriately using ChatGPT
for problem-solving tasks, free from social desirability bias.

This  study  tested  how  specific  features  and  functionalities  of
ChatGPT-3.5 affected the management of cognitive load for the general
population  of  learners.  It  also  examined  how  ChatGPT  interventions
influenced intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load in learning
contexts involving mathematics. A semi-structured interview was used
to investigate real-life experiences from sixty-one students as they solved
a mathematics word problem of moderate complexity that transformed
into systems of equations with the help of ChatGPT.

The data were collected using detailed, non-structured interviews.
The interviews were conducted personally, with follow-up questions to
ensure  comprehensive  answers  that  addressed  several  aspects  of  the
students’ personal experiences. We also strove to reduce socially desir-
able responses. The interview began with the following question: "What
is your experience using ChatGPT to solve mathematical problems?" As
previously  stated,  according  to  Colaizzi,  quality  in  phenomenological
studies  is  measured  by  asking  questions  that  direct  attention  to  the
personal experiences of each participant. The data were further explored
using follow-up questions and the responses provided (Colaizzi, 1978).
The instruments used to collect data included a digital audio recorder
and  field  notes.  The  digital  audio  recorder  captured  all  verbal  infor-
mation during interviews, while field notes documented emotional cues
displayed by participants during the discussions. The transcription of the
notes and audio data was completed immediately after the fulfillment of
the study’s objectives.

Data transcriptions were analyzed using Colaizzi’s phenomenolog-
ical  approach.  Transcriptions  were  developed  while  the  audio  was
played. The process involved actively describing the lived experiences of
the respondents (Sanders, 2003). The data were thoroughly examined to
identify and create significant statements, which were then converted
into formulated meanings. The respondents’ words were analyzed using
the seven steps of Colaizzi’s method to extract themes and sub-themes.
These were subsequently condensed to formulate more significant ideas.
To protect anonymity, pseudonyms such as Student A, B, and C were
used in the transcription of the data. De-identification was performed
after the interviews to ensure the minimal release of personal data.

2.1. Limitations

The study had some limitations that may affect its applicability to a
wider audience. For instance, it concentrated on a small group of stu-
dents already acquainted with ChatGPT, hence it does not fully explore
the  responses  of  varied  learners  to  the  technology  (Yi  et  al.,  2024).
Furthermore, as the participants exclusively utilized mobile phones, the
study couldn’t examine the potential impact of alternative devices, such
as laptops or tablets, on the outcomes (Yim & Su, 2024). Although the
study observed students’ actual interactions with ChatGPT, such as how
they  asked  follow-up  questions,  including  broader  metrics  like  task
performance or cognitive load measurements could provide even deeper
insights  (Beˇsli´c  et  al.,  2024).  Ultimately,  by  concentrating  solely  on
ChatGPT-3.5, the study overlooked the chance to evaluate its effects in
comparison to other AI technologies (Li, Gould, & Zaki, 2024).

3.1.1.

Intrinsic cognitive load

Assignments, in most cases, come with inherent complexity, which
requires massive use of cognitive resources in order to get it through
processing and understanding. Overdependence may be a manifestation
that participants view as a tool to aid dealing with this complexity.

It  also  reduces  the  intrinsic  cognitive  load  by  simplifying  compli-
cated  problems  into  easy  steps,  providing  clear  explanations  for  con-
cepts, and even offering tips and advice on how to tackle a problematic
task,  thereby  making  it  easy  and  more  understandable.  Similarly,
Ko´c-Januchta  et  al.  (2022) found  that  AI-enhanced  textbooks  signifi-
cantly aid students in tackling complex material by clarifying difficult
conceptss.

It  helps  improve  comprehension  by  providing  immediate  answers
and  clarifications.  This  becomes  quite  helpful  in  topics  that  may  be
complex  in  a  way  that,  without  such  support,  some  concepts  end  up
blurred  to  learners.  However,  overreliance  could  hinder  the  develop-
ment of independent learning skills development (Kikalishvili, 2023).

3.1.2. Extraneous cognitive load

Another major concern that would increase the cognitive burden of
participants  is  the  inadequately  designed  instructional  materials,
confusing information presentations. This deficit is reduced by the use of
ChatGPT.

The information retrieval and presentation aspects actually decrease
the extraneous cognitive load on the student caused by poorly structured
materials, and the student perhaps begins focusing on the actual content
rather than how the information is being presented.

In addition, ChatGPT provides the learners with essential and concise
information;  hence,  they  will  concentrate  on  the  most  critical  and
relevant parts of their assignments. This reduces the extraneous load,
which in turn boosts efficiency in completing the assignments.

3.1.3. Germane cognitive load

Participants do not use ChatGPT solely to answer the task but also to
check their answers and understand it deeply. This means that ChatGPT
is  a  support  practice  for  cognitive  process  of  learning  and  also  the
schema construction.

Schemas  are  designed  with  the  help  of  ChatGPT;  they  come  with
detailed explanations and examples from relevant contexts to provide
the information. This type of assistance allows the learning to integrate
new  information  within  the  myriad  knowledge  the  learner  already
possesses  which  resulted  in  better  understanding  and  long-term
memory.

The  use  of  ChatGPT  increases  higher-order  thinking  and  active
learning strategies of the learner which involve self-explanation, elab-
oration, etc. This, in turn increases germane cognitive load to a greater
extent which would create a meaningful learning situation and result in
the construction of robust mental models.

The results manifest how various participants have depicted different
emotions  in  relation  to  the  responses  from  ChatGPT,  which  clearly
pointed out both satisfaction and dissatisfaction. For instance.

A  "I am not satisfied with the response that ChatGPT provided."
B  “I think there might be something wrong with ChatGPT’s response.”
C  “Some of the equations or symbols were not correct.”

This proves that in some cases, the AI model did not meet partici-
pants’  expectations  or  needs.  But  there  are  also  narratives  that  are
satisfied, for example.

A  “I am happy and satisfied with the answers provided by ChatGPT”
B  “ChatGPT helped me a lot in meeting the deadlines.”
C  “ChatGPT never fails me”

3.2.1.

Intrinsic cognitive load

Participants rely on ChatGPT to assist them in complex tasks, and
whether it satisfies or dissatisfies them greatly would have an effect on
them  being  able  to  handle  intrinsic  cognitive  load.  In  this  way,  the
successful  reduction  of  intrinsic  cognitive  load,  with  the  help  of
responsive  and  effective  help  from  ChatGPT,  incrementally  simplifies
complex tasks and successively leads to satisfaction among participants
because cognitive resources are efficiently used for understanding and
learning.  These  findings  reflect  insights  from  Mayer  (2021),  who  dis-
cusses  the  effectiveness  of  well-structured  tools  in  reducing  both
intrinsic and extraneous cognitive load.

On  the  other  hand,  if  these  responses  of  ChatGPT  are  not  very
satisfying  or  simply  wrong,  then  the  intrinsic  cognitive  load  may  be
raised. Participants may get frustrated because they need more cognitive
effort  to  clear  up  or  correct  the  information,  making  the  task  more
challenging than it has to be.

3.2.2. Extraneous cognitive load

The  satisfaction  or  dissatisfaction  of  the  participants  with  the  an-
swers of the ChatGPT is related to extraneous cognitive load, as deter-
mined  by  the  way  the  information  is  presented  and  accessed.  The
effective and well-structured responses by ChatGPT reduce extraneous
cognitive  load  through  easing  the  pieces  of  information  and  making
them understandable, thereby increasing user satisfaction. Participants
pay less attention to navigating confusing information and more to the
content of learning. The mixed emotions participants expressed mirror
findings from studies like Li, Wang, and Li (2024), which show that user
satisfaction with AI systems largely depends on the accuracy and rele-
vance of responses.

Effective answers by ChatGPT are clear, well structured, and reduce
the so-called extraneous cognitive burden on the user to a great extent.
This aspect basically raises the level of satisfaction among the users. The
participants  can  better  focus  on  the  learning  content  rather  than  the
process of searching through confusing information.

Inadequate  responses  from  ChatGPT  lead  to  increased  extraneous
cognitive  load  since  these  participants  need  to  spend  more  time  and
effort in seeking extra information or clarification. It leads to increased
dissatisfaction since the cognitive burden is diverted from learning the
material to overcoming the difficulties brought about by the inadequacy
of the AI.

3.2.3. Germane cognitive load

Satisfactory  responses  from  ChatGPT  also  help  to  support  the
germane cognitive load. Replies about relevant examples, more detailed
explanations, and specific context information help participants to build
and refine some of the relevant cognitive schemas. Participants derive
higher  satisfaction  since  they  are  experiencing  more  meaningful

3.3. Theme 3: expectation misalignment with ChatGPT

The  findings  show  there  are  a  number of  disparities  between  par-
ticipants’ anticipation and what ChatGPT responds with. A statement by
one participant, "The ChatGPT response is quite different from what I ex-
pected,"  shows  instances  where  output  from  the  AI  was  not  as
anticipated.

3.3.1.

Intrinsic cognitive load

Participants use ChatGPT as a tool that helps to maintain the intrinsic
complexity of their tasks. This misalignment between expectations and
responses can affect intrinsic cognitive load to a large extent. In cases
where such responses from ChatGPT do not meet participant expecta-
tions,  increased  intrinsic  cognitive  load  may  result.  This  is  normally
because learners might use more mental effort to make reconciliation
between such an unexpected response and their perception of the task at
hand, hence leading to frustration and more mental effort.

Moreover,  such  misalignment  may  further  lead  to  confusion  and
uncertainty as to how the implementation of a complex task should be
treated. This can alter participants’  chances of engaging and learning
effectively from the material.

3.3.2. Extraneous cognitive load

Misaligned expectations can also increase extraneous working load,
because participants might deal longer or more and, therefore, invest
more mental effort in case of multiple responses or the need for clari-
fication. This distracts from focus on the core learning material.

A  mismatch  between  the  anticipated  and  real  responses  may
eminently increase the cognitive load by the way of distraction from a
task. This extra cognitive load can lead to lower efficiency and a higher
degree of frustration.

3.3.3. Germane cognitive load

The alignment of the responses from ChatGPT with what participants
expect  will  be  significant  in  understanding  the  cognitive  process  that
drives  learning  and  schema  construction.  In  case  the  replies  from
ChatGPT turn out not to meet expectations, the construction process of
the schema is disrupted. It becomes hard to fit the unexpected replies
from the AI into participants’  prior knowledge structures, and mental
models are not developed in a robust way.

Therefore,  because  of  inappropriate  alignment,  germane  cognitive
load  could  be  reduced  in  effectiveness  by  misaligning  that  carries
cognitive resources from meaningful learning activities such as under-
standing  and  applying  concepts  to  resolve  discrepancies  between  ex-
pected and actual responses.

3.4.1.

Intrinsic cognitive load

Participants use ChatGPT to manage the intrinsic complexity of their

load. ChatGPT can break down complex topics into more manageable

intrinsic cognitive load will make it easier for participants to grasp the
difficult  concepts.  These  findings  align  with  Xu  et  al.  (2023),  which
emphasizes the role of AI tools in helping students manage time effec-

ChatGPT is helpful in going beyond to provide explanations, giving

ChatGPT can provide information clearly, concisely, and in a struc-

focused on the main points of the lesson and not get too distracted or

3.4.3. Germane cognitive load

enhanced construction and refinement of cognitive schemas by deeper
understandings and further insight. ChatGPT helps participants create
and  adjust  their  cognitive  schemas  through  elaborate  explanations,
relevant  examples,  and  pieces  of  information  that  make  sense  in  a
particular context. All this creates supportive germane cognitive load for
better learning and knowledge retention.

By making students do some amount of deeper thinking on topics and
understand  them  further,  ChatGPT  supports  the  cognitive  processes
leading  to  meaningful  learning.  This  deep  processing  helps  to  create
mental solid development and ensures long-term retention.

4.2. Negative impacts

4.2.1. Over-reliance and reduced independent thinking

A critical concern is if students begin to over-rely on ChatGPT and, in
a  way,  hinder  the  development  of  independent  problem-solving  and
critical thinking skills (Capraro et al., 2024). Being armed with ChatGPT
for answers means that there will be shallow intellectual engagement
with  the  material  since  the  student  might  go  for  shortcuts  in
problem-solving instead of engaging with the concepts in depth. This
over-reliance will work against the growth of essential cognitive skills
required for independent learning.

4.2.2. Surface learning

This runs a risk of turning into surface learning if the quick answer
that  students  are  getting  from  ChatGPT  is  what  they  are  all  about,
instead of an attempt to get into the material. Such an approach could
lead  to  information  being  temporarily  memorized  without  an  actual
depth of understanding regarding its meaning, which overall degrades
learning  quality  and  hampers  actual  knowledge  retention  in  the  long
run.

5. Conclusion

The students use ChatGPT as an activity to manage cognitive load
actively, thus improving their academic performances in the class. The
students  make  it  manageable  for  intrinsic  and  extraneous  cognitive
loads in task reduction to be effective through the offering of specific
queries and the use of output as supplementary questions for practical
understanding and productive learning. This is further complemented
by the help provided by ChatGPT in the verification of responses and the
management of more complex tasks, for instance, inputting equations
and  symbols,  which  further  promotes  critical  thinking  and  schema
construction.  There  are  still  issues,  however,  in  the  accurate  input  of
mathematical  notation,  which  can  add  a  significant  amount  to  extra-
neous cognitive load. What matters most is the integration of advanced
input  tools  with  ChatGPT,  teaching  effective  questioning  techniques
along with the encouragement of activity during the questioning process
together with critical appraisal, which in this way will balance cognitive
loads in a learning experience whole of productivity and enrichment.

Balanced use

Encourage balanced use of ChatGPT; make students integrate it as a
supplementary tool, but not a standalone resource. Educators should, to
