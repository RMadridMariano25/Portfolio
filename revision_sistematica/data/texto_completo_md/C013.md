Abstract
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies has led to a trans-
formation  in  higher  education  worldwide.  AI  tools  provide  academic  support  to
students  anywhere  and  anytime  to  enhance  their  knowledge  and  skills.  Those  fac-
ing difficulties have been relying on traditional support and guidance. However, this
support  has  experienced  difficulties,  including  availability  and  accessibility.  This
study examines the potential of AI-powered tools to address these challenges, aim-
ing  to  make  academic  support  more  accessible,  efficient,  and  effective.  This  study
focuses on understanding the determinants of AI tools’ acceptance and use for aca-
demic support among students, influencing student satisfaction and academic perfor-
mance in Pakistan and Malaysia. The research on AI tool acceptance and use in the
higher education Institutions (HEI) context is still new and less explored in Pakistani
and Malaysian higher education institutions. A theoretical model based on the Uni-
fied Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) and other factors was
employed  to  identify  factors  that  affect  AI  tool  adoption  in  higher  education.  The
survey research design was employed, and the total sample size was 305 respond-
ents, with 203 students from Quaid-e-Awam University of Science and Technology
(QUEST), Pakistan, and 102 students from Universiti Teknologi Malaysia (UTM).
A  “Partial  least  squares  structural  equation  modeling  (PLS-SEM)  Analysis”  was
employed to assess the research model and hypotheses using SmartPls 4.0. In Paki-
stan  and  Malaysia,  students  are  more  concerned  about  using  AI  tools  to  improve
their  academic  performance.  The  findings  indicated  that  performance  and  effort
expectancy, information accuracy of AI tools, pedagogical fit to meet the student’s
expectations,  and  student  interaction  with  tools  were  important  factors  in  predict-
ing the acceptance and use of AI tools among students of both countries in higher
education, and the rising use of these AI tools has improved students’ satisfaction
levels  and  significantly  impacted  students  learning  outcomes  in  both  countries.
Additionally, student engagement and personal innovativeness have not significantly

1  Introduction

Higher education institutions (HEIs) are crucibles for nurturing future leaders, inno-
vators, and professionals. Students embark on innovative journeys within these insti-
tutions, seeking knowledge, skills, and personal growth. However, the path to aca-
demic  success  is  often  strewn  with  obstacles,  including  complex  course  offerings
and challenges in accessing academic support, guidance, and resources (Assiri et al.,
2020). In recent years, academic support has evolved into a vital component of HEIs,
serving a dual purpose. It supports students in academic pursuits while contributing
to  broader  institutional  goals  of  enhancing  student  retention  and  persistence  (Pil-
lai et al., 2023). These efforts aim to create an environment conducive to academic
excellence within HEIs. Studies like Kilinc and Granello (2003) have shed light on
students’ preferences for assistance (Kilinc & Granello, 2003). According to John-
son et al. (2022), 90% of students encounter academic challenges like stress, anxiety,
despair, and future planning. Surprisingly, only 15% admit to seeking academic sup-
port from other sources. (Johnson et al., 2022). Another Research study has empha-
sized  gender-based  differences  in  help-seeking  behaviors,  Females  tend  to  exhibit
more  favorable  attitudes  toward  seeking  help  and  are  more  inclined  to  accept  the
support  necessary  for  academic  success  (Al-Fraihat  et  al.,  2017;  R.  Twum,  2014).
This  propensity  among  female  students  to  engage  in  help-seeking  behaviors  when
confronted with academic challenges contrasts with the reluctance observed among
male  students,  who  often  face  difficulties  seeking  assistance  from  professionals  or
their  social  networks  when  encountering  academic  difficulties  (Waleed  Mugahe
Al-Rahmi & Othman, 2013a, 2013b). Academic support is a important element of
higher education, aiming to facilitate students in attaining academic success while
addressing  their  personal  and  career  aspirations.  It  plays  a  pivotal  role  in  enhanc-
ing  student  satisfaction,  resolving  academic  challenges,  and  ensuring  achievement
of academic and professional career (Assiri et al., 2020). According to the O’Banion
Model,  academic  support  involves  an  active  relationship  between  advisors  (teach-
ers) and students, with advisors acting as mentors to foster self-awareness and goal
completion  (Al-Nory,  2012;  Assiri  et  al.,  2020).  The  National  Academic  Support
Association  defines  it  as  a  process  where  advisors  help  students  comprehend  and
achieve their goals, access information and services, and make informed decisions
associated with their abilities and academic requirements (Assiri et al., 2020; Bur-
ton & Wellington, 1998). Although various definitions exist, the consensus among
researchers is that academic support involves the interaction between students and

et al., 2022).

Pakistani  higher  education  institutions  face  significant  challenges  in  provid-
ing  academic  support  systems,  with  academic  guides  failing  to  develop  personal-
ized study plans and assist students in accessing necessary resources (Ahmad, 2014;
Chaudary,  2011;  Soomro  et  al.,  2018).  These  advisors  also  help  students  navigate
institutional procedures, but their extensive responsibilities often fail to deliver the
high-quality guidance students and institutions expect (Huang et al., 2022).

Students  in  Pakistani  and  Malaysian  higher  education  institutions  encounter
various  academic  challenges  that  can  impact  their  achievements,  including  lan-
guage  barriers  for  non-native  english  speakers,  rigorous  coursework,  and  limited
access  to  critical  academic  resources(Arain  et  al.,  2019;  Mohamed,  2023;  Raman
et al., 2014). Academic writing difficulties, structuring research papers, and proper
citations  are  also  prevalent  (Huang,  2015).  The  inadequate  academic  support  ser-
vices within these institutions exacerbate these challenges, with students struggling
to  access  tutors,  advisors,  and  mentors  (Kilinc  &  Granello,  2003).  Financial  con-
straints,  including  tuition  fees  and  educational  expenses,  further  hinder  students’
access to resources and support, affecting their academic progress (Bilquise et al.,
2023).  Academic  support,  Universities  worldwide  prioritize  improving  academic
support due to its role in achieving the student success, satisfaction, and graduation
rates. Advisors also play a key role by guiding students on academic, personal, and
social issues (Gordon et al., 2011). The tasks involved in academic support include
explaining  foremost,  career,  and  life  goals,  aiding  in  course  selection,  clarifying
institutional  requirements,  promoting  awareness  of  learning  assistance  programs,
evaluating  student  performance  and  goals,  and  reinforcing  student  self-direction.
Advisors also act as intermediaries in large lecture courses(Assiri et al., 2020). The
traditional style of academic support, conducted through paper-based methods, faces
challenges that impact its efficiency (Assiri et al., 2020). In this approach, advisors
and students meet in person, where advisors review academic records and require-
ments, offering face-to-face advice. However, this method encounters difficulties in
large universities with a high student population, making individual communication
impractical  (Henderson  &  Goodridge,  2015).  Traditional  support  is  time-consum-
ing, relying heavily on the advisor’s efforts and potentially leading to errors due to
limited  time  and  experience.  Advisors  must  possess  comprehensive  knowledge  of
academic requirements, educational plans, and rules. Inaccuracies may arise when
programs  are  under  review  or  advisors  are  not  up  to  date(Al-Nory,  2012;  Hender-
son  &  Goodridge,  2015).  Support  sessions  are  often  constrained  by  time  limita-
tions,  particularly  during  peak  periods,  impacting  the  quality  of  advice.  Students
may experience lengthy waiting times to meet advisors, potentially conflicting with
the advisor’s lecture schedule. The reliance on multiple files and documents during
support can lead to information representation errors and a tedious support process
(Assiri et al., 2020; Caratiquit & Caratiquit, 2023).

Technology  is  revolutionizing  academic  support  by  shifting  from  traditional,
human-centered  approaches  to  automated  AI  based  online  methods  (Al-Nory,
2012;  Caratiquit  &  Caratiquit,  2023).  Electronic  resources,  readily  available  in
most  universities,  offer  a  non-traditional  support  experience.  This  technological

transformation aims to reduce costs, increase flexibility, accountability, and stream-
line services, ultimately enhancing graduation rates and student satisfaction (Dahri
et al., 2023a, b; Dahri, Vighio, Alismaiel, et al., 2022). While technology provides
an alternative for students unable to meet advisors in person, it should be viewed as
a supportive tool rather than a replacement for human advice (Assiri et al., 2020).
The  integration  of  human  and  technological  elements  can  save  time  for  advisors,

Artificial intelligence tools have gained prominence in higher education institu-
tions (HEIs), and have been used in various nations, like Singapore (Hua*, 2012),
Malaysia  (Ahmad  &  Ghapar,  2019),  and  Pakistan  (Ahmad  et  al.,  2021;  Dahri,
Vighio, Al-Rahmi, et al., 2022) increasingly integrating technology into their edu-
cational  systems.  This  shift  towards  AI  in  education  has  garnered  attention  from
various sectors, including economists, political analysts, military strategists, security
experts, and educators (Ahmad et al., 2021). These AI tools have helped in a trans-
formative era of academic support within HEIs, going beyond conventional bounda-
ries (AlDhaen, 2022). Their multifaceted advantages include personalized guidance,
timely assistance, data-driven insights, and 24/7 availability through AI chatbots and
virtual advisors. This constant accessibility is particularly valuable in the digital age,
where  students  seek  instant  solutions  to  academic  queries  and  enhanced  engage-
ment. These tools foster active support, allowing students to seamlessly interact with
academic  guides,  strengthening  their  connection  and  engagement  with  the  educa-
tional institution (Bilquise et al., 2023; Dahri et al., 2019; Huang, 2015).

Moreover, the application of AI in higher education extends beyond basic auto-
mation. Notably, intelligent tutoring systems serve as personalized mentors, provid-
ing real-time guidance and adaptive practice in various subjects, a common feature
in Pakistan and Malaysian HEIs (M. F. Ahmad & Ghapar, 2019; Amin & Rajadurai,
2018). These AI-powered platforms enable continuous assessment and offer imme-
diate feedback, empowering students to monitor their progress and refine their study
strategies. This real-time feedback loop cultivates critical thinking, problem-solving,
and metacognition (Bilquise et al., 2023).

AI tools like ChatGPT have gained attention for their integration into academic
research,  writing,  publishing,  and  authorship  (Foroughi  et  al.,  2023).  ChatGPT  is
gaining prominence in higher education, assisting students in tasks like generating
citations and assignments (Crawford et al., 2023). This adoption prompts institutions
to  explore  ChatGPT’s  potential  to  transform  higher  education  (Yuce  et  al.,  2019).
ChatGPT and similar AI tools are increasingly used in higher education, spanning
various  academic  tasks  and  discussions,  including  applications  in  medical  educa-
tion Safranek et al. (2023). These tools are relatively new in academia and higher
education, and their adoption continues to be explored. In addition to ChatGPT, and
various other platforms such as scite.ai, Consensus, Scinapse, Research Rabbit, and
ReadCube  Papers  offer  tools  for  academic  research,  including  context-aware  cita-
tions and AI-driven organization of research materials (Chatterjee & Bhattacharjee,
2020). Innovative applications like DiaChat and Education Copilot leverage natural
language processing to automate diagram creation and streamline educational con-
tent (Ellerton, 2023). In academic support and enhancing writing, Natural Language

The  higher  education  sector  in  Malaysia  and  Pakistan  is  currently  undergoing
a  period  of  change,  with  a  notable  increase  in  student  enrollments  and  changing
demands.  This  presents  considerable  challenges  for  Higher  Education  Institutions
(HEIs).  The  scarcity  of  teaching  faculty  is  also  a  significant  challenge,  leading  to
strained  faculty-to-student  ratios  and  hindering  personalized  attention.  The  cur-
rent  educational  models  face  challenges  in  catering  to  the  various  learning  styles
and changing requirements of students in the twenty-first century. This can result in
students feeling unsupported and may impact their academic performance (Qureshi
et  al.,  2012;  Zubairi  et  al.,  2021).In  this  context,  AI-based  academic  support
emerges as a promising solution. The benefits of AI systems in education are numer-
ous. These systems can provide personalized learning pathways, cater to individual
needs,  and  provide  academic  support  (Foroughi  et  al.,  2023;  Khan,  2023).  They
effectively overcome limitations such as time constraints, geographical barriers, and
faculty availability. The use of AI-powered tools  can  bring a new way of  learning
process  by  incorporating  interactive  experiences,  and  personalized  feedback.  This
has the potential to greatly increase student engagement and motivation(Assiri et al.,
2020; Ouyang et al., 2022; J. Su & Yang, 2023). The potential of these features is
to create a learning environment that is more supportive and engaging, bridging the
gap between existing resources and student needs. The effectiveness and acceptance
of AI-based support in the context of Malaysian and Pakistani HEIs have not been
thoroughly explored. This study seeks to provide understanding of these important
aspects,  examining  the  factors  that  influence  student  acceptance  of  AI-based  aca-
demic support systems and analyzing how they affect academic performance in both
countries.

2   Theoretical Model

Numerous researchers have extensively investigated technology acceptance in vari-
ous  industries,  enhancing  existing  models  and  proposing  additional  variables  to
explain  the  factors  affecting  technology  acceptance.  Models  like  the  Composite
Technology  Acceptance  Model  and  Theory  of  Planned  Behavior  (TAM-TPB),  PC
Utilization  Model  (MPCU),  Task  Technology  Fit  (TTF),  Social  Cognition  Theory

1 3
Education and Information Technologies

(SCT),  and  Motivation  Model  (MM)  have  been  applied  in  specific  industries.  In
2003, Venkatesh et al. consolidated these factors into the Unified Theory of Accept-
ance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), a prominent framework. The UTAUT model
has  been  demonstrated  to  account  for  around  70%  of  the  variance  in  behavioral
intention,  surpassing  the  explanatory  power  of  other  models  that  typically  explain
only 17% to 53% of the variance. UTAUT is recognized for its comprehensiveness
in considering various adoption drivers (Dahri et al., 2021; Foroughi et al., 2023).
Foroughi  et  al.  (2023),  have  demonstrated  UTAUT  applicability  across  diverse
technologies  such  as  mobile  apps  (Arain  et  al.,  2018),  e-commerce  (Chiemeke  &
Evwiekpaefe,  2011),  e-learning  platforms  (Zacharis  &  Nikolopoulou,  2022),  ani-
mation  (Dajani  &  Hegleh,  2019),  mobile  devices  (Hoi,  2020),  and  learning  man-
agement systems (Raza et al., 2022; Strzelecki, 2023), and lecture capture systems
(Farooq  et  al.,  2017),  Social  media  use  (Al-Rahmi  et  al.,  2022),  and  online  learn-
ing  (Waleed  Mugahed  Al-Rahmi  et  al.,  2021).  Therefore,  the  UTAUT  model  has
emerged as a valuable framework for interpreting users’ intentions to accept emerg-
ing technologies like AI (Almaiah et al., 2019). Many researchers have adopted and
modified this model to align with their specific contexts and have achieved favorable
results. In AI tool adoption, the UTAUT framework has been instrumental in under-
standing users’ willingness to embrace these technologies.

Integrating  AI-based  academic  support  to  enhance  students’  performance  in
higher  education has gained significant  attention.  However, the  absence  of  a com-
prehensive  acceptance  model  for  AI  tools  in  this  context  is  a  notable  gap.  The
adoption  of  AI  tools  for  academic  support  in  higher  education  is  increasingly  sig-
nificant.  Constructs  drawn  from  the  Unified  Theory  of  Acceptance  and  Use  of
Technology (UTAUT) framework play crucial roles in understanding AI tool adop-
tion. "Performance Expectancy" assesses the perceived benefits students anticipate
from  AI  tools  in  enhancing  learning  outcomes  and  academic  performance  (Dahri
et  al.,  2023a,  b;  Venkatesh  et  al.,  2016).  Facilitating  conditions  (FC)  pertain  to
the resources and assistance available to individuals for effectively utilizing a spe-
cific  technology(Venkatesh  et  al.,  2016).  Additional  constructs  include  "Students’
Engagement,"  which  focuses  on  interaction  and  involvement  (F.  Su  et  al.,  2023),
and "Assessment Effectiveness," evaluating the role of tools in efficient assessment
methods  (Dodeen,  2013).  "Students’  Interaction"  explores  peer  engagement  (Wei
et  al.,  2012),  while  "Information  Accuracy"  assesses  content  reliability  (Farooq
et  al.,  2017;  Filieri  &  McLeay,  2014).  "Personal  Innovations"  looks  at  AI  tools’
capacity to inspire creative thinking (Foroughi et al., 2023), and "Pedagogical Fit"
examines  alignment  with  course  objectives  and  teaching  methods  (Pittalis,  2021).
"Behavioral Intention" explores what motivates students to engage with these tools
(Dahri et al., 2021; Venkatesh et al., 2016). "Use" measures students’ active incorpo-
ration of AI tools into their academic routines (Dahri et al., 2021; Venkatesh et al.,
2016). "Students’ Satisfaction" is crucial for sustained usage (Waleed Mugahed Al-
Rahmi et al., 2014; Alyoussef, 2021; Gopal et al., 2021), and "Enhancement of Stu-
dents’  Academic  Performance"  evaluates  the  tools’  tangible  impact  on  grades  and
knowledge acquisition (Alyoussef, 2021; Gopal et al., 2021; Tawafak et al., 2023).
These constructs, tailored to AI tool adoption, provide a comprehensive framework
for  understanding  factors  influencing  their  acceptance  and  utilization  in  higher

2.1   Hypothesis development

In  this  research,  we  developed  a  new  theoretical  model  that  extends  the  Unified
Theory  of  Acceptance  and  Use  of  Technology  (UTAUT).  This  extended  model
add UTAUT constructs like performance expectancy, behavioral intention, and use
with other factors related to AI tool adoption, such as student engagement, assess-
ment  effectiveness,  students’  interaction,  information  accuracy,  personal  innova-
tions, pedagogical fit, students’ satisfaction, and enhancement of students’ academic
performance.  it  is  worth  mentioning  that  our  research  places  less  attention  on  the
effort  expectancy  variable  of  the  UTAUT  model  and  more  focus  on  understand-
ing  how  AI-based  academic  support  is  accepted  and  affects  student  performance.
In  contrast  to  the  adoption  of  technology  in  general,  effort  expectancy  might  not
be as relevant in the context of academic support,  where AI systems  are designed
to minimize learning effort by providing easily accessible support and personalized
learning (de Blanes et al., 2022). Furthermore, students may see AI systems as help-
ful  tools  that  reduce  effort  owing  to  resource  constraints  and  conventional  educa-
tional practices, which eliminates the need for our model to explicitly include effort
expectations(Moussawi et al., 2023).

2.1.1   Performance Expectancy (PE)

This construct refers to students’ expectations regarding the effectiveness and ben-
efits of using AI tools in their learning. Students who believe AI tools will enhance
their academic performance and understanding are more likely to have positive per-
formance expectations. So et al. (2012) investigated students’ perceptions of using
AI tools for language learning. Students with higher performance expectations were
likelier  to  adopt  and  use  these  tools  effectively  (So  et  al.,  2012).  Foroughi  et  al.
(2023) found that students who perceived AI tools as valuable aids for understand-
ing  complex  subjects  strongly  intended  to  adopt  them  for  their  learning  (Foroughi
et al., 2023).

H1: Performance Expectancy positively influences Behavioral Intentions.

2.1.2   Facilitating Conditions (FC)

The  FC  construct  focuses  on  resources  and  support  for  students  to  adopt  and  use
AI  tools  effectively.  It  includes  technical  infrastructure,  tool  access,  training,  and
institutional  support.  Foroughi  et  al.  (2023)  highlighted  the  importance  of  techni-
cal  support  and  institutional  resources  in  influencing  students’  intention  to  use  AI
tools(Foroughi  et  al.,  2023).  Chatterjee  and  Bhattacharjee  (2020)  found  that  the
availability  of  essential  tools  and  resources  positively  affected  students’  perceived
ease of use and intention to use AI-infused tools(Chatterjee & Bhattacharjee, 2020).

H2: Facilitating Conditions positively influence Behavioral Intentions.

2.1.3   Students’ Engagement (SE)

The  SE  construct  identifies  students’  active  participation  and  participation  when
using  AI  tools  for  learning.  Engaged  students  are  motivated  and  willingly  explore
these tools. Studies by (Schwarz & Zhu, 2015; F. Su et al., 2023) delved into stu-
dent engagement with AI tools in higher education, finding that higher engagement
correlated  with  increased  satisfaction  and  academic  performance.  Su  et  al.  (2023)
specifically  explored  AI-powered  chatbots’  impact  on  engagement,  revealing  that
interactive conversations with chatbots boosted student interest and participation in
learning (F. Su et al., 2023).

H3: Students’ Engagement positively influences Behavioral Intentions.

2.1.4   Assessment Effectiveness (AE)

The AE construct assesses how AI tools enhance assessment methods by providing
timely feedback and insights to students. Students’ perceptions of assessment effec-
tiveness influence their willingness to adopt these tools (Dodeen, 2013). A study by
Terzis and Economides (2011) examined the impact of assessment tools on students’
learning  outcomes.  Students  who  received  personalized  feedback  through  these
tools improved their understanding and performance (Terzis & Economides, 2011).
In a research study by Fergus et al. (2023) and Rolim and Isaias (2019), the integra-
tion of predictive analytics and AI tools helped identify struggling students early and
provided tailored interventions, leading to higher retention rates and academic suc-
cess (Fergus et al., 2023; Rolim & Isaias, 2019).

H4: Assessment Effectiveness positively influences Behavioral Intentions.

2.1.5   Student Interaction (TI)

The  student  interaction  construct  focuses  on  students’  interaction  with  AI  tools
regarding  integrating  and  using  AI  tools.  Student  interaction,  also  called  "Social
interaction,"  refers  to  student-instructor  involvement  when  teachers  use  different
strategies  to  increase  students’  interaction  and  social  inclusion  (Alenezi,  2022);
according to Lonn et al., no teacher is needed for virtual learner-to-learner interac-
tions  (Lonn  et  al.,  2011).  Positive  student  interactions  can  influence  students’  per-
ceptions and intentions to use these tools (W. Al-Rahmi & Othman, 2013a, 2013b).
Al-Rahmi  et  al.  (2022),  explored  the  impact  of  students’  interactions  on  students’
intentions to use digital technologies (A. M. Al-Rahmi et al., 2022). Positive interac-
tions between instructors and students enhanced the perceived utility of these tools
(Strzelecki, 2023) and investigated students’ perceptions of AI tools as open educa-
tional resources. Positive student involvement and guidance were identified as fac-
tors impacting students’ engagement and usage of AI tools.

H5: Students’ Interaction positively influences Behavioral Intentions.
H6: Students’ Interaction positively influences AI Tools Use.

2.1.6   Information Accuracy (IA)

The AC construct refers to students’ perceptions of the reliability and correctness of
information  provided  by  AI  tools.  Trust  in  the  accuracy  of  information  influences
students’ willingness to use these tools. (Foroughi et al., 2023) studied students’ per-
ceptions  of  using  AI-based  machine  translation  tools.  Students’  confidence  in  the
accuracy of translated content positively influenced their intention to use these tools.
A research study by Foroughi et al. (2023) explored students’ perceptions of using
AI-driven writing assistants (Foroughi et al., 2023). Students valued tools that pro-
vided accurate suggestions and corrections for improving their writing skills.

H7: Information accuracy positively influences the use of AI tools.

2.1.7   Personal Innovations (PI)

The  PI  construct  represents  students’  willingness  to  explore  new  and  innovative
learning approaches enabled by AI tools. Students who view AI tools as platforms
for experimenting with novel learning methods are likelier to adopt them. In a study
(Strzelecki, 2023), students who reported being inspired to try innovative learning
strategies with AI tools were more inclined to use these tools effectively. Research
by  (Alkawsi  et  al.,  2021;  K.  K.  Twum  et  al.,  2022)  investigated  the  role  of  tech-
nological tools in fostering personal innovations in higher education. Students who
creatively  integrated  AI  tools  into  their  learning  processes  reported  enhanced  aca-
demic performance.

H8: Personal innovations positively influence the use of AI tools.

2.1.8   Pedagogical Fit (PF)

Pedagogical fit is the alignment between AI tools and instructors’ teaching methods.
When AI tools seamlessly complement instructional approaches, students perceive
them as valuable and are more likely to adopt them. Research by An et al. (2023)
explored  the  pedagogical  fit  of  AI-driven  content  delivery  platforms  (An  et  al.,
2023). Students who found these platforms aligned with their instructors’ teaching
styles were more motivated to engage with them. A study by H. Huang et al. (2022)
investigated the integration of intelligent tutoring systems. The pedagogical fit was
identified as a critical factor influencing students’ perceptions of the usefulness of
these AI tools(Pittalis, 2021).

H9: Pedagogical Fit positively influences AI Tools Use.

2.1.9   Behavioral Intentions (BI)

This construct refers to students’ readiness and intention to use AI tools for learn-
ing.  Positive  behavioral  intentions  indicate  that  students  are  motivated  to  adopt
and  integrate  AI  tools  into  their  educational  practices.  Davis  (1989)  proposed  the

Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), which highlights the influence of behavioral
intentions on technology adoption. Various studies have found positive intentions to
strongly predict actual technology usage (Davis, 1989). Chatterjee and Bhattachar-
jee (2020) investigated students’ behavioral intentions to use AI agents or chatbots.
Positive intentions were found to lead to increased usage of these tools.

H10: Behavioral Intentions positively influence AI Tools Use.
H11: Behavioral Intentions positively influence Students’ Satisfaction.
H12: Behavioral Intentions positively influence Improve Students’ Academic Per-
formance.

2.1.10   AI Tools Use (AIU)

AI tool use refers to how students use AI-infused tools for their learning activities.
This  construct  considers  adopting  and  integrating  AI  technologies  into  students’
educational  experiences.  AI  tools  have  demonstrated  their  potential  to  enhance
learning outcomes. For instance, Li and Xu (2020) conducted a study involving an
AI-driven language learning platform and found that students who actively engaged
with AI tools showed improved language proficiency compared to those who didn’t.
Additionally, a study by Strzelecki (2023) explored using AI-powered chatbots for
student  support,  highlighting  how  these  tools  positively  impacted  engagement  and
learning experiences.

H13: The use of AI tools positively influences students’ satisfaction.
H14:  AI  Tools  Use  Positive  Influences  on  Improve  Students’  Academic  Perfor-
mance.

2.1.11   Students’ Satisfaction (SS)

Students’ satisfaction reflects their contentment and positive perceptions of AI tools
and  their  overall  learning  experiences  (Al-Maatouk  et  al.,  2020;  Waleed  Mugahed
Al-Rahmi et al., 2023). High levels of satisfaction indicate the effectiveness of AI
tools in meeting students’ needs. Studies have shown that satisfied students are more
likely to be engaged and perform academically. Memon et al. (2022) examined the
impact  of  AI-guided  feedback  on  student  satisfaction  and  found  that  personalized
AI-driven feedback improved satisfaction levels. Similarly, a study investigated stu-
dents’ perceptions of AI-driven adaptive learning systems and revealed that higher
satisfaction levels were associated with improved learning outcomes (Memon et al.,
2022).

H15: Students’ Satisfaction positively influences AI Tools Use.

2.1.12   Improve students’ academic performance

This  construct  assesses  how  AI  tools  enhance  students’  overall  academic  achieve-
ments  and  performance,  encompassing  grades,  knowledge  retention,  and  skill

development (Bilquise et al., 2023). The relationship between AI tools and academic
performance  has  garnered  attention  in  various  studies.  For  instance,  research  on
intelligent  tutoring  systems  has  shown  that  students  who  used  these  tools  signifi-
cantly improved their understanding of complex subjects (Huang et al., 2022; Lee
&  Yeo,  2022;  Yuce  et  al.,  2019).  Furthermore,  Bilquise  et  al.  (2023)  explored  the
impact  of  AI-driven  assessment  tools  on  student  performance,  emphasizing  their
potential  to  identify  and  address  learning  gaps.  AI  Tool  Use,  Students’  Satisfac-

collectively  illustrating  the  influence  of  AI-infused  practices  on  students’  educa-

engagement, satisfaction, and performance, offering valuable insights into the poten-
tial of AI in higher education.

3   Research methodology

3.1   Sample selection and data collection

The  data  for  this  study  was  collected  from  students  from  Universiti  Teknologi
Malaysia  (UTM)  and  Quest  University  Pakistan.  The  study  focused  on  students
enrolled in coursework programs in universities, the participants were chosen based
on their knowledge of and use of various AI tools for academic support purposes.
Specifically, students actively using AI tools for learning, ranging from undergradu-
ate to postgraduate levels, narrowing down the sample to coursework students due
to their relevance in providing an understanding of AI-based academic support for
learning tasks. Data collection took place between June 1 and July 30, 2023, with an
online survey link shared by student’s class WhatsApp groups and physical distribu-
tion in classes with the assistance of co-authors. The questionnaire utilized a 5-point
Likert  scale  (1 = strongly  disagree,  5 = strongly  agree)  to  measure  items  related  to
AI tool usage, academic performance, and demographic data. Additionally, a cover
letter  was  included  to  explain  the  participants’  voluntary  and  anonymous  services
for this study. Out of 350 distributed questionnaires, 325 were returned, resulting in
a significant 98.0% response rate, and 15 were excluded due to low variability. The
final dataset consisted of 305 usable responses. Univariate outlier analysis showed
no  outliers  and  multivariate  outliers.  The  demographic  characteristics  of  respond-
ents are displayed in Table 3.

To minimize non-response errors, strategies were implemented before and after

3.2   Measurement instruments

The questionnaire in this study comprised two main sections. The first section gath-

details regarding AI tool usage and knowledge. The second section integrated con-
structs from the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT),
including  Performance  Expectancy,  Facilitating  conditions,  Student  Engagement,
Assessment  Effectiveness,  Teacher’  Interaction,  Information  Accuracy,  Personal

sources from previous research to ensure content validity, as indicated in Table 2.

3.3   Piloting of research tool

Before the primary analysis, a pilot test was conducted with 60 students to assess the
reliability of the questionnaire. As suggested by previous research, Cronbach’s alpha
method was used to check for data consistency, with a threshold value of 0.7 (J. Hair
et al., 2017; J. F. Hair et al., 2006). The Cronbach’s alpha values for all constructs
exceeded  this  threshold,  indicating  the  reliability  of  the  data  for  subsequent  struc-
tural equation modeling (SEM) analysis.

5   Discussion

Academic  support  is  essential  in  higher  education,  focusing  on  students’  success
and addressing personal and career goals. It involves an active relationship between
advisors  and  students,  aiming  to  enhance  satisfaction  and  resolve  academic  chal-
lenges. Universities prioritize improving academic support globally to boost student
success,  satisfaction,  and  graduation  rates.  However,  traditional  support  methods,
relying  on  in-person  meetings  and  paper-based  processes,  face  challenges  such  as
time  constraints  and  potential  errors.  Technology  is  transforming  academic  sup-
port,  offering  automated  online  methods  to  increase  flexibility,  reduce  costs,  and
improve services. While artificial intelligence (AI) technology serves as a valuable
tool, it, fostering a combined approach for efficient and effective academic support
to improve students’ academic successes.

The results of the hypothesis testing across the complete dataset in Malaysia and
Pakistan  reveal  valuable  insights  into  the  relationships  between  various  constructs
in the context of AI tool adoption and their impact on student’s academic outcomes.
Several hypotheses were supported in the complete dataset, Pakistan, and Malaysia,
indicating the significance of certain factors. These included the positive influence of
performance expectation and facilitating conditions on behavioral intentions, along
with  the  positive  effects  of  student  interaction,  information  accuracy,  pedagogical
fit, and behavioral intentions on AI tool use. Additionally, hypotheses related to the
positive  influence  of  behavioral  intentions  on  students’  satisfaction  and  improving
students’ academic performance received support. However, there were differences
in  the  results  across  the  datasets.  For  instance,  while  assessment  effectiveness  did
not significantly influence behavioral intentions in all three datasets, the influence of
personal innovations on AI tool use was not supported. Furthermore, the discussion
of each hypothesis unfolds as follows: First and foremost, H1 = PE—> IBL, in both
countries, the influence of behavioral intention on AI tool adoption was positively
associated with performance expectancy. This finding underscores the significance
of  performance  expectancy  in  shaping  users’  perceptions  when  utilizing  AI  appli-
cations for receiving accurate support. This outcome aligns with prior research by
scholars  (AlDhaen,  2022;  Dahri  et  al.,  2023a,  b;  Mohd  Rahim  et  al.,  2022),  who

1 3Education and Information Technologies

concurred that this aspect should center on users’ perceptions of technology adop-
tion within their community. This finding helps address a concern in previous stud-
ies,  where  even  if  AI  tools  were  user-friendly,  they  might  not  be  adopted  unless
users perceived them as valuable. Users are more inclined to adopt tools when they
perceive them as beneficial. H2 = FC—> IBL. Current research findings support the
idea that facilitating conditions plays a significant role in predicting the adoption of
AI tools within educational settings. This finding is in line with earlier studies, such
as those conducted by (AlDhaen, 2022; Dahri et al., 2018, 2021) and (ARAIN et al.,

relationship  between  facilitating  conditions  and  intention  to  use  AI.  However,  the

conditions on AI adoption intentions. The slightly higher p-value in Malaysia might
suggest a less pronounced effect compared to the more robust association observed

to their effectiveness as educational aids. This ease of use enhances the appeal of AI
tools  as  educational  resources,  thereby  increasing  the  chances  of  students  involv-
ing them. Furthermore, the ease of use associated with AI tools can reduce cogni-
tive load, as Chen et al. (2021) suggested, enabling students to direct their mental
resources toward learning the subject matter rather than navigating complex technol-
ogy.  This  reduction  in  cognitive  load  may  result  in  more  efficient  learning  experi-

tools can enhance learning efficiency and promote better comprehension and reten-

H3 SE—> IBL and H4 = AE—> IBL hypotheses were examined in the context of
Pakistan and Malaysia, and the results did not yield significant findings about behav-

to students’ satisfaction and learning outcomes, particularly academic achievement.
These  results  suggest  that  student  engagement  and  assessment  effectiveness  in  the
context of AI tools did not influence students’ overall attitudes toward AI technolo-

First, it is possible that the measures employed to evaluate student engagement and
assessment effectiveness in the context of AI tools were not sensitive or comprehen-
sive  enough  to  capture  their  true  influence  on  behavioral  intentions.  Furthermore,
the  impact  of  these  factors  may  vary  depending  on  specific  contextual  factors  in
Pakistani and Malaysian higher education institutions. Another consideration is that
students may not fully grasp the potential benefits of AI tools for their engagement
and assessment processes. A lack of awareness or understanding regarding the capa-
bilities of AI tools could have contributed to the non-significant results.

Additionally, students’ prior experiences with traditional methods of engagement
and assessment may have influenced their perceptions and attitudes. The result dif-
fers  from  the  study  (Mugahed  Al-Rahmi  et  al.,  2015;  Dahri  et  al.,  2023a,  b;  Fan
et  al.,  2021).  Furthermore,  the  non-significant  findings  could  be  attributed  to  the

learning processes. Moreover, these findings underscore the potential for AI tools to
enhance traditional teaching methods and positively contribute to students’ academic
achievements and overall learning experiences in Pakistan and Malaysia.

5.1   Theoretical and practical implications

Theoretically, the study advances our knowledge of AI tool acceptance in higher edu-
cation  settings,  particularly  in  Pakistan  and  Malaysia.  The  study  contributes  to  the
enrichment of current theoretical frameworks by providing empirical validation of the
relevance of categories like Performance Expectancy, Facilitating Conditions, Student
Engagement, Assessment Effectiveness, and more. It adds knowledge about the manner
in which many factors interact to affect the usage of AI in educational settings and adds
to the body of literature on technological acceptability.

In terms of application, the study offers useful information to Pakistani and Malay-
sian higher education institutions (HEIs). The variables impacting the use of AI tools
that  have  been  discovered  provide  HEIs  with  recommendations  on  how  to  create
focused integration plans. HEIs may concentrate on raising performance expectations
by highlighting the advantages AI tools have for academics, making sure Facilitating
Conditions  have  user-friendly  interfaces,  and  adding  features  that  encourage  student
engagement  and  assessment  effectiveness.  The  study  provide  HEIs  more  flexibility
in how they implement AI tools, which will lead to more successful tool uptake and,
eventually, higher-quality academic assistance in the AI age.

This  research  explored  the  adoption  of  AI  tools  in  higher  education  across
Pakistan  and  Malaysia.  The  primary  objective  was  to  explore  the  perception  of
students  in  both  countries  about  AI  technologies  and  how  these  relate  to  their
educational  experiences.  This  study  aimed  to  provide  insights  that  would  con-
tribute  to  designing  more  effective  educational  strategies  and  policies,  thereby
enhancing the quality of higher education in the AI era. The research employed
a  rigorous  methodology,  incorporating  datasets  from  Pakistan  and  Malaysia.
The  sample  size  was  considerable,  offering  a  robust  foundation  for  analysis.  By
investigating  12  constructs  and  15  associated  hypotheses,  this  study  adopted  a
holistic  approach  to  understanding  the  multifaceted  factors  influencing  AI  tool
adoption. The statistical analysis employed path analysis, allowing for a compre-
hensive  examination  of  the  relationships  between  variables.  The  overall  sample
size of 305 respondents, with 203 from Pakistan and 102 from Malaysia, ensured
that  the  findings  were  sufficiently  representative.  The  12  constructs,  including
performance  expectancy,  facilitating  conditions,  and  information  accuracy,  and
others,  were  selected  based  on  existing  literature  highlighting  their  relevance  to
technology adoption in educational contexts. Corresponding hypotheses were for-
mulated and evaluated carefully using path analysis. The analysis of the datasets
revealed numerous critical insights. In all datasets, the research demonstrated that
students’ behavior intentions were crucial in determining their acceptance of AI
tools. This aligns with established technology adoption models, highlighting the
significance of user intentions in shaping technology adoption. In addition, a pos-
itive correlation between AI tool adoption and student satisfaction was observed
in  all  cases,  indicating  the  positive  impact  of  technology  acceptance  on  overall
educational experiences.

Limitations  While  this  study  offers  valuable  insights,  certain  limitations  should
be  acknowledged.  The  sample  size,  though  substantial,  might  not  fully  represent
the  diversity  within  Pakistan  and  Malaysia’s  higher  education  landscapes.  Moreo-
ver,  this  research  focused  exclusively  on  the  student  perspective,  and  future  stud-
ies should consider the perspectives of educators and administrators to gain a more
comprehensive understanding.

Future Work  To address the limitations, future research should provide more exten-
sive  and  diverse  samples  of  educational  institutions  and  regions  in  Pakistan  and
Malaysia.  Additionally,  exploring  the  perspectives  of  educators  and  administrators
could  provide  a  more  holistic  view  of  the  challenges  and  opportunities  associated
with  AI  adoption  in  higher  education.  Finally,  longitudinal  studies  could  trace  the
evolution  of  AI  tool  adoption  trends  over  time,  offering  valuable  insights  into  the
long-term  impact  of  these  technologies  on  higher  education.  Future  research  must
involve  comparative  analysis,  delving  into  data  to  pinpoint  areas  where  results
diverge.  We  propose  examining  differences  in  acceptance  rates,  perceived  benefits
and drawbacks of AI, and its impact on specific academic performance across both
countries. Furthermore, qualitative studies such as interviews and focus groups can
