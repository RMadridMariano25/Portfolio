1. Introduction

Soto et al. / Procedia Computer Science 00 (2024) 000–000

The outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic has catalyzed a digital and online transformation in the landscape of
higher education, compelling institutions worldwide to adapt and innovate in the face of unprecedented challenges.
With the imperative of ensuring academic continuity and protecting the interests of students, faculty, staff, and re-
searchers, higher education institutions (HEIs) have increasingly moved to online learning and e-learning as essential
mechanisms for delivering quality education in a remote and distributed fashion [6]. In the domain of technical ed-
ucation, particularly in disciplines such as computer networking, the demand for effective online learning solutions
has never been greater. As industries increasingly rely on digital infrastructure and connectivity, the need for skilled
professionals adept in IT, networking, and cloud concepts is on constant growth.

In this sense, the adoption of Interactive Learning Environments (ILEs) represents one such innovation that holds
immense promise for enriching the educational experience in a virtual context, and helping enhance student engage-
ment and motivation in educational processes [11, 15, 22]. By blending multimedia resources, simulations, remote
access to virtual devices, real-time interaction, and collaborative tools, ILEs offer students dynamic and immersive
learning environments that facilitate active engagement, knowledge construction, and skill development [13]. These
environments enable learners to explore concepts at their own pace and receive immediate feedback, leading to im-
proved learning outcomes. This learner-centered approach is particularly beneficial in technical disciplines such as
programming and computer networking, where practical skills and problem-solving abilities are of the essence [5, 19].
Moreover, advancements in generative AI have opened up new possibilities for enhancing the interactivity and re-
sponsiveness of ILEs. Chatbots, in particular, have emerged as versatile tools for providing personalized support and
guidance to learners. The novel and well-known ChatGPT, developed and launched by OpenAI in 2022, is a prominent
example of AI-driven conversational agents capable of understanding and generating human-like text responses [17].
As an emerging AI technology, ChatGPT is being extensively explored as a tool to enhance personalized and interac-
tive learning experiences [1, 20, 21]. Furthermore, it has also been applied in multiple educational contexts, including
tutoring, content generation, formative feedback, and generating educational content and assignments [3, 16, 25].

With the surge of online learning, interactive learning, and generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, HEIs face the
challenges of imparting comprehensive technical knowledge while leveraging innovative pedagogical approaches that
resonate with digital-native learners. This paper seeks to address these challenges by examining the efficacy of an
ILE enhanced with ChatGPT in the context of computer networking education. Drawing on insights from a pilot
experiment conducted with three graduate students, the impact of integrating ChatGPT within an ILE on students’
engagement, comprehension, and overall learning outcomes is assessed.

2. Related Work

The integration of ChatGPT within ILEs has generated both excitement and concerns among students, educators,
and researchers since it was released to the public in November 2022. Recent systematic reviews on ChatGPT show
its uses, applications, opportunities, challenges, and impact, not only in higher education but in many other fields such
as economics and STEM sciences as well [9, 23].

Recent research on the integration of ChatGPT in ILEs is focused on highlighting its potential to enhance the
learning experience and the challenges faced by students and teachers. A study by [8] explored the benefits of inte-
grating ChatGPT in education in Thailand. The authors reported on their main findings that ChatGPT could adapt to
individual students’ preferences to provide personalized learning and that educators could focus more on personalized
interactions with students by delegating some administrative tasks such as grading and feedback. A multi-case study
laboratory experiment conducted with both undergraduate and graduate students in Australia [4] explored the use of
ChatGPT as a classroom aid in completing particular tasks while looking at its impact on learning outcomes and expe-
riences. Their findings suggest a positive effect on functionality and student comprehension among the control groups
that used ChatGPT. Similarly, a case study conducted in another Asian university used ChatGPT in the context of AI
teaching and learning support tools in an integrated circuit systems course [14], while using interactive programming
exercises and code snippets. Other studies have also looked at the use of ChatGPT in general computer education and
data sciences with favorable results promoting the use of AI tools and large language models in interactive learning
[10, 2].

However, despite the positive learning outcomes and personalized experiences reported by students, the use and
effective adoption of AI tools such as ChatGPT in education still poses many challenges. As discussed by [7], key
challenges and risks such as bias and fairness, copyright issues, students and teachers becoming heavily reliant on the
tools, lack of understanding and expertise, difficulties in distinguishing AI-generated and student-generated content,
and data and privacy issues, mandates for the need of a clear strategy and pedagogical approach from all stakeholders
including students, teachers, staff, and researchers, to effectively integrate AI tools in interactive learning settings and
personalized learning in a responsible and ethical manner.

In line with these previous efforts, this research aspires to contribute to the expanding body of literature concerning
the application of ChatGPT within educational environments, in particular exploring the opportunities and challenges
of its integration within ILEs.

3. Methodology

3.1. Experiment Overview

This experiment aims to assess the effectiveness of integrating ChatGPT within an ILE in the context of computer
networking education. Specifically, it seeks to measure the impact of ChatGPT on students’ engagement, compre-
hension, and overall learning outcomes. Three graduate students participated in a series of interactive lessons deliv-
ered through the ILE while using ChatGPT as a learning support and consultation tool. By analyzing pre- and post-
assessment tests, quizzes, and student feedback, this study aims to determine whether ChatGPT enhanced students’
learning experience and contributed to a better comprehension of networking concepts. Additionally, a recording of
the computer screen of each student was taken during the entire experiment for further analysis. The research received
ethical approval from an IRB committee of the Cybermedia Center, Osaka University (code number-2023-22).

While students had access to ChatGPT during quizzes and the post-test, its primary role was as a support tool
to enhance comprehension and articulate information regarding configuration snippets and interconnections between
network devices. It was not used for directly soliciting answers to specific problems. In the context of this experiment,
comprehension and learning outcomes are defined by students’ performance and scores on tests and quizzes, as well as
their ability to apply the acquired knowledge to effectively resolve network troubleshooting scenarios, and showcase
the practical utility of both the ILE and ChatGPT.

3.2. Participants

Due to the nature of a pilot experiment, it was decided to conduct the lessons with a small number of participants
to have direct live input and feedback from the students while measuring the effectiveness of the lessons and the
performance of all systems. Therefore, only three graduate students with a basic IT networking background inside the
School of Computer Science and Technologies were invited for this experiment.

3.3. Learning Program

The main topic selected for the class was on introductory concepts of computer networking and IP routing pro-
tocols. The learning program was organized into two sessions of 30 minutes for pre- and post-assessment tests and
surveys and two sessions of 90 minutes for lessons’ content and quizzes. A layout of the learning program and learn-
ing activities is presented in Table 1. An introductory lesson on how to use ChatGPT and the ILE’s features was
conducted by a designated professor (Lesson 1 in Table 1). Additionally, students were given a basic summary of
the concepts and learning expectations at the beginning of each lesson before utilizing the ILE. During the ILE and
ChatGPT exercise (Lesson 2 - 4 in Table 1), they learned each topic by themselves through the ILE.

ChatGPT instructions were provided at least once during each lesson, allowing students the autonomy to utilize
it at their discretion thereafter. In Lesson 1, students were introduced to ChatGPT’s usage and encouraged to exper-
iment with related concepts. In Lesson 2, instructions were given to use ChatGPT in comparing the advantages and
disadvantages of Static Routing. Lesson 3 involved using ChatGPT to discern disparities between RIPv1 and RIPv2.
Finally, in Lesson 4, students received guidance on understanding OSPF Areas and how to properly implement them.

each student during the experiment. A timeline of the ChatGPT queries from each student is presented in Fig. 5. This

the post-test (100%) after having the lowest score on the pre-test (13%). This insight could indicate that the use of
ChatGPT combined with ILEs could have a positive effect on learning.

and adaptive learning experiences. Tailored responses from ChatGPT could deepen understanding and engagement,

logs could also be used to track progress, customize instructions, and further enhance the learning experience.

6. Limitations and Future Work

Some limitations of this study were in terms of sample size, interface usability, timely assistance, balanced inte-
gration between both the ILE and ChatGPT, scalability, and validity concerns. As an initial pilot experiment, lessons
were conducted with only three students, thus limiting the sample size and the significance of the results. However,
insights from the data and feedback from the students are being used to improve the overall experiment. For the next
steps, the experiment is scheduled to be conducted on a larger scale to improve the validity of the results.

Ease of use of the ChatGPT interface was one of the major complaints from the students. In that aspect, improve-
ments such as the placement and size of the ChatGPT chatbox, increasing the responsiveness of the systems through
better API and ChatGPT settings, and enhancing the interaction process by providing more accurate instructions, are
being improved promptly. The integration between the ILE and ChatGPT is also under refinement. Lessons’ contents
are being redefined from an instructional design perspective, trying to provide more opportunities for students to
interact with ChatGPT without underwhelming the instructional content, thus leveraging the benefits of both tools.

While the pilot has provided valuable preliminary insights, addressing scalability and validity concerns is criti-
cal for future research with larger student groups. Expected challenges include methods for assessing the similarity
between the sentences in the course materials and the ChatGPT prompts, and ensuring the validity of ChatGPT re-
sponses. To overcome these challenges, automated methods for computing similarity will be implemented, leveraging
natural language processing techniques to streamline the analysis process efficiently. Furthermore, other statistical
techniques such as pre- and post-test control group design with and without ChatGPT and effect size calculation
methods are under consideration to validate the effectiveness of ChatGPT on learning outcomes across students.

7. Conclusion

A pilot experiment was conducted integrating an ILE [18] and ChatGPT [17]. Three graduate students took lessons
on introductory concepts of computer networking topics. Knowledge assessment and satisfaction with the ILE were
made through surveys and pre- and post-tests and quizzes. Overall results indicate a positive experience for the stu-
dents in regards to the ILE, the use of ChatGPT, and the experiment settings.

Quizzes, pre- and post-test scores indicate that using ILEs and generative AI tools with immediate feedback may

As for the next steps, subsequent experiments are scheduled to be conducted on a larger scale to improve the

the interface of the systems, and to balance the interactions between the ILE and ChatGPT.
