Emerging discussions have boomed in relation to recent trends and applications of generative artificial intelli-
gence in education, but few attention were paid to AI chatbots in terms of critical thinking ability and second
language (L2) writing self-efficacy. To address such research gap, this study aims to delve into effectiveness of AI
chatbot-integrated writing tasks on critical thinking ability and writing self-efficacy among vocational college
students.  Followed  the  quasi-experiment  design,  this  study  recruited  80  first-year  vocational  college  students
from two intact class. Throughout seven-week’s instruction, quantitative data were collected with pre- and post-
questionnaires of writing self-efficacy and critical thinking ability. The results revealed no statistical difference in
terms  of  both  critical  thinking  ability  and  writing  self-efficacy  even  with  greater  increase  among  the  experi-
mental  group  than  that  of  the  control  peers.  Further  analysis  of  each  dimension  demonstrated  the  statistical
difference of language construction, indicating that vocational college students with limited language proficiency
strive to promote language organization in their writings in terms of critical thinking ability development. The
research  contributes  valuable  empirical  evidence  of  incorporating  generative  AI  chatbots  into  L2  writing  by
extending the Cognitive Load Theory and Social Cognitive Theory. It also underscores the need to leveraging AI
chatbots  as  tools  for  cognitive  transformation  rather  than  mere  procedural  assistance,  and  relevant  training
programs  are  essential  to  optimize  the  educational  benefits  of  AI  tools  and  cultivate  digital  literacy  among
vocational college learners.

1. Introduction

Second language (L2) writing is known as a demanding process for
learners  to  elaborate  coherent  and  arguable  content  with  clear  struc-
tures on specified topics around the globe (Golparvar & Khafi, 2021; Yin
et al., 2023). This challenging issue also imposes impressive pressure on
English  teachers  in  Chinese  vocational  colleges  which  had  to  admit
low-achieved language learners according to the current entrance exam
system (Tadesse et al., 2022; Zhang & Osman, 2024). While recognizing
the importance of English learning, vocational students are demotivated
and reluctant to spare their efforts in studying English for a lack of in-
terest  or  adequate  learning  strategies  (Chuane  et  al.,  2023).  The  lin-
guistic deficiencies not only impair L2 writing performance and  their
self-efficacy  (Hao  &  Lu,  2024),  but  also  constitute  a  major  cognitive
for  their  decision-making  along  with  critical  thinking

terms of writing self-efficacy between the experimental group and
the control group?

2. Literature review

2.1. AI chatbots and L2 writing

Chatbots are not a new thing that have been widely applied into the
education since the early 1970s. With the technological advancements,
the types of chatbots have been updated from text-based, voice-based to
multi-modal ones (Han, 2020; Luo et al., 2022). AI chatbots are featured

2

with intuitive and engaging interactions akin to human beings, which
cannot only help learners construct theoretical knowledge by answering
questions across different disciplines (Cheng et al., 2024; Jeon, 2021;
Kohnke, 2023; Kuo & Chen, 2023), but also offer reflective and empa-
thetic communication for emotional or psychological support (Liu et al.,
2024; Pani et al., 2024). Based on a systematic review of 24 articles, Ji
et al. (2023) summarized four roles of AI chatbots as “conversational
partner, feedback provider, resource provider, and needs analyst”. To
illustrate, the interaction with AI chatbots cannot only respond language
learners with individualized feedback at any time and location, but also
enable them to acquire adaptive linguistic knowledge including vocab-
ulary, grammar, writing organization and coherence (Jeon, 2021).

Large language models trained on billions of human-generated texts
empowers AI chatbots with new features and potentials such as instant
feedback,  detection  of  writing  errors,  timely  assessment,  thereby
boosting  writing  enthusiasm  of  users  (Guo  et  al.,  2022).  AI  chatbots
cannot only serve as problem-solving assistants by answering questions
or generating texts to enriching their writing content (Ebadi & Rahimi,
2018; Kasneci et al., 2023). Moreover, clerical or logical errors in En-
glish  texts  can  be  detected  by  AI  chatbots  so  that  students  can
consciously  identify  language  inaccuracies  and  make  self-correction
based on feedback from AI chatbots (Kohnke et al., 2023; Zhang et al.,
2023). Therefore, AI chatbots could offer feedback and guidance on a
one-to-one  basis with  personalized  learning content  (Fidan &  Gencel,
2022),  thereby  creating  a  more  interactive  and  authentic  language
environment (Hwang et al., 2023).

2.3. Writing self-efficacy

Building  on  the  pioneering  study  of  Bandura  (1993),  researchers
have well explored the self-efficacy as a crucial learning incentive for
developing writing skills or competence (Kormos, 2012; McCarthy et al.,
1985). Emerging studies started to focus on domain-specific self-efficacy
to illustrate psychological, behavioral, and linguistic features in foreign
language  learning,  of  which  writing  self-efficacy  refers  to  “students’
self-confidence in their capabilities to complete writing tasks” (Zhang &
Zhang,  2024).  Bruning  et  al.  (2013) indicated  writing  ideation,  con-
ventions, and self-regulation as three dimensions of writing self-efficacy.
Building on such model, Teng et al. (2018) designed a three-factor scale
of writing self-efficacy to include satisfactory psychometric properties
from the perspective of linguistic, performance and self-regulatory fea-
tures. Sun and Wang (2020) further elaborated the linguistic informa-
tion of writing self-efficacy such as ideation, organization, grammar and
spelling, etc. Another empirical study from Tsao (2021) indicated the
lack  of  self-efficacy  for  writing  conventions  among  Taiwanese  high
school learners and highlighted the importance of instructing effective
methods to help learners compose grammatically correct sentences.

The significance of writing self-efficacy can be found in a plethora of
studies that explained its close connections with engagement, strategy
use and performance in the process of English writing. Learners’ efficacy
in linguistic, self-regulatory and language competence can support their
motivational  beliefs  of  engaging,  controlling  and  regulating  their
writing process (L. S. Teng, 2024), benefiting subsequent efforts of more
participation in L2 writing (Tsao, 2021), thus functioning as a mediating
effect  between  prior  language  performance  and  writing  achievement
(Hao & Lu, 2024). Zabihi (2018) found that writers with high level of
self-efficacy not only significantly predicted the complexity, accuracy,
and fluency of L2 narrative writing, but also influenced their affective
factors, such as decreasing their writing anxiety. In a similar vein, based
on  the  results  of  structural  equation  modelling,  Golparvar  and  Khafi
(2021) concluded the predicting role of linguistic, self-regulatory and
performance self-efficacy related to summary writing performance, and
also  highlighted  close  connections  between  linguistic  self-efficacy,
discourse synthesis and source use strategies. Zhang and Zhang (2024)
also profiled 396 Chinese students into low-, average- and high-achieved
groups  according  to  their  overall  self-efficacy  and  found  disparity  in
their reported use of self-regulated writing strategies alongside writing
achievement  in  three  groups.  As  such,  in  response  to  the  booming
development of artificial intelligence, more efforts should be made to
explore the effective tools and approaches of integrating the recent AI
technology with bolstering learners’ writing-efficacy.

2.4. Theoretical groundings

The present study is grounded on both the social cognitive theory
and the cognitive load theory. The social cognitive theory interprets that
learning takes place in a social context based on interaction among in-
dividual  factors,  environment,  and  their  behavior  (Bandura,  1986,
2014). For language learners, individuals could improve linguistic skills
through adjusting learning behavior based on feedback from teachers,
peers or technological tools (Fidan & Gencel, 2022; Link et al., 2022; M.
F. Teng, 2024). The progress based on AI-assisted writing could further
help low-achieved learners build their confidence, or self-efficacy that
would motivate them to make more dedication to L2 writing (Zhang &
Zhang,  2024).  Therefore,  it  is  suitable  to  apply  the  social  cognitive
theory into exploring the effectiveness of AI chatbot-integrated writing
tasks on writing self-efficacy from the perspective of the holistic learning

ecology.

The cognitive load theory illustrates the limited capacity of working
memory to process information simultaneously (Sweller, 1988), which
provides another theoretical foundation to support the critical thinking
in  the  AI-assisted  writing  process.  The  cognitive  load  of  pedagogical
tasks could be intrinsic, extraneous or germane (Sweller et al., 1998). To
illustrate, the complexity of the task with multiple steps at the same time
is  classified  into  intrinsic  cognitive  load,  while  the  extraneous  ones
represents  the  additional  load  of  tasks  that  does  not  contribute  to
learning (Sweller et al., 1998). Germane cognitive load is conducive to
the cognitive processing for deep learning due to its role of retaining
information  and  generating  schema  of  the  knowledge  to  be  learned
(Costley, 2021; Kalyuga, 2011). Recent studies argued that the support
from  AI  tools  can  help  learners  handle  complicated  information  and
alleviate cognitive load, which contributes to the development of critical
thinking ability (Fabio et al., 2025). Therefore, the cognitive load theory
is well-suited to observe the dynamics of critical thinking ability under
the context of AI-enhanced writing.

3. Methodology

3.1. Participants

The participants from two classes of non-English majors voluntarily
participated in the current study. They are 80 first-year vocational col-
lege students, averaging 19 years old (M = 18.91, SD = 0.72) and with
15 males and 65 females. Such gender distribution in the sample reflects
the enrolment pattern of the business major that attracts more female
students than male peers. Two classes were randomly divided into the
experimental group (EG, N = 40) and the control group (CG, N = 40).
Based on scores of their final exam in the first semester, there was no
significant difference (t = (cid:0) 0.645, df = 78, p = 0.521) in English per-
formance between the control group (M = 74.41, SD = 10.837) and the
experimental  group  (M  = 72.71,  SD  = 12.666).  The  College  English
course  was  instructed  by  the  same  lecturer  in  both  CG  and  EG,  and
participants  shared  similar  characteristics  as  native  Chinese-speaking
students  with  roughly  seven-year’s  English  learning  experience.  All
participants from two groups did not have any experience of applying AI
chatbots into language learning before.

3.2. The AI chatbot for this study

The current research employed an AI chatbot, namely Doubao AI, to
facilitate  the  L2  writing  process  given  its  database  on  large  language
models  and  availability  for  Chinese  vocational  learners.  This  AI-
powered tool, developed by ByteDance with 26 million monthly active
users,  is  featured  with  multiple  AI  characters  that  could  facilitate
interactive language practice and provide real-time answers on various
topics  from  knowledge  learning  to  regular  life  activities.  Language
learners  can  get  access  to this  chatbot through  their  smart phones  or
computers  for  the  use  of  text  generation,  image  generation,  English
language learning, and writing assistance. Specifically, students can also
consult the chatbot about goal setting and study schedule of preparing
language exams, and obtain learning materials according to personal-
ized  needs.  The  role-play  function  can  make  the  interaction  more
engaging and interesting.

writing self-efficacy. The 34-item critical thinking ability questionnaire
was formulated by Li and Liu (2024), with seven-factor model of infer-
ence (6 items), analysis (7 items), argument evaluation (4 items), lan-

than 0.8 (inference: α = 0.92; analysis: α = 0.93; argument evaluation: α

The writing self-efficacy questionnaire on five-point Likert scale was
adapted  from two  previous studies  (Bruning  et  al., 2013;  Teng  et  al.,
2018). It contained three dimensions, including 4-item self-efficacy for
writing  self-regulation  (with  the  sample  question  “I  can  set  goals  for
learning  English  writing  I  need  to  achieve.”),  4-item  self-efficacy  for
writing conventions (with the sample question “when writing in English,
I  can  compose  a  coherent  and  well-structured  essay”),  and  3-item
self-efficacy  for  writing  ideation  (with  the  sample  question  “when
writing in English, I can think of many words to express myself”). To
illustrate, ideation refers to writers’ abilities to generate ideas as the first
step of the writing task, convention refers to writers’ abilities to express
the generated ideas applying linguistics skills, and self-regulation refers
to  writers’  “self-management  and  effective  control  but  also  involves
judgments  about  cognitive  and  linguistic  features  as  writing  is  being

produced”  (Tsao,  2021).  The  scale  was  proved  with  strong  internal
consistency  with  Cronbach’s  alpha  coefficient  of  0.90,  and  with
adequate  construct  validity  (KMO  = 0.86).  The  alpha  coefficients  of
three dimensions were 0.81 for writing ideation, 0.84 for writing con-
ventions, and 0.81 for self-regulatory efficacy.

3.4. Experiment procedure

Ethical approval was obtained from the affiliation of the author(s)
and the participants before the experiment. The current study followed a
quasi-experimental  design,  which  consists  of  a  pre-  and  a  post-test
completed  by  both  the  control  and  experimental  groups  (Rogers  &
Revesz, 2019). Fig. 1 presented the experiment procedure of this study.
Before  the  experiment,  participants  from  two  groups  also  filled  out
pre-test  questionnaires  of  writing  self-efficacy  and  critical  thinking
ability.  Participants  in  the  experimental  group  attended  30  min’s
workshop to get familiar with the use of Doubao AI, essential prompts,
and  writing  rubrics.  Then,  students  of  both  groups  were  required  to
complete L2 writing tasks related to their opinions on online education,
technology  dependence,  reading  books,  aging  population,  studying
abroad,  holding  sports  events.  Both  two  classes  attended  the  College
English  course  twice  a  week  (90  min  each  time),  with  the  writing
learning arranged for 45 min every week. Participants were required to
submit  writing  drafts  of  eighty  to  one  hundred  words  completed  in
different  procedures  at  class  respectively  in  the  control  group  and
experimental  group.  After  the  experiment,  questionnaires  for  the
post-test were distributed to collect students’  writing self-efficacy and
critical thinking ability.

Participants  from  the  experimental  group  were  allowed  to  utilize
Doubao  AI  in  completing  their  writing  tasks.  For  each  writing  class
lasting for 45 min, their use of Doubao AI mainly centered on the process
of discussing about writing ideas (around 10 min) and polishing articles
(around  20  min).  Specifically,  the  instructor  assigned  the  topic  of  a
writing  task  at  the  beginning  of  each  class,  and  then  learners  were
required to generate ideas with the help of Doubao AI. They also needed
to reflect on the rationality or logic of opinions offered by Doubao AI
during  this  process.  The  chatbot  was  also  applied  by  participants  to
revise writing drafts from aspects of word choice, grammar, synonym
replacements or sentence structures (as shown in Fig. 2). The instructor
would be also consulted to address doubts that students could not un-
derstand. Finally, participants submitted their writing at the end of class.
The  arrangement  of  brainstorming,  writing,  and  revising  procedures
took  reference  from  the  study  from  Rahimi  and  Fathi  (2022),  who

feedback of their writing tasks in the next writing class.

3.5. Data analysis

SPSS 25.0 statistical software was used to analyze the pre- and post-
questionnaires. The data cleaning was made in terms of missing values,
outliers  and  inconsistencies.  The  skewness  and  kurtosis  fell  within
appropriate range of (cid:0) 2 and +2, indicating that the data were normally
distributed. Subsequently, descriptive analysis, independent samples t-
tests and one-way ANCOVA tests were made to present the statistical
results. Independent samples t-tests were employed to answer the first
research question with regard to the influence of AI chatbot-integrated
writing  tasks  on  participants’  critical  thinking  ability  and  its  seven

dimension.  In  response  to  the  second  research  question,  one-way
ANCOVA  was  adopted  to  assess  the  effect  of  AI  chatbot  on  writing
self-efficacy  and  its  three  dimensions.  The  statistical  difference  was
determined if p was lower than 0.05.

This  study  aimed  to  reveal  influences  of  AI  chatbot-integrated
writing  tasks  on  vocational  college  learners’  critical  thinking  ability
and writing self-efficacy. In response to the first question, both groups
made progress in overall critical thinking ability, but no statistical dif-
ference  was  identified  across  two  groups  when  it  comes  to  the  inter-
vention of AI chatbot-integrated writing tasks. Such finding is consistent
with a recent study from Liang and Wu (2024) who also reported that
participants  did  not  experience  significant  changes
the
ChatGPT-based  critical  thinking  skill  cultivation  programme.  The
improvement of critical thinking ability of both groups aligns with the
statement that such high-order thinking skill could be interdependent
with the L2 writing process when learners clearly expressed perspectives
and evaluated diversified information to formulate their logical argu-
ments (Spector & Ma, 2019; Yin et al., 2023). Moreover, the insignifi-
cant effect can be partly attributed to the short-term intervention on the
AI-mediated  writing  practice  and
limited  prior  experience  of
chatbot-based language learning among participants. Fabio et al. (2025)
reported that participants with the prolonged use of ChatGPT demon-
strated a significant improvement in critical thinking skills than those
who use it little and who do not use it at all. In line with the Cognitive
Load Theory, discriminating the accuracy of AI-generated feedback and
selecting  useful  responses  for  L2  writing  might  impose  extraneous
cognitive load for vocational learners without experiences and knowl-
edge of AI chatbot-based learning, which impedes the development of
their critical thinking ability (Derakhshan & Taghizadeh, 2025).

In  terms  of  specific  dimensions,  the  gap  of  language  construction
between two groups was a bit larger, indicating the better progress of
language  construction  among  students  who  employed  AI  chatbots  in
preparing their drafts. By contrast, the statistical difference in cultural
comparison before the intervention disappeared after the experiment,
which  reflected  that  vocational  college  students  with  below-average
language  proficiency  still  endeavored  to  promote  language  organiza-
tion  in  their  writings,  but  overlooked  the  in-depth  reflection  on  the
cultural comparison. With a view to this issue, Du and Reynolds (2025)
argued that the frequent use of the surface-level Q&A with AI tools made
it difficult for language learners to have deep reasoning, which demands
for essential teacher support from the perspectives of prompting design,
emotional engagement, and ethical stewardship. By contrast, learners
with advanced linguistic skills put more focus using chatbots to improve
their arguments, rather than merely error correction (Guo, 2024). Du
and  Zhang  (2022) also  underscored  that  higher-achieving  students
demonstrated better perceptions of the critical thinking learning affor-
dances than lower-achieving students. Therefore, the training of meta-
cognitive awareness, such as cultural comparison, can be intentionally
integrated  into  writing  tasks  after  vocational  college  learners  have  a
mastery use of AI-enhanced language learning.

When it came to the second research question, an increase of writing
self-efficacy was observed among participants of two groups after the
experiment,  and  results  of  ANCOVA  did  not  reveal  a  statistically  sig-
nificant  difference  between  the  experimental  and  control  groups  in
writing  self-efficacy.  This  suggests  that  the  improvements  cannot  be
completely attributed to the assistance of the AI chatbot. Similarly, Deng
et al. (2025) also reported that the use of ChatGPT had no significant
effect on self-efficacy based on a meta-analysis review of 62 empirical
studies.  Moreover,  Zhang  et  al.  (2023) indicated  that  chatbot-based
learning  might  reduce  students’  writing  self-efficacy  according  to  the
semi-structured interviews from 15 participants, because they became

6. Implications

6.1. Implications for research

Findings of this study provides pedagogical implications for future
research. It extends the application of Cognitive Load Theory and Social
Cognitive Theory to the emerging domain of L2 writing assisted by AI
chatbots. According to the Cognitive Load Theory, incorporating the AI
chatbot  into  L2  writing  might  lead  to  extraneous  load  for  vocational
learners  without  experiences  and  knowledge  of  AI  chatbot-based
learning,  while  the  germane  cognitive  load  helped  them  employ  AI
tools to have cognitive processing for deep learning in the long term,
which benefited their progress of critical thinking ability (Fabio et al.,
2025).  The  shift  from  extraneous  load  to  germane  cognitive  load  re-
quires essential strategies of leveraging chatbots as tools for cognitive
transformation  rather  than  procedural  assistance  (Du  &  Reynolds,
2025).  Moreover,  drawing  on  the  Social  Cognitive  Theory,  human
teachers are expected to offer the additional and continuous support for
vocational learners to enhance mastery experiences and the regulation
of  emotional  and  physiological  states  in  the  AI-enhanced  language
learning (Deng et al., 2025). In this case, tripartite interactions among
teachers,  students,  and  generative  AI  are  pivotal  for  addressing  the
critical challenge of writing self-efficacy in the post-ChatGPT era (Guan
et al., 2025).

7. Conclusion and limitations

This  study  intended  to  explore  the  effectiveness  of  AI  chatbot-
integrated  writing  tasks  on  writing  self-efficacy  and  critical  thinking
ability among vocational college learners. The results revealed no sta-
tistical difference in terms of both critical thinking ability and writing
self-efficacy even with greater increase among the experimental group
than  that  of  the  control  peers.  Further  analysis  of  each  dimension
demonstrated the  statistical difference of  language construction, indi-
cating  that  vocational  college  students  with  limited  language  profi-
ciency strive to promote language organization in their writings in terms
of critical thinking ability development. The present study extended the
Cognitive Load Theory and Social Cognitive Theory into the emerging
context of AI chatbot-assisted L2 writing, and highlights the need to shift
learners from extraneous to germane cognitive load through strategies
that treat chatbots as tools for cognitive transformation rather than mere
procedural assistance. It also emphasized the role of human teachers to
foster writing self-efficacy through tripartite interactions among teach-
ers, students, and generative AI. Finally, policymakers are suggested to
organize training programs with the purpose of optimizing the educa-
tional benefits  of AI tools  and cultivating  digital literacy among  both
teachers and learners.

This study is not free from limitations. First of all, it is possible that
the short duration of this experiment failed to yield significant effect on
writing self-efficacy and critical thinking ability, which suggests a lon-
gitudinal study to ascertain the long-term effect of AI-assisted language
learning in future research. Secondly, only self-reported data of writing
self-efficacy  and  critical  thinking  ability  was  measured  to  reflect  the
progress in this experiment, and future studies can combine qualitative
