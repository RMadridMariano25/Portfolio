Artiicial intelligence technologies, such as ChatGPT, Bert, LaMDA, and T5 have become deeply integrated into
universities, influencing numerous functions within higher education and various aspects of students’ lives. This
article examined how students’ perceptions of ChatGPT influence their engagement and performance in online
learning,  focusing  on  the  mediating  role  of  engagement.  The  perception  of  ChatGPT  involves  a  student’s  un-
derstanding of, willingness to utilize, and concerns regarding the technology. These interconnected factors can
influence  students’  online  learning  engagement  with  the  software  and,  consequently,  affect  their  academic
performance. The study used self-reported data from 305 participants at a large university in Iran. The findings
from Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) revealed that students’ perception of ChatGPT accounts for 19.2 % of
the variance in online learning engagement, while the combination of ChatGPT perception and online learning
engagement explains 40.4 % of the variance in academic performance. Furthermore, students’  perceptions of
ChatGPT had direct effects on online learning engagement (β = 0.438) and academic performance (β = 0.268).
Additionally, online engagement significantly mediated this relationship, with an indirect effect of β  = 0.206.
The observed direct and indirect effects highlighted the essential role of online learning engagement in fostering
interaction  and  enhancing  students’  performance.  Overall,  our  conceptual  model  provides  researchers  and
university  administrators  with  a  comprehensive  understanding  of  students’  perceptions  of  ChatGPT,  their
engagement, and their academic performance in an online setting. This model also provides valuable insights for
future  research  on  designing  student  activities  that  leverage  artificial  intelligence  technologies,  including
ChatGPT-based tools.

1. Introduction

Generative artificial intelligence such as ChatGPT has significantly
improved virtual education in terms of accessibility, productivity, and
adaptability, and has had significant implications for learners and their
educational achievements (Al-Qaysi et al., 2024; Nguyen, Stec, et al.,
2023;  Rangel-de  Lazaro  &  Duart,  2023;  Xing  et  al.,  2025;  Mehrvarz
et  al.,  2025).  Recent  studies  have  emphasized  the  increasing  role  of
artificial  intelligence  in  education,  particularly  through  AI-powered
mobile  and  cloud-based  learning  platforms  (El  Koshiry  et  al.,  2024)
and  machine  learning  techniques  for  predicting  student  challenges
(Koshiry et al., 2022). These emerging technologies enable AI tools, such

as  ChatGPT,  to  support  student  engagement,  facilitate  personalized
learning, and enhance academic outcomes. ChatGPT (Chat Generative
Pre-Trained  Transformer),  developed  by  OpenAI  and  released  in
November 2022, is a large language model (LLM) capable of generating
human-like  responses  across  a  wide  range  of  contexts  (Aydın  &  Kar-
aarslan, 2023; Teubner et al., 2023). It can process diverse text-based
queries, from simple prompts to complex tasks (Xu et al., 2024). As a
result, it has the potential to enhance students’ learning and academic
performance. Academic performance, which is widely regarded as a key
indicator of success in higher education (Mthimunye & Daniels, 2020),
refers  to  students’  capacity  to  complete  classroom  tasks,  engage  in
problem-solving  activities,  and  manage  academic  responsibilities

creating an economic equality in access.

education, students—who are one of the primary stakeholders—remain
underrepresented  in  this  discourse.  Their  understanding,  acceptance,

within a single model in the context of higher education.

2. Background

This section reviews existing research on the educational impact of
ChatGPT,  highlighting  its  potential  to  enhance  learning  outcomes,
engagement, and motivation, while also addressing concerns related to
overreliance  and  diminished  critical  thinking.  An  increasing  body  of
evidence  suggests  that  ChatGPT  can  positively  influence  students’
engagement and academic performance (Wang & Fan, 2025).

Several  studies  indicate  that  students’  positive  perceptions  of
ChatGPT are associated with improved academic outcomes (Caratiquit
& Caratiquit, 2023; Odeniyi, 2024; Singh et al., 2023). ChatGPT facili-
tates  personalized  learning,  supports  various  learning  styles,  and  re-
sponds to the diverse needs of students (Jayaputri, 2024; Nguyen et al.,
2024). Baek et al. (2024), in a large-scale U.S. survey, reported that most
college students had started using ChatGPT for tasks such as writing and
coding.  Usage  varied  based  on  student  demographics  and  institution
type, with older students and non-native English speakers using it more
frequently, particularly for assistance with writing. This indicates stu-
dents find ChatGPT especially beneficial for overcoming language bar-
riers. In a study by Ding et al. (2023), undergraduate physics students
used ChatGPT as a virtual tutor. Although the tool occasionally provided
incorrect information, most students still trusted it to deliver accurate
answers.  The  researchers  observed  a  strong  connection  between  stu-
dents’  trust  in  ChatGPT  and  their  positive  views  of  it  as  a  valuable
learning resource. Similarly, Albayati (2024) found that students who
perceived  ChatGPT  as  easy  to  use  and  helpful  generally  developed
favorable attitudes toward using it. Al-Qaysi et al. (2024) explored the
factors affecting students’ use of ChatGPT and found a significant pos-
itive impact on learning performance. Importantly, their study empha-
sized that students’  motivations and concerns—such as ease of use or
fear of dependency—play a key role in shaping their attitudes toward
adopting such tools. Not only in the context of ChatGPT but also in other
domains,  researchers  have  demonstrated  how  AI-driven  tools  can
enhance learner engagement and personalize learning experiences. For
instance,  Badawy  et  al.  (2021) developed  a  system  that  utilizes
NLP-based topic extraction and interactive knowledge graphs, allowing
students to explore learning resources dynamically and independently.
Similarly,  Mahmoud  et  al.  (2013) proposed  an  intelligent  e-learning
framework  leveraging semantic  web technologies to  deliver personal-
ized content tailored to individual learning preferences. These studies
illustrate the broader potential of NLP and AI tools to provide adaptive
support,  aligning  closely  with  the  role  of  ChatGPT  in  facilitating
personalized, engaging educational interactions.  This aligns  with pre-
vious research indicating that students primarily benefit from AI chat-
bots through personalized learning support and homework assistance.
For  instance,  Labadze  et  al.  (2023),  in  their  systematic  review  of  67
studies, concluded that educational chatbots offer adaptive explanations

and resources tailored to individual learning preferences, enabling stu-
dents  to  independently  address  knowledge  gaps.  Thus,  integrating
ChatGPT  and  similar  AI-based  tutoring  tools  presents  a  promising
pathway  toward  learner-centered  education,  effectively  adapting  in-

Building  on  this,  several  studies  have  gone  beyond  performance
metrics to examine ChatGPT’s emotional, motivational, and behavioral
impacts.  These  studies  showed  ChatGPT  can  improve  online  learning
engagement (Ivanov & Soliman, 2023; Jayaputri, 2024; Kasneci et al.,
2023; Lai et al., 2023), as it provides interactive and context-rich con-
tent, reduces boredom, and enhances immersion experiences (Qu & Wu,
2024). For example, Qu and Wu (2024) showed that students’ percep-
tion  of  ChatGPT  as  a  useful  and  efficient  learning  tool  significantly
increased  their  emotional  engagement.  Their  findings  underscore  the
importance of perceived usefulness, suggesting that when learners view
AI tools as beneficial and tailored to their needs, they experience greater
control, reduced boredom, and increased enjoyment. These emotional
benefits align with Heung and Chiu’s (2025) meta-analysis of 17 studies,
which reported medium-sized positive effects of ChatGPT on emotional
engagement (e.g., enthusiasm and interest) and large positive effects on
cognitive engagement, indicating deeper intellectual involvement. Such
emotional connections foster stronger learning motivation and persis-
tence (Heung &  Chiu, 2025). Similarly, research on mobile platforms,
such as Edmodo, found that enhancing psychological security, particu-
larly among marginalized students, significantly boosted engagement,
reinforcing  the  importance  of  emotional  comfort  in  digital  learning
environments (El Koshiry et al., 2024). Research by Cai et al. (2023) and
Nguyen et al. (2024) confirmed that students who perceive ChatGPT as
facilitating  meaningful  learning  are  more  actively  engaged  online.  In
fact, ChatGPT enhances student engagement through personalized re-
sponses, timely feedback, and rapid information access, increasing both
learner autonomy and enjoyment (Bettayeb et al., 2024). Additionally,
Guo et al. (2025) observed higher student motivation and confidence
linked  to  ChatGPT’s  continuous  availability  and  responsiveness  over
extended  usage.  One  explanation  for  heightened  engagement  is
ChatGPT’s ability to reduce cognitive load and facilitate learning. Sup-
porting  this,  Wang  and  Fan  (2025) conducted  a  meta-analysis  of  51
studies and reported a large effect size (g = 0.87) for ChatGPT’s impact
on  academic  performance,  alongside  moderate  improvements  in
learning  perceptions  and  higher-order  thinking.  Their  analysis  also
indicated  significant  improvements  in  behavioral  engagement  when
students  actively  interacted  with  ChatGPT,  although  instances  of
behavioral disengagement were noted when the tool was misused (Wang
& Fan, 2025). Collectively, these findings suggest that ChatGPT provides
substantial cognitive and affective benefits, positively influencing stu-
dent engagement and reducing cognitive load.

However, alongside these promising results, some researchers have
expressed  caution  regarding  the  potential  downsides  of  widespread
ChatGPT use, particularly the risk of overreliance. Although studies by
Ivanov  and  Soliman  (2023) and  Kasneci  et  al.  (2023) acknowledged
increases in motivation and performance, others highlighted concerns
that  dependence  on  ChatGPT  might  undermine  critical  thinking  and
academic integrity. For example, Lai et al. (2023) warned that habitual
use  of  ChatGPT  could  lead  to  passivity,  diminishing  students’
problem-solving skills over time. Expanding on this, Zhai et al. (2024)
synthesized  findings  from  14  empirical  studies  and  concluded  that
routine  overuse  of  AI  dialogue  systems  was  linked  to  measurable  de-
clines  in  critical  thinking,  decision-making,  and  analytical  reasoning.
Supporting this claim, Gerlich (2025), in a mixed-methods study of 666
participants,  identified  a  strong  negative  correlation  (r  = (cid:0) 0.68,  p  <
0.001) between AI tool usage and critical-thinking scores, with cognitive
offloading  emerging  as  a  key  mediating  factor.  These  studies  suggest
that while AI tools may offer some support, they could also pose some
risks to students’ independent thinking and learning autonomy.

Despite this growing interest, many existing studies tend to examine

these outcomes in isolation, such as investigating engagement without
performance, or perceptions without motivation. As a result, the com-
plex interplay between students’ perceptions, engagement, and learning
outcomes remains underexplored. Additionally, few studies have posi-
tioned  students  as  the  central  stakeholders  in  AI  integration,  often
overlooking  their  voices  in  shaping  how  these  technologies  are  used.
Therefore, a critical gap remains in understanding how students’ overall
perceptions of ChatGPT simultaneously influence their engagement and
academic performance—a gap that this study seeks to address.

3. Theoretical framework

This  study  is  grounded  in  two  theoretical  perspectives:  the  Tech-
nology  Acceptance  Model  (TAM)  and  Social  Cognitive  Theory  (SCT),
which together offer a robust framework for exploring students’ use and
perception of AI in learning.

3.1. Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)

The theoretical basis of this research is the Technology Acceptance
Model  (TAM),  which  was  first  introduced  by  Davis  in  1980  and  then
improved by Davis et al. (1989) and Davis (1993). This model is used to
explain  the  behavior  of  users  towards  technology;  In  other  words,  it
helps us to understand why users accept or reject a particular technology
and also to predict user behavior in using that technology (Davis et al.,
1989). In this model, the two main factors, “perceived usefulness” and
“perceived ease of use,”  which are influenced by “external variables,”
are key factors in the formation of “attitude toward using”. Davis (1986)
explains that perceived usefulness refers to an individual’s belief that
using a system will improve their job performance, while perceived ease
of use relates to the belief that the system requires minimal physical and
mental  effort  to  operate.  These  factors  influence  the  attitude  toward
using the system, which then shapes the intention to use it and even-
tually leads to actual usage.

Based on this theory, it can be said that for students to effectively
utilize ChatGPT, their attitude toward this technology—whether posi-
tive, negative, or mixed—is the most significant factor in its acceptance
and practical application (Acosta Enriquez et al., 2024). Regarding the
effect of “perceived usefulness” on students’ attitudes, it can be stated
that the more students believe that using ChatGPT helps them do their
class  work,  the  more  positive  their  attitude  towards  its  use  becomes,
ultimately leading to its usage. Conversely, negative attitudes and the
lack of use of ChatGPT arise from the belief that it does not significantly
impact  their  learning.  Furthermore,  in  relation  to  the  effect  of
“perceived  ease of  use”  on students’  attitudes, it  can be said that the
more students believe that using ChatGPT is easy, the more positive their
attitude  becomes,  leading  to  actual  use.  However,  if  they  perceive
ChatGPT  as  requiring  significant  physical  and/or  mental  effort,  their
attitude will turn negative, resulting in a lack of use.

Based on this theory, students’  acceptance of AI and their percep-
tions of its applications are key to the successful use of this technology
(Davis  et  al.,  1989;  Hazaimeh  &  Al-Ansi,  2024;  Gado  et  al.,  2022).
Studies have shown that students with a stronger understanding of AI
often experience lower levels of anxiety related to it (Bin Dahmash et al.,
2020;  Fulmer  et  al.,  2018;  Zhang  et  al.,  2024).  Understanding  how
students  perceive  this  technology  is  crucial,  as  their  perceptions  can
greatly impact  their willingness to engage  with the  tool and, in  turn,
affect their overall learning outcomes (Chen et al., 2023; Lai et al., 2023;
Yilmaz et al., 2023). Conversely, negative perceptions may diminish the
tool’s  effectiveness  and  restrict  its  potential  benefits  (Albayati,  2024;
Farhi et al., 2023). The present study employed the framework proposed
by Chan and Hu (2023) to investigate students’ perceptions of ChatGPT.
According  to  Chan  and  Hu  (2023),  students’  perceptions  of  ChatGPT
refer to three main factors: Knowledge of Generative Artificial Intelli-
gence Technology (KGAT), Willingness to Use Generative Artificial In-
telligence  Technologies  (WUGAT),  and  Concerns  about  Generative

3.2. Social Cognitive Theory (SCT)

Social  Cognitive  Theory  (SCT)  of  Bandura  (1986) explains  human
performance  in  terms  of  “triadic  reciprocal  causation”,  where  “per-
sonal”, “behavioral”, and “environmental” factors are the determinants
of an individual’s performance, mutually influencing each other. Based
on this theory, it can be said that “personal” factors such as beliefs, at-
titudes, interests, motivation, cognitive structure, and other individual
differences  can  influence  the  extent  of  students’  online  learning
engagement with ChatGPT. For instance, if students believe that using
ChatGPT  is  beneficial  for  their  academic  performance  or  if  they  are
personally interested in using it, they will have higher engagement with
the tool. “Behavioral” factors also determine the level of students’ online
learning engagement with ChatGPT. When a student makes an effort to
use ChatGPT for learning (such as searching for how to use it, practicing
with friends to use it, or seeking solutions when encountering problems
in using it), and they experience positive outcomes and benefits in their
academic performance, this behavior is reinforced, and they are likely to
continue  using  it  with  increased  engagement.  Additionally,  if  “envi-
ronmental”  factors promote and support the use of ChatGPT, students
are  more likely  to  engage  with it.  For  instance,  if  students  have  easy
access  to  ChatGPT  without  any  external  barriers,  if  instructors
encourage its use, or if the educational structure of the society actively
promotes and encourages the use of such technologies in the learning
process, students are more likely to become engaged in using ChatGPT.
The combination of these factors, which mutually influence one another,
can affect students’ online learning engagement with ChatGPT and ul-
timately impact their academic performance.

In this study, online learning engagement is described as the extent
to which students invest time, energy, interest, attitudes, learning stra-
tegies, and creative thinking in e-learning environments, as well as the
motivational and action processes that emerge in these contexts (Yang
et al., 2018). It has three main dimensions: behavioral, cognitive, and
emotional  (Deng  et  al.,  2020;  Kuo  et  al.,  2021;  Sun  &  Rueda,  2012).
Behavioral  engagement  refers  to  the  visible  actions  essential  for  aca-
demic success, including attending classes, actively participating, and
completing assignments in the online learning environment (Fredricks
et  al.,  2004;  Gregory  et  al.,  2014;  Nguyen  et  al.,  2018).  Emotional
engagement encompasses learners’ overall emotional responses to their
learning  experiences,  such  as  feelings  of  interest,  enthusiasm,  enjoy-
ment, vitality, frustration, or boredom, as well as their sense of social
connection  with  others,  rather  than  their  temporary  emotional  states
(Fredricks et al., 2004; Skinner et al., 2008). Cognitive engagement re-
fers  to  the  focused  effort  learners  invest  in  deeply  understanding  the

4. Research hypotheses and models

4.1. Students’ perceptions of ChatGPT and online learning engagement

Students’  perceptions  of  technology  are  pivotal  in  shaping  their
willingness  to  use  it,  which  in  turn  affects  its  integration  into  the
learning process and overall engagement (Acosta Enriquez et al., 2024;
Chan & Hu, 2023; Lai et al., 2023). As previously mentioned, behavioral
engagement includes the visible actions essential for academic success,
such as attending classes, actively participating, and dedicating time to
completing  tasks  and  assignments  in  an  online  environment  (Bond,
2020; Fredricks et al., 2004; Gregory et al., 2014; Nguyen et al., 2018).
Malik et al. (2023) examined students’ attitudes toward using AI tools in
academic essay writing and found that these tools improved students’
writing  skills,  self-efficacy,  and  understanding  of  academic  integrity.
Students  emphasized  the  advantages  of  AI  tools,  including  grammar
correction,  plagiarism  detection,  language  translation,  and  essay  out-
lining  assistance.  In  this  context,  students  can  utilize  ChatGPT  to
proofread and enhance the quality of their writing (Liu &  Ma, 2024).
Additionally,  regarding  emotional  and  cognitive  engagement,  Liang
et  al.  (2024) demonstrated  that  AI-based  chatbots  could  significantly
enhance  learners’  metacognitive  awareness,  emotional  engagement,
and  behavioral  engagement,  showing  that  generative  AI  tools  like
ChatGPT  offer  new  opportunities  to  serve  as  smart  learning  partners,
providing  immediate  and  adaptive  feedback  that  fosters  double-loop
learning.  Qu  and  Wu  (2024) also  conducted  an  experimental  study
with  40  students  from  the  Fisheries  Academy  in  Biak,  Indonesia,
demonstrating that ChatGPT significantly enhanced student motivation
for  learning  English  through  personalized  and  real-time  interactions.
Their  statistical analysis  of  pre-  and post-test  data revealed  a  notable
improvement in motivation after the introduction of ChatGPT, driven by
its  adaptive  learning  features  and  user-friendly  interface  (Qu  &  Wu,
2024).

While ChatGPT offers potential benefits in enhancing engagement, it
is equally important to recognize its limitations. Several studies suggest
that the ease with which ChatGPT provides information and problem-
solving  assistance  may  diminish  students’  motivation  for  deep
thinking  and  independent  problem-solving  (Chan  &  Hu,  2023;  Ding
et al., 2023; Sallam, 2023), potentially leading to a decline in academic
performance  over  time  (Nguyen  et  al.,  2024).  On  the  other  hand,
Stampfl et al. (2024) found that integrating AI, such as ChatGPT, into
educational  simulations  significantly  enhances  student  engagement,
critical  thinking,  and  communication  skills  by  connecting  theoretical
knowledge  to  practical  applications.  However,  students’  feedback
emphasized  the  need  for  enhancements  in  both  interactivity  and
emotional engagement. One student remarked, ‘A real conversation with
a supervisor would be more emotional and interactive,’ while another
stated, ‘ChatGPT is not mature enough to be a good negotiation partner.’
Thus,  while  ChatGPT  shows  great  promise,  its  current  limitations  in
fostering  emotionally  rich,  interactive  learning  experiences  pose  a
challenge.

Given the evidence from prior studies, while ChatGPT could foster
engagement,  it  may  also  present  challenges  to  encouraging  deeper
learning  and  engagement.  Balancing  these  potential  benefits  and
drawbacks is crucial in understanding the overall impact of ChatGPT on
student engagement. This leads us to our first hypothesis to be studied:

4.2. Online learning engagement and academic performance

One  of  the  crucial  factors  in  the  learning  process  is  learning
engagement, which is positively related to outcomes such as academic
success and dropout rates (Fredricks et al., 2004; Kuo et al., 2021). Many
studies support the claim that student engagement in online courses has
a significant impact on academic performance and learning outcomes
(Kuzminykh et al., 2021; Pardo et al., 2016; Schaeffer & Konetes, 2010;
Wang et al., 2022; You, 2022). In an online program, Kuzminykh et al.
(2021) explored the connection between student engagement and aca-
demic  performance.  Using  data  such  as  content  views,  forum  posts,
completed  assignments,  and  video  watching,  their  study  identified  a
positive  correlation  between  student  engagement  and  academic  per-
formance. Similarly, You (2022) examined the impact of e-learning in-
vestment on learning completion in modern educational models, such as
micro  classrooms,  MOOCs,  and  flipped  classrooms.  Drawing  on
self-efficacy  and  exploratory  community  theory,  the  study  concluded
that students’ engagement in e-learning significantly enhanced learning
completion.  Learning  engagement  is  directly  linked  to  students’  aca-
demic success, including attendance and exam performance (Vayre &
Vonthron,  2019),  while  a  lack  of  engagement  increases  the  risk  of
dropout (Kuo et al., 2021; Lee & Choi, 2011).

Although research consistently demonstrates that academic perfor-
mance is positively influenced by online learning engagement (Fredricks
et al., 2004; Kuo et al., 2021; Vayre & Vonthron, 2019), Kuo et al. (2021)
emphasized that further exploration is necessary. They noted that most
studies primarily focus on behavioral engagement—the actions students
take  to  participate  in  the  learning  process—while  emotional  and
cognitive  engagement  are  equally  important  yet  underexplored  di-
mensions. One of our hypotheses in this study aims to fill this gap by
examining  how  all  three  dimensions  of  engagement—behavioral,
emotional,  and  cognitive—impact  academic  performance  in  online
learning environments. Thus, this leads us to our second hypothesis:

Hypothesis  2. Students’  online learning engagement is significantly
correlated with their academic performance.

4.3. Students’ perception of ChatGPT and academic performance

Studies  have  shown  that  students’  perceptions  of  AI  tools  such  as
ChatGPT  can  significantly  influence  their  academic  performance
(Caratiquit & Caratiquit, 2023; Odeniyi, 2024; Singh et al., 2023). In a
related study, Al Shloul et al. (2024) employed a comparative approach
to evaluate the impact of ChatGPT in conjunction with traditional and
activity-based  learning  methods.  The  results  showed  that  ChatGPT
provided individualized assistance, facilitated interactive learning, and
improved students’ understanding of complex concepts, contributing to
enhanced academic performance. This highlights the potential of both
ChatGPT  and  activity-based  learning  in  fostering  active  participation
and  practical  learning,  ultimately  leading  to  better  educational  out-
comes. In another study, Xu et al. (2024) noted, through semi-structured
interviews  with  professors  and  ICT  experts,  that  ChatGPT  supports
learning by generating trustworthy information, providing personalized
resources, and fostering motivation, perseverance, self-regulation, and
critical thinking.

Despite  the  benefits  of  ChatGPT  on  students’  performance  and
learning, several studies have criticized its responses for lacking depth,
contextual  understanding,  and  accuracy  (Nguyen,  Ngo,  et  al.,  2023;
Nguyen,  Stec,  et  al.,  2023;  S´anchez-Ruiz  et  al.,  2023;  Stampfl  et  al.,
2024),  which  can  negatively  impact  students’  performance.  For
example,  a  study  by  Sila  et  al.  (2023) revealed  that  while  students
recognized the benefits of ChatGPT—such as enhancing their learning
experience  and  saving  time—they  also  expressed  concerns  about  the
reliability and accuracy of the information provided. Another study by
Mezzaro et al. (2024) revealed that integrating a GPT-based smart as-
sistant  (AI  Defenders)  into  a  software  testing  learning  environment

showed potential benefits but also resulted in mixed learning outcomes.
Many  students  developed  unrealistic  expectations  of  the  assistant,
blindly trusting its outputs and using it to shortcut their learning rather
than as a supplementary tool. This reliance impaired learning, as stu-
dents  who  frequently  used  the  assistant  produced  8.6  %  fewer  valid
tests, and 78 % of those tests were ineffective.

While  some  studies  have  suggested  that  ChatGPT  can  improve
learning outcomes (Chen et al., 2020; Odeniyi, 2024; Singh et al., 2023),
studies  on  its  impact  on  students’  academic  performance  remains
limited – at least in part due to its very recent emergence in widespread
use – and requires further exploration (Al-Qaysi et al., 2024; Chen et al.,
2023;  Lo,  2023),  especially  from  the  perspective  of  students’  percep-
tions. The lack of definitive conclusions about students’ perceptions of
ChatGPT’s  impact  on  academic  performance  highlights  a  critical  gap
that this hypothesis aimed to address.

Hypothesis  3. Students’  perception  of  ChatGPT  is  significantly
correlated to their academic performance.

4.4. The role of engagement as a mediator in online learning

Online learning engagement is recognized as a critical factor in ac-
ademic  success,  encompassing  three  main  dimensions:  cognitive
engagement,  behavioral  engagement,  and  emotional  engagement
(Bond,  2020;  Fredricks  et  al.,  2004).  These  dimensions  can  directly
impact students’ academic performance (Kahu, 2013; Kuzminykh et al.,
2021; Schaeffer & Konetes, 2010). Particularly in online learning envi-
ronments, student engagement can act as an essential mediator between
students’  perceptions  of  educational  technologies  and  their  academic
outcomes (Qu & Wu, 2024; Sharif Nia et al., 2023).

Several  studies  indirectly  investigated  the  role  of  online  learning
engagement as a mediator between students’ perceptions of technology
and academic performance. For instance, Sharif Nia et al. (2023) found
that  online  learning  engagement  served  as  a  mediator  between
e-learning acceptance, online interaction, and students’  academic per-
formance. Higher levels of engagement significantly enhanced academic
efficacy,  highlighting  the  crucial  role  of  engagement  in  linking  these
factors  to  academic  success.  In  another  study,  Qu  and  Wu  (2024)
investigated the factors influencing the adoption of ChatGPT for English
language  learning  among  Chinese  international  students  attending
British  universities.  They  identified  key  mediators—boredom,  joy,
focused immersion—between perceived ease of use and the intention to
adopt ChatGPT. They investigated the factors influencing the acceptance
of ChatGPT, particularly the mediating roles of emotional and cognitive
factors on behavioral intention, rather than examining how these factors
affect students’ academic performance. Caratiquit and Caratiquit (2023)
also  examined  178  high  school  students  using  a  mixed-method  trian-
gulation design and found that ChatGPT positively influences academic
performance  by  enhancing  learning  motivation.  Their  study  revealed
that ChatGPT’s role as an academic support tool is influenced mainly by
students’  motivation to learn.  This suggested  that ChatGPT  indirectly
improved academic outcomes by increasing students’ curiosity, interest,
and engagement in their studies through personalized feedback, helpful
resources, and interactive elements.

Although  the  literature  acknowledges  the  importance  of  online
learning  engagement  and  its  mediating  role  between  technology
perception and academic performance, there is limited direct explora-
tion  of  how  students’  perceptions  of  ChatGPT  influence  different  di-
mensions  of  engagement  (cognitive,  behavioral,  emotional)  and,
consequently, academic  performance.  Existing studies  primarily  focus
on  general  technology  acceptance  (Sharif  Nia  et  al.,  2023),  the  moti-
vational  aspects  of  ChatGPT  (Caratiquit  &  Caratiquit,  2023),  and  the
factors influencing its adoption (Qu & Wu, 2024), However, they do not
address how engagement mediates the relationship between perception
and performance in online learning contexts. This gap emphasizes the
importance  of  conducting  a  study  to  specifically  examine how  online

5. Method

From  a  methodological  standpoint,  this  study  aligns  with  recent
research  on  AI  like  ChatGpt  in  higher  education  through  its  use  of
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to analyze self-reported data from
university students (Alfaisal et al., 2024; Salifu et al., 2024; Thongsri
et  al.,  2025).  For  example,  Salifu  et  al.  (2024) employed  a  hybrid
SEM–ANN approach to investigate the use of ChatGPT among economics
students  in  Ghana,  illustrating  how  SEM  can  reveal  key  relationships
between  students’  perceptions  of  AI  and  their  academic  behavior.
Similarly,  our  use  of  SEM  enables  the  simultaneous  analysis  of  both
direct and indirect effects through online engagement, offering advan-
tages  over  the  simpler  regression  models  employed  by  Chan  and  Hu
(2023) and Yilmaz et al. (2023). The model fit indices in our study also
provide  strong  support  for  our  proposed  theoretical  framework.
Furthermore,  our  mediation  analysis  using  bootstrapping—similar  to
approaches used in other studies (Heidari et al., 2021; Mehrvarz et al.,
2024)—enhances the reliability and accuracy of the estimated indirect
effects, allowing for a more robust understanding of the mediating role
of online engagement in the relationship between students’ perceptions
of ChatGPT and their academic performance.

5.1. Participants

The total number of graduate students at Shiraz University during
the 2024–2025 academic year was approximately 7382. The dataset for
this  study  consisted  of  305  completed  responses  collected  from  the
population,  including  both  master’s  and  PhD  students  (Table  1).  Ac-
cording  to  Hoe  (2008),  a  sample  size  of  200  is  generally  considered
adequate for SEM analysis. Based on a power analysis at a 0.01 signifi-
cance level and aiming to determine path coefficients with a minimum
value of 0.11, a sample size of 251 participants was required (Joseph
et  al.,  2022).  Ultimately,  305  useable  questionnaires  were  collected,
exceeding  the  required  number.  A  stratified  sampling  method  was
employed to ensure balanced representation across educational levels.
Given  the  total  population  size,  the  final  sample  can  be  considered
reasonably representative of the broader graduate student community.
The  dataset  features  included  demographic  information  (e.g.,
gender,  academic  program,  and  field  of  study,  see  Table  1),  prior
experience  with  AI  tools,  perceived  ease  of  use  and  usefulness  of
ChatGPT, online learning engagement metrics (e.g., frequency of use,
types of activities), and self-reported academic performance.

the variables on a two-dimensional map where the horizontal axis rep-
resents the importance of each variable, and the vertical axis denotes
their performance (see Fig. 2). This approach provides crucial insights
into  which  variables  are  most  impactful  and  how  well  they  are
performing.

7. Discussion

The rapid integration of AI tools such as ChatGPT into educational
environments has opened new avenues for enhancing student learning
experiences. As these tools become more prevalent, understanding their
impact  on  student  engagement  and  academic  performance  becomes
crucial. Additionally, the mediating role of online learning engagement
in this relationship offers valuable insights into how these perceptions
translate into academic outcomes. This study’s findings confirm that all
the  research  hypotheses  were  supported,  highlighting  the  significant
role of students’ perceptions of ChatGPT in online learning engagement
and academic performance, with online learning engagement playing a
crucial mediating role in this relationship.

Based on the first hypothesis, it can be said that students’ perceptions
of  ChatGPT  influence  their  engagement  in  online  learning  environ-
ments.  In  fact,  viewing  ChatGPT  as  a  helpful  tool  increases  students’
motivation and reduces frustration, making the learning process more
enjoyable and meaningful (Fredricks et al., 2004; Skinner et al., 2008).
This finding aligns with the broader body of research that underscores
the importance of technology acceptance in educational settings (Chan
& Hu, 2023; Grani´c & Maranguni´c, 2019; Kemp et al., 2019; Teo, 2009).
This hypothesis is theoretically consistent with TAM (Davis et al., 1989),
which suggests that the more positively students perceive ChatGPT in
terms of its usefulness and ease of use, the more positive their attitude
toward using it will become, and they are likely to engage more deeply
with it in real learning activities. In other words, when students perceive
technology as valuable and effective, it enhances their willingness to use
it  and  fosters  deeper  engagement  with  learning  activities  (Albayati,
2024; Lai et al., 2023; Qu & Wu, 2024). In this regard, Qu and Wu (2024)
showed  in  their  study  that  the  perceived  usefulness  of  ChatGPT  in
improving  learning  efficiency can directly impact  learners’  emotional
states and engagement levels, and positive perceptions of its value in
facilitating personalized, efficient learning may increase learners’ sense
of control, reduce boredom, and enhance their overall enjoyment of the
learning process. In fact, students who recognize ChatGPT’s ability to
provide personalized assistance and facilitate learning are more likely to
engage  actively  in  online  education  (Cai  et  al.,  2023;  Nguyen  et  al.,
2024).  Although  the  results  of  some  studies  showed  that  the  ease
ChatGPT provides in completing educational tasks may reduce students’
motivation for deep engagement with the learning material (Chan & Hu,
2023; Ding et al., 2023; Sallam, 2023), the findings of numerous other
studies  align  with  and  support  the  first  hypothesis  of  this  research
(Acosta Enriquez et al., 2024; Chan & Hu, 2023; Lai et al., 2023; Liang
et al., 2024; Malik et al., 2023).

Additionally, the second hypothesis of this study found that online
learning engagement positively correlates with academic performance,
a finding consistent with previous studies (Fredricks et al., 2004; Kahu,
2013; Kuo et al., 2021; Kuzminykh et al., 2021; Lee & Choi, 2011; Pardo
et al., 2016; Schaeffer & Konetes, 2010; Vayre & Vonthron, 2019; Wang
et  al., 2022; You, 2022). The confirmation of this hypothesis is  theo-
retically  aligned  with  cognitive-behavioral  therapy  CBT  (Bandura,
1986),  which  suggests  that  the  more  personal,  behavioral,  and

environmental factors encourage and reinforce the use of ChatGPT in
the learning process, the more likely students are to engage with it and
achieve  better  academic  performance.  Also,  this  hypothesis  suggests
that  active  participation  in  educational  activities,  such  as  regular
attendance in online classes, participation in discussions, and comple-
tion of assignments, leads to improved academic outcomes. This finding
emphasizes  the need for strategies to increase student  engagement in
online learning processes.

research  suggesting  that  students’  positive  perceptions  of  educational

TAM (Davis, 1989). That is, the more positive students’ attitudes toward
using ChatGPT are—perceiving it as more useful and easier to use—the
more likely they are to utilize it in the educational environment, which
in  turn  may  lead  to  improved  academic  performance.  Also,  this  hy-

of ChatGPT to support and enhance students’ academic achievements,
reinforcing  the  importance  of  integrating  such  technologies  into
educational  practices.  However,  despite  all  the  evidence  highlighting
students’  awareness  of  ChatGPT’s  benefits  and  its  positive  impact  on
academic performance, some studies have raised concerns about its ef-

learning through this tool (Mezzaro et al., 2024; Nguyen, Ngo, et al.,
2023; S´anchez-Ruiz et al., 2023; Sila et al., 2023; Stampfl et al., 2024).
Finally,  the  mediation  analysis  confirmed  that  online  learning
engagement mediates the relationship between students’ perceptions of
ChatGPT  and  their  academic  performance,  supporting  the  fourth  hy-
pothesis. The confirmation of this hypothesis aligns with the findings of
studies  such  as  Caratiquit  and  Caratiquit  (2023),  Qu  and  Wu  (2024),
Sharif Nia et al. (2023), and Xu et al. (2024). For example, Qu and Wu
(2024) examined the perceptions and motivation of English as a Second
Language  (ESL)  learners  in  using ChatGPT  for  English  learning.  They
concluded  that  boredom,  joy,  focused  immersion,  and  control  are
important and influential factors in the acceptance and use of ChatGPT
for English learning. Lai et al. (2023) concluded that intrinsic motivation
is the strongest motivator of students’  intention to use ChatGPT. This
means that students’ positive subjective experiences with ChatGPT and
their enjoyment of using it for learning play a crucial role. If students
find using ChatGPT to be enjoyable, their intrinsic motivation to use it
increases.  Additionally,  perceived  usefulness  also  affects  students’
intention to use ChatGPT. Students are more likely to use ChatGPT if
they view it as a fast, effective, and useful tool for improving their ac-
ademic performance. Consequently, their engagement can impact their
academic performance.

This  suggests  that  while  positive  perceptions  of  ChatGPT  directly
influence academic  performance, the  impact is  even more substantial
when  mediated  by  increased  engagement  in  learning  activities.  This
finding is critical as it highlights the multifaceted role of engagement in
translating the perceived benefits of technology into tangible academic
outcomes.

These findings have critical pedagogical implications, grounded in

tools, such as ChatGPT, are becoming increasingly integrated. According
to  the  TAM  (Davis,  1989),  when  students  perceive  educational  tech-

willingly,  which  in  turn  enhances  their  motivation  and  engagement

study  supports  this,  showing  that  fostering  positive  perceptions  of
ChatGPT can promote meaningful participation in online learning tasks.
Therefore, educators should actively highlight the value and usability of
such  tools  by  embedding  them  in  purposeful  and  supportive  instruc-
tional contexts.

From the perspective of CBT (Bandura, 1986), student behavior in
learning environments is influenced by the interaction of personal be-
liefs,  behavioral  engagement,  and  external  reinforcement.  When
ChatGPT is used not only to deliver information but also to encourage
student autonomy, self-efficacy, and positive emotional experiences, it
can significantly improve engagement and performance (Ng et al., 2024;
Qu & Wu, 2024). Instructors are encouraged to design online learning
environments that support feedback, emotional regulation, and behav-
ioral reinforcement through responsible and reflective use of AI tools.
These  strategies  are  particularly  crucial  in  digital  education  settings,
where direct teacher-student interaction is limited and students benefit
from tools that enhance both motivation and cognitive involvement.

Additionally,  young  students,  particularly  those  who  have  tried
ChatGPT, seem to have readily adopted its use. Younger individuals tend
to  adopt  new  technologies  more  quickly  than  older  generations  and
demonstrate a greater understanding of ChatGPT. Furthermore, partic-
ipants  with  higher  levels  of  education  exhibited  greater  awareness  of
ChatGPT compared to those with lower educational levels (Bodani et al.,
2023).  So,  educators  and  educational  policymakers  should  focus  on
improving  students’  perception  and  understanding  of  ChatGPT  to
enhance their academic engagement and performance. While ChatGPT
offers  valuable  support  in  terms  of  personalized  feedback,  immediate
answers, and facilitation of learning tasks, there is a need for caution in
its overuse. To maximize the benefits of ChatGPT while mitigating its
potential downsides, educators should strike a balance between utilizing
AI tools and fostering student-driven learning processes. This includes
designing  activities

7.3. Limitations and future research direction

This study, like many others, faces certain limitations. For instance,
the participants were limited to a single university in Iran, which might
not be applicable to different cultures and situations. Given that various
universities and cultures hold different perceptions of ChatGPT and its
application,  subsequent  research  should  involve  a  broader  range  of
participants  from  varied  cultural  and  educational  backgrounds.  This
approach would provide a more comprehensive understanding of how
students  perceive  ChatGPT  and  its  relationship  with  other  factors.
Furthermore, the research heavily depended on self-reported informa-
tion, which could be influenced by biases related to students’  percep-
tions  and  behaviors.  Future  studies  should  employ  experimental
methods that incorporate ChatGPT into real-time educational settings
and  evaluate  its  direct  influence  on  student  outcomes,  while  also
incorporating behavioral measures—such as time-on-task—to comple-
ment self-reported data. Additionally, this study didn’t consider other
aspects, such as the quality of internet access, the use of Virtual Private
Networks (VPNs) to access ChatGPT, and the availability of ChatGPT.
These  elements  can  significantly  impact  students’  perceptions,  in-
teractions, and academic outcomes in online learning settings. Future
studies  should  consider  these  aspects  to  gain  a  more  comprehensive
understanding of students’  views on ChatGPT, their engagement, and
academic success. Moreover, investigating the ethical considerations of
employing artificial intelligence in education is a critical area for further
exploration.  It  is  vital  to  ensure  that  AI-based  educational  tools  are
utilized responsibly and fairly.
