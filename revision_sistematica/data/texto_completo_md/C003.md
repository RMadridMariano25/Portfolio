ABSTRACT
The rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence (GAI) and the extensive use of social media have transformed how
students engage with educational materials and interact with their peers. Collaborative learning (CL) platforms, empowered by
artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms, have gained popularity due to their potential to enhance learning outcomes and provide
personalised educational experiences. This research examines the effects of generative AI (ChatGPT- 4) and social media use on
young students' academic performance and psychological well- being, focusing on CL. The study conceptual framework was ex-
amined based on a sample of 441 Chinese university students. The statistical technique PLS- SEM is put into practice to measure
the structural framework of academic performance and psychological well- being. The findings of this study show that genera-
tive AI (ChatGPT- 4) and social media positively influence young students' academic performance and psychological well- being.
Additionally, the results of this research study show that CL positively mediates between social media, academic performance
and psychological well- being. Conversely, it negatively mediates the association between generative AI (ChatGPT- 4), academic
performance (AP), and psychological well- being. The findings can facilitate a better understanding of the implications of tech-
nologies in the educational context and subsequently aid in formulating evidence- based strategies to optimise their impact on
students's academic success and well- being.

in authentic contexts (Shahzad, Xu, and Javed 2024).

the  probable  benefits  of  GAI  technologies  for  university  stu-
dents'  AP  and  mental  health.  High  levels  of  stress,  pressure,
and  anxiety  often  accompany  university  life.  The  constant
exposure  to  social  media  platforms,  with  their  carefully  cu-
rated images and idealised lifestyles, can positively contribute
to  low  self- esteem,  social  comparison,  and  feelings  of  inad-
equacy  (Giunchiglia  et  al.  2018).  Moreover,  earlier  research
investigated the addictive nature of social media use and the
potential  for  cyberbullying  and  online  harassment  to  pose
additional  mental  health  challenges  (Yosep,  Hikmat,  and
Mardhiyah  2023).  Understanding  the  mechanisms  through
which  these  technologies  influence  researchers  (Brougham
and  Haar  2018;  Delanerolle  et  al.  2021)  identified  the  poten-
tial  benefits  and  risks  and  developed  strategies  to  maximise
positive  outcomes  while  mitigating  the  negative  effects.  CL
in  China's  higher  education  promotes  a  deeper  understand-
ing of the learning material. Students engaging in discussion
and problem- solving with peers are likelier to retain informa-
tion  and  concepts  (Alamri,  Almaiah,  and  Al- Rahmi  2020).
The social interaction element of CL fosters a more engaging
and  stimulating  learning  environment,  which  can  enhance
motivation  and  AP  (Guilmette  et  al.  2019).  Therefore,  this
study  explores  the  CL  process  to  understand  generative  AI
(ChatGPT- 4)  and  its  impact  on  individual  performance  and
mental health.

The  GAI  technologies  such  as  ChatGPT- 4  and  social  media
usage present a novel area of investigation, particularly concern-
ing their combined impact on AP and psychological well- being
in China's higher education context. Despite the proliferation of
studies (Lee et al. 2021; Li and Che 2022; Tlili et al. 2023) ex-
ploring the individual effects of AI and social media, there re-
mains a significant gap in understanding their synergistic and
potentially  compounding  influences  on  students'  educational
performances. Limited studies were conducted on these topics
to analyse the ChatGPT- 4 influence towards students' outcomes
and  psychological  well- being.  Additionally,  the  mediating  role
of CL is not clearly defined in the context of generative AI tech-
nologies and social media users. Therefore, this study also seeks
to collect information from young generations on how genera-
tive AI (ChatGPT- 4) and social media platforms affect their AP
and  psychological  well- being  through  CL.  Furthermore,  the
study  explains  two  research  questions:  First,  do  generative  AI
(ChatGPT- 4) and social media link to Chinese students' AP and
psychological well- being? Second, does CL mediate the relation-
ship  among  generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4),  social  media,  AP,  and
psychological well- being?

Four  major  sections  make  up  the  rest  of  the  paper:  The  brief

2.1   |   Social Learning Theory

Albert Bandura created the social learning theory (SLT), which
holds that people pick up knowledge from one another through
modelling,  imitation,  and  observation.  Based  on  this  theory,
learning can take place just through direct instruction or obser-
vation in a social setting, even in the absence of motor reproduc-
tion or direct reinforcement (Zhang et al. 2024). Bandura's SLT
theory  is  used  in  this  study  to  create  a  theoretical  foundation
that connects the use of social media and related technologies,
such  as  generative  AI,  to  AP.  Generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  and
social media provide students vast information and educational
resources.  By  observing  the  use  of  these  tools  by  peers  and
educators,  students  learn  to  leverage  them  for  academic  pur-
poses,  enhancing  their  learning  experiences  and  AP  (Alturki,
Cohausz,  and  Stuckenschmidt  2022).  Social  media  platforms
simplify CL by enabling students to observe and participate in
study groups, discussion forums, and virtual classrooms (Zhang
et  al.  2024).  This  interactive  environment  encourages  sharing
knowledge and problem- solving strategies, which students can
model and incorporate into their study practices. When students
experience success using generative AI and social media, such
as achieving higher grades or receiving positive feedback, they
are  more  likely  to  continue  using  these  tools.  This  reinforce-
ment  strengthens  their  academic  behaviours  and  can  lead  to
sustained  improvement  in  performance  (Ali,  Gongbing,  and
Mehreen 2018).

2.2   |   Generative AI (ChatGPT- 4) and AP

Generative  AI  has  gained  educational  momentum  and
improved  individual  learning  (Ivanov  et  al.  2024).  (Gao
et  al.  2024)  explained  that  generative  AI  can  improve  AP  by
providing  tailored  learning  experiences,  task  automation,
and focused assistance. However, challenges associated with
biases,  resource  limitations  and  ethical  considerations  must
be  addressed  for  ChatGPT- 4  effective  and  equitable  integra-
tion  in  the  education  sector  (Shahzad,  Xu,  and  Javed  2024).
Through  CL  efforts,  researchers,  educators,  legislators  and
tech developers can optimise AI's benefits while ensuring its
ethical application (Yu 2023). A past study focused on address-
ing the identified challenges, exploring best practices for inte-
grating generative AI, and evaluating their long- term impact
on  educational  outcomes  (Obrenovic  et  al.  2024).  Generative
AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  offers  immersive  and  experiential  learning
environments for students to explore new things. These tech-
nologies can recreate real- world scenarios, allowing students
to  apply  theoretical  understanding  and  gain  expertise  and
experience  in  problem- solving  (Tsai,  Ong,  and  Chen  2023).
Generative AI (ChatGPT- 4) in the educational sector requires
effective  implementation  strategies,  instructor  training,  and
consideration of ethical and privacy concerns. Incorporating
GAI,  such  as  ChatGPT- 4,  into  education  enhances  human
instruction  and
improves  academic  success  (Obrenovic
et al. 2024).

Personalised  learning  opportunities  that  are  catered  to  each
student's aptitudes, interests, and learning styles can increase
motivation, engagement, and topic comprehension. Using GAI
adaptive learning systems, they spot gaps, give remedies, and
streamline administrative work, enabling instructors to focus
on teaching (Crompton and Burke 2023). Generative AI cus-
tomises resources to improve learning results by automating
educational  content  development.  ChatGPT- 4  has  the  ability
to completely transform AP in the classroom by offering indi-
vidualised and flexible learning opportunities (Tsai, Ong, and
Chen 2023). By gaining a long- term and deep consideration of
the role of GAI in education, we can connect its potential to
empower students, improve learning outcomes and shape the
future of education. Therefore, generative AI offers promising
opportunities to enhance AP among university students (Gao
et  al.  2024).  Instant  feedback,  intelligent  guidance,  comput-
erised  grading,  data  analytics,  and  realistic  simulations  are

some  main  ways  generative  AI  improves  student  AP  in  the
classroom  (Kumar,  Islam,  and  Ahmad  2024).  Therefore,  we
hypothesized,

H1.  Generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  positively  affects  university
students' academic performance.

2.3   |   Generative AI (ChatGPT- 4) and Psychological
Well- Being

Generative AI in education has transformed learning experi-
ences  and  potentially  impacted  students'  psychological  well-
being (Gupta and Singhal 2023). GAI can influence students'
psychological  well- being,  including  individualised  therapies,
self- reflection,  psychological  assistance,  and  stress  reduc-
tion.  Generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4),  or  virtual  assistants,  can
offer students emotional support and a non- judgmental outlet
for  expressing  their  thoughts  and  concerns  (Javaid,  Haleem,
and  Pratap  2023).  (Chan  and  Zhou  2023)  expressed  that  in-
teracting with generative AI- based conversational agents can
positively  affect  students'  emotional  well- being  by  offering  a
safe space to discuss their feelings, reducing stress, and pro-
moting self- awareness. A past study (Gupta and Singhal 2023)
highlighted  AI  algorithms  that  can  reliably  analyse  a  vari-
ety  of  data  sources  to  uncover  mental  health  disorders  early
on,  hence  improving  diagnosis  and  treatment.  Generative
AI- powered  virtual  assistants  provide  personalised  men-
tal  health  care  through  gentle  listening  and  evidence- based
therapies,  making  mental  health  management  more  acces-
sible  (Shahzad,  Xu,  and  Asif  2024).  GAI  can  also  help  with
treatment outcomes by adjusting approaches to each patient's
needs. By analysing treatment response data and recommend-
ing  tailored  treatment  plans,  machine  learning  algorithms
can improve therapeutic modalities and raise the possibility of
successful outcomes (Johnston et al. 2024).

Generative  AI- powered  applications  may  provide  immersive
worlds encouraging rest, stress relief, and general mental health
(Horesh et al. 2022). Similarly, these technologies enhance ac-
cessibility by providing material summaries customised to each
student's requirements and captioning and transcribing for stu-
dents who struggle with mental health issues (Zhang et al. 2024).
ChatGPT- 4  improves  the  mental  well- being  of  students  by  en-
couraging  diversity,  lowering  obstacles  to  learning,  offering
emotional support, reducing stress, encouraging self- reflection,
and  improving  accessibility  in  learning  environments  (Urban
et al. 2024). However, balancing GAI technologies and human
interaction is crucial, ensuring students receive appropriate sup-
port from educators and mental health professionals (Johnston
et al. 2024). A past study (Bahroun et al. 2023) highlighted the
long- term  effects,  ethical  considerations,  and  optimal  imple-
mentation strategies to maximise the impact of GAI on students'
psychological well- being. By responsibly leveraging ChatGPT- 4,
educational  institutions  can  create  supportive  environments
that promote academic success and students' overall psycholog-
ical well- being (Johnston et al. 2024). As a result, we have the
following hypothesis:

H2.  Generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  positively  affects  university
students' psychological well- being.

Social  media  use  has  the  potential  to  be  a  source  of  informa-

ing,  learning  opportunities,  networking,  peer  collaboration,

Similarly, a few more studies have examined how social media
use affects AP in the long run (Hussain, Khan, and Malik 2024;
Oueder and Abousaber 2019). It was assessed how changing so-
cial  media  platforms  and  trends  affected  student  achievement
(Alshuaibi  et  al.  2018).  However,  responsible  and  purposeful
use  of  social  media  and  effective  time  management  strategies
are crucial in maximising its positive effects on AP. Individual
personal characteristics and motivations also play a role in using
social media for educational purposes (Malik et al. 2021). Some
students  may  have  better  self- regulation  skills  and  the  ability
to balance social media use with their academic commitments,
which leads to their success. AP can be impacted by the reasons
people use social media, such as enjoyment, knowledge, or so-
cial connection (Oueder and Abousaber 2019).

Online  platforms  like  Facebook  or  WhatsApp  enable  students
to ask questions, share notes, and discuss course- related topics
(Kross et al. 2021). CL and peer support can improve compre-
hension,  analytical  reasoning,  and  problem- solving  abilities,
positively  influencing  AP  (Zhang  et  al.  2024).  Our  study  liter-
ature aims to give a general overview of the possible effects of
university students' social media usage on their AP. The positive
impact of social media usage on performance in academic activ-
ities depends on factors such as the quality of content consumed,
time  management,  and  individual  self- regulation  (Alshuaibi
et al. 2018). Excessive social media use, engaging with irrelevant
or  non- academic  content,  or  ignoring  online  distractions  can
harm  AP  (Maqableh  et  al.  2015).  Different  activities  on  social
media, such as browsing news feeds and engaging in online dis-
cussions, can have varying effects on AP (Donthu et al. 2021).
Social media engages students in academic responsibilities, such
as problem- solving techniques on different online platforms and
connecting with classmates for academic purposes (Oueder and
Abousaber  2019).  Based  on  the  above  discussion,  we  have  the
following hypothesis:

H3.  Social  media  significantly  influences  the  university  stu-
dents' academic performance.

2.5   |   Social Media and Psychological Well- Being

Positive  emotions  and  people's  psychological  functioning  are
components  of  psychological  well- being  (Park,  Song,  and
Min  2014).  Social  media  offers  advantages,  including  social
encouragement  and  campaign  participation,  while  facilitat-
ing  communication,  knowledge  exchange,  and  self- expression
(Bano et al. 2019). Students utilising social media for education
characterised mental well- being as having supportive families,
feeling  upbeat,  and  organising  ideas  collaboratively  (Whelan,
Golden,  and  Tarafdar  2022).  Academic  achievement  provides

mental  fulfilment,  but  social  media  assists  university  students
in connecting, forming networks, and maintaining connections
for mental relaxation (Twenge and Martin 2020). Students can
access  the  prosperity  of  information  and  tools  through  social
media  platforms  to  help  them  in  their  academic  endeavours.
Social media and online communities can help kids feel a sense
of belonging. These groups might be based on shared pastimes,
hobbies, or academic interests and offer a forum for cooperation,
learning, and support (Bano et al. 2019).

However, responsible and mindful use of social media and ad-
dressing  potential  risks  and  challenges  is  crucial  in  maximis-
ing  its  positive  effects  on  mental  well- being  among  university
students  (Fan  et  al.  2019).  Social  media  platforms,  including
knowledge sharing, teamwork, and educational resource access,
can provide advantages (Zhang et al. 2024). Numerous studies
(Bruggeman  et  al.  2019;  Hardy  and  Castonguay  2018)  on  the
effects of social media on mental health have suggested a con-
nection between negative consequences and overuse. Similarly,
another  study  (Coyne  et  al.  2020)  focused  on  factors  such  as
frequency and use quality leading to psychological well- being.
Individuals' use of different social media platforms significantly
affects  their  psychological  well- being  and  enjoyment.  Social
media use also benefits mental health by providing information,
self- care, and well- being to individuals. University students can
access  educational  content,  tips,  and  strategies  for  managing
stress, improving mental health, and seeking professional help
through social media (Park, Song, and Min 2014). The availabil-
ity  of  such  resources  can  empower  students  to  take  proactive
steps  in  maintaining  their  psychological  well- being  (Whelan,
Golden, and Tarafdar 2022). Based on the discussion, we have
the following hypothesis:

H4.  Social  media  significantly  influences  the  university  stu-
dents' psychological well- being.

2.6   |   Mediating Role of CL

CL  refers  to  integrating  digital  technologies,  including  social
media  platforms  and  GAI  technologies  (ChatGPT- 4),  into  the
educational  sector  to  create  innovative  and  effective  learn-
ing  environments  (Allal- Chérif,  Yela  Aránega,  and  Castaño
Sánchez  2021).  Peers  or  larger  groups  can  engage  in  CL.  Peer
learning, sometimes referred to as peer instruction, is a type of
cooperative learning in which students solve problems in pairs
or small groups and share ideas (Wang 2009). Similar to the idea
that two or three heads are better than one, educational experts
have  found  that  through  peer  instruction,  students  teach  one
another  by  resolving  misunderstandings  and  dispelling  mis-
conceptions (Anderson, Boaler, and Dieckmann 2018). Students
gain  from  CL  platforms  by  having  better  access  to  materials,
peer  cooperation,  and  independent  study,  improving  their  AP
and  mental  health  (Guilmette  et  al.  2019).  Similarly,  universi-
ties can provide engaging and inclusive learning environments
that maximise students potential and well- being by utilising the
promise of CL (Zang, Kim, and Dong 2022).

Social media and GAI technologies provide opportunities for
CL  environments,  active  engagement,  and  personalised  sup-
port  (Saeidnia  2022).  Students'  performance  and  well- being

ing,  mental  well- being,  and  academic  success  (Saeidnia  2022).
Therefore, CL between students leads to their academic success.
As discussed above, we have the following hypothesis:

H5a.  Collaborative  learnings  mediate  between  generative  AI
(ChatGPT- 4) and students' academic performance.

H5b.  Collaborative  learnings  mediates  between  generative  AI
(ChatGPT- 4) and psychological well- being.

H5c.  Collaborative  learnings  mediates  positively  between  so-
cial media and students' academic performance.

H5d.  Collaborative  learnings  mediates  positively  between  so-
cial media and psychological well- being.

3   |   Methodology

3.1   |   Measures

The  survey  method  is  adopted  to  examine  the  latent  variables
by using scales taken from earlier research. The items of scales
have  been  changed  to  more  closely  match  the  selected  sam-
ple  (Hair  et  al.  2017).  The  Likert  scale's  goal  is  to  assess  how
strongly  participants  agree  or  disagree  with  a  particular  point
of view. A Likert scale of five points from 1 (strongly disagree)
to 5 (strongly agree) is used to show the larger range of respon-
dents'  views  and  attitudes  while  providing  more  possibilities
(Bernardez. 2007). Social media (SM) six items were taken from
the study (Lau 2017) in the students. Generative AI (GAI) was
assessed by eight items adopted from (Li 2023; Shoufan 2023).
CL,  five  items  were  taken  from  (Zang,  Kim,  and  Dong  2022).
The student's AP is measured by a scale established by Whelan,
Islam, and Brooks (2020) to evaluate students' AP. The six items
of  psychological  well- being  (PWB)  were  adopted  from  Alam
et al. (2021). Further details are provided in Table 2.

formation and resources beyond traditional classroom boundar-
ies. Social media can provide supportive communities and raise
mental health awareness through GAI- driven resources (Ivanov
et  al.  2024).  Nowadays,  many  young  generations  use  technol-

4.1   |   Common Method Bias

To test for common method bias (CBM), Harman's one- factor test
(1976) was employed together with a time lag survey (Podsakoff
et al. 2003). Furthermore, SPSS software, as studies suggest, is
the most simple and meaningful way to assess common method
bias despite its flaws (and no diagnostic method is perfect any-
way) and may be more effective diagnostically than previously
(Babin,  Grif,  and  Jr  2015;  Shahzad,  Xu,  and  Javed  2024).  The
analysis showed that the single component contributed only 21%
of  the  variation,  significantly  less  than  the  recommended  50%
threshold  for  common  technique  bias  (Podsakoff  et  al.  2003).
This conclusion implies that common method bias is not a con-
cern  for  this  investigation.  Robustness  in  evaluating  common
method bias was also ensured by adhering to the Bagozzi and
Edwards (1998) technique, which indicates that any correlation
between the study variables, if bigger than 0.9, would show the
presence  of  common  method  bias.  The  analysis  demonstrates
that there is no evidence of common technique bias; hence, the
greatest correlation between any two variables is 0.72 (Table 2).

4.2   |   Measurement Assessment Model

The measuring model verifies the validity of the constructs as
well as their dependability and factor loadings (Hair et al. 2017).
PLS- SEM  aids  in  measuring  Cronbach's  alpha,  R2  value,  dis-
criminant validity, composite reliability (CR), average variance
extracted  (AVE)  and  hypothesis  testing  in  the  measurement
model. The validity and reliability analysis was conducted using
PLS- SEM, demonstrating that each item was valid and reliable
for evaluating the proposed link between the variables (Bagozzi
and Edwards 1998). Every association between the variables is
assessed and tested by the bootstrapping approach. The internal
consistency of each variable is assessed using the AVE, CR and
Cronbach's alpha. Internal consistency demonstrates each vari-
able's dependability. It implies the relationships between every
building piece.

The  threshold  for  reliability  is  0.7  (Anser,  Shahzad,  and  Xu
2024). A variable is considered internally trustworthy and con-
sistent if its value is > 0.7. For every construct, the CR score is
higher than 0.7. As demonstrated in Table 2, each construct has
a  CR  score  larger  than  0.7.  If  the  AVE  value  for  each  variable
exceeds 0.5, the PLS model is shown to exhibit convergent va-
lidity (Shahzad, Liu, and Zahid 2024). All values represent high
internal  consistency  across  all  constructs.  Convergent  validity
is  present  in  the  object  outer  loading  of  all  variables,  and  the

the change in variance that a given factor may explain. The SEM
technique strongly suggests values with outer loadings of 0.7 or

5   |   Discussion and Recommendations

Generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  has  become  integral  to  our  daily
lives,  transforming  various  sectors,  including  education  and
communication.  Similarly,  social  media  has  gained  tremen-
dous  popularity  worldwide,  connecting  people  across  borders
and enabling the exchange of ideas and information (Alqahtani
et  al.  2023).  Generative  AI  technologies  like  ChatGPT- 4  have
completely  transformed  information  access,  processing,  and
utilisation. In the same way, social networking platforms have
revolutionised how people interact, exchange information, and
connect. Although these developments have many advantages,
questions have been raised about how they may affect students'
AP and mental health (Gupta and Singhal 2023). It is important
to strike a balance and promote the responsible and mindful use
of generative AI and social media to mitigate the negative im-
pacts on young individuals' AP and well- being. Thus, using CL,
this  study  investigated  the  connections  between  social  media,
generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4),  AP,  and  psychological  well- being.

nings. The inquiry yielded five significant findings.

First,  the  findings  of  this  study  show  that  generative  AI
(ChatGPT- 4)  positively  impacts  AP.  ChatGPT- 4  has  recently
gained  significant  attention,  revolutionising  various  industries
and fields, including education. A prior study (Gao et al. 2024)
discussed the aims of exploring the findings that highlight the
positive impact of GAI on AP. Understanding the potential ad-
vantages of integrating generative AI into educational systems
by examining how it affects learning experiences, personaliza-
tion and educational results. Social media and GAI technologies
provide individualised learning routes for each student's goals,
skills and preferences. For instance, intelligent tutoring systems
may adjust the curriculum and pace to each student's develop-
ment, offering real- time feedback and more resources as needed
(Gupta  and  Singhal  2023).  Generative  AI  can  spot  trends,  pat-
terns and student behaviour using machine learning algorithms

Third, the results of this research show that social media pos-

5.1   |   Theoretical and Practical Implications

This  study  has  some  theoretical  and  practical  implications.
CL  platforms  that  use  generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  offer  tai-
lored  and  interactive  learning  experiences  that  let  students
access knowledge, ask questions and get answers in real- time.
This could enhance students' AP and help them retain more
information. Young Chinese students' cognitive development
may  benefit  from  generative  AI  and  intelligent  learning  sys-
tems.  These  systems  frequently  include  adaptive  learning
algorithms,  which  examine  user  learning  styles  and  modify
the information as necessary. GAI- based technologies can en-
courage critical thinking, problem- solving skills, and creative
abilities, which are crucial for cognitive growth, by tailoring
to students' individual requirements and learning preferences
(Zhang et al. 2024). Social media platforms can serve as a me-
dium  for  knowledge  sharing,  collaboration,  and  networking
among  students.  Using  GAI  platforms  can  encourage  peer-
to- peer connections, suggest pertinent educational resources,
and  promote  a  sense  of  community.  Due  to  its  interconnec-
tion,  learning  can  be  improved  in  terms  of  social  skills  and
a  deeper  comprehension  of  various  viewpoints.  Generative
AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  can  give  students  a  confidential  and  non-
judgmental  outlet  to  express  their  thoughts,  concerns,  and
emotions.  ChatGPT- 4,  equipped  with  natural  language  pro-
cessing capabilities, can offer empathetic responses and pro-
vide resources for mental health support. This integration can
promote  psychological  well- being  by  reducing  stigma,  pro-
viding  accessible  mental  health  resources,  and  encouraging
open  conversations  about  mental  health  among  the  younger
generation.

Furthermore,  this  study  has  some  practical  implications.
Individual  learning  styles  and  preferences  are  the  subject  of
personalised learning, which uses generative AI algorithms to
modify instructional materials to meet the needs of individual
students.  Students  can  explore  themes  of  interest  at  their  own
speed, concentrate on areas of difficulty, and learn at their own
pace. As a result, practical implications include improved learn-
ing  outcomes,  increased  motivation,  and  more  efficient  use  of
learning  time  and  following  the  ethics  (Nguyen  et  al.  2023).
Real- time assessment and feedback are possible applications of
GAI technologies. These tools may evaluate student input, spot
misconceptions,  and  provide  quick  feedback  to  help  students
fix their errors. Students may monitor their progress, pinpoint
areas for growth, and take prompt action to boost their academic
achievement  due  to  this  real- time  feedback  loop.  Similarly,  it
includes  enhanced  engagement,  knowledge  acquisition,  and  a
deeper  understanding  of  subjects.  Generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4),
equipped  with  natural  language  processing  capabilities,  can
offer  practical  implications  for  24/7  mental  health  assistance.
Students can communicate with these GAI- based technologies

to seek guidance, express their emotions, and receive appropri-
ate  resources.  This  technology  support  enhances  accessibility,
reduces  wait  times,  and  provides  continuous  support  to  pro-
mote  the  mental  well- being  of  the  young  generation  in  China.
Educating  students  about  responsible  social  media,  online  eti-
quette, and critical thinking can help mitigate potential negative
effects  on  educational  performance  and  psychological  well-
being. Universities and parents can collaborate to foster healthy
online habits and create a supportive digital environment.

5.2   |   Conclusion and Policy Recommendations

This  research  study  examined  the  influence  of  generative  AI
(ChatGPT- 4)  and  social  media  towards  AP  and  psychological
well- being  through  CL  in  China's  young  generation.  Five  im-
portant  conclusions  were  found  in  the  research.  First,  genera-
tive  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  positively  impacts  Chinese  students'  AP.
Second,  generative  AI  (ChatGPT- 4)  positively  affects  students'
psychological  well- being.  Third,  social  media  (SM)  positively
influences students' AP. Fourth, social media (SM) positively in-
fluences students' psychological well- being. Fifth, CL positively
mediates  the  association  between  social  media  (SM),  AP,  and
psychological well- being. Conversely, it negatively mediates the
association between generative AI (ChatGPT- 4), academic per-
formance (AP), and psychological well- being.

This study's findings provide clear guidelines for the ethical and
responsible  use  of  generative  AI  (such  as  ChatGPT- 4)  and  so-
cial media platforms in academic settings. Policymakers provide
training  programs  for  students  and  faculty  to  raise  awareness
of potential benefits and risks. Social media and GAI platforms
should support interactive discussions, group projects, and peer
feedback.  Increase  the  availability  of  mental  health  resources,
including counselling services and GAI- based chatbots, to pro-
vide timely support for students experiencing stress or anxiety
related  to  academic  pressures.  Ensure  that  all  students  have
equal access to technology and internet connectivity to engage
in CL with AI and social media. Higher education institutions
in China can take advantage of social media and generative AI
to  boost  CL,  raise  academic  achievement,  and  assist  students'
psychological well- being by putting these policy proposals into
practice.

5.3   |   Limitations and Future Directions

There  are  some  restrictions  on  this  study  as  well  as  potential
directions. First, these research findings may be specific to the
young generation in China and may not apply to other cultural
contexts or age groups. For future research, factors such as cul-
tural  differences  and  educational  systems  could  influence  the
relationships between generative AI, social media (SM), AP, and
psychological  well- being  differently.  Second,  this  study's  find-
ings may be limited by the characteristics of the sample popu-
lation. This study relies on a specific group of participants, such
as university students from Beijing, China; the results may not
represent all the students in China. Future studies will focus on
diverse  backgrounds,  socioeconomic  statuses,  or  educational
environments within other countries' contexts. Third, the data
