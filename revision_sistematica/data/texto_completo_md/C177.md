Keywords: This study examines the use of LLMs in text linguistics education, focusing on novices in generative AI (genAI)
LLM and their ability to design prompts and evaluate AI-generated outputs. Employing an action research design in
Text linguistics higher education, it investigates prompting strategies, student perceptions, and learning outcomes in text
Language education analysis tasks. Students’ reports containing prompts and LLM outputs show that novices refine prompts through
Prompting
trial and error and occasionally use in-context examples or simplify complex instructions. End-of-term reflections
Metacognition
indicate limited prompting competence but growing confidence in applying subject-specific knowledge, although
students often attributed unsatisfactory results to the LLM’s limitations rather than to their own prompt
formulation. Challenges such as anthropomorphizing the models and overgeneralizing limited outcomes were
also observed. The findings suggest that, in this specific classroom context, students reported engaging in met-
acognitive reflection while using LLMs, particularly when disciplinary knowledge was already well consolidated.
However, limited knowledge of prompt design remains a major obstacle.
