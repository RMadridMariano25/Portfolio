Introduction
TheintegrationofArtificialIntelligence(AI)technologies,specificallychatbots,have
beenintegratedintomanyaspectsofourcontemporarylivessuchasbusiness,educa-
tion, customer service, and digital communication which attracted more research on
these technologies. Research on chatbots has demonstrated their effectiveness across
various contexts such as customer service (Xu et al., 2017) language translation
(Park & Aiken, 2019), content generation (Fang et al., 2018), and higher education
(Kutty etal.,2024).Additionally,studieshaveexploredtheirimpactonhumancom-
munication(Hilletal.,2015;Lew&Walther,2022)andtheethicalandsocialimpli-
cations of their use(Park et al., 2021).
In educational settings, chatbots have been used to answer student questions
(Clarizia et al., 2018; Ranoliya et al., 2017; Sinha et al., 2020), teach programming
concepts (Okonkwo & Ade-Ibijola, 2020; Zhao et al., 2020), assess student perfor-
mance (Benotti et al., 2018; Durall & Kapros, 2020), andprovide administrative ser-
vices (Röhrig & Heß, 2019). These applications suggest that chatbots can enhance
learningexperiencesandstudentsatisfaction(Winkler&Söllner,2018)byproviding
quickandinteractiveresponses(Adamopoulou&Moussiades,2020;Molnár&Szüts,
2018). However, concerns persist regarding over-reliance on chatbots, which could
impede critical thinking and promote academic dishonesty (Ruane et al., 2019).
Recently,anAI-poweredchatbottechnologythatstoodoutamongothers,hasbeen
ChatGPT (Conversational Generative Pre-training Transformer). Developed by
OpenAIandreleasedinNovember2022,ChatGPTleveragesadvanceddeeplearning
techniquestogeneratehuman-liketextwithremarkableaccuracyandcoherence.This
model is designed to handle complex queries, engage in sophisticated conversations,
and provide precise, contextually relevant information across a wide range of topics
(Bansal et al., 2024). With its enhanced capabilities in analysis and synthesis,
GPT-4 sets a new benchmark for AI-driven text generation, making it an invaluable
tool for applications in research, education, and industry. For educational contexts,
withitsfeaturesofreplicatingnaturalcommunicationbetweenhumansandcomputers,
generating assessments for instructional purposes, personalizing learning experiences
and providing feedback, ChatGPT has proved to help maintain student motivation

Literature Review
Perceived Usefulness of ChatGPT in Higher Education
AkeythemeinrecentliteratureistheperceivedusefulnessofChatGPTinenhancing
students’ academic experiences. Multiple studies highlight how students view
ChatGPTasatoolthatincreasesefficiency,savestime,andsupportsacademicengage-
ment. For instance, Holland & Ciachir (2024) found that higher education (HE) stu-
dents in Management programs appreciated ChatGPT’s immediacy and time-saving
capabilities, particularly in generating ideas and providing reassurance during aca-
demic work. Similarly, Fauzi et al. (2023) reported that ChatGPT helps students
quickly access relevant information, refine grammar, expand vocabulary, and
improvewritingstyle.Theseperceivedbenefitscontributetoagreatersenseofproduc-
tivity, enhanced timemanagement, and increased academic motivation.
Das&Madhusudan(2024)alsoobservedthatstudentsuseChatGPTtoaidunder-
standingofcomplexconceptsandgainconvenientaccesstoacademicresources.These
findingsareechoedin(Zou&Huang,2023)study,whichfoundthatperceiveduseful-
ness was a major factor influencing doctoral students’ behavioral intention to use
ChatGPT,especiallyforacademicwriting.Collectively,thesestudiessuggestthatstu-
dentsseeChatGPTnotonlyasafunctionaltoolbutasasupportivelearningcompanion
that fosters academic success.

Factors Influencing Behavioral Intention to Use ChatGPT
Much of the emerging research has focused on identifying the factors that influence
students’behavioralintention(BI)touseChatGPT.Oneofthemostconsistentpredic-
tors across studies is performance expectancy—the belief that using ChatGPT will
improve academic outcomes. This was evident in (Habibi et al., 2023) large-scale
surveyofIndonesianHEstudents,whereperformanceexpectancy,alongwithfacilitat-
ingconditionsandhedonicmotivation,werethestrongestpredictorsofBI.Theirstudy
also identified social influence and habit as significant contributors, suggesting that
peer usage androutine integrationencourage continued use.
Similarly, Strzelecki (2024) found that among Polish HE students, habit was the
most significant predictor of BI, followed closely by performance expectancy and
hedonicmotivation.Effortexpectancy,socialinfluence,andpricevaluealsopositively

behavior than to intention.

Theoretical Framework
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) developed by Davis (1989b) was used as the
theoretical framework to guide our study. Intending to explain how users come to
accept and use technology, TAM posits that two primary factors, Perceived
Usefulness(PU)andPerceivedEaseofUse(PEU),influenceanindividual’sdecision
to adopt technology. PU refers to the degree to which a person believes that using a
particular system would enhance their job performance, while PEU refers to the
degree to which a person believes that using the system would be free of effort.
Thesefactorsshapeusers’attitudestowardusingthetechnology,whichsubsequently
affecttheirbehavioralintentionstouseit,andultimately,theiractualusagebehavior.
TAM also considers external variables, such as system features and user training,
which can impact PU and PEU. For our study, especially the aspect of PEU in
theTAMModelisusefultounderstandthepatternsofChatGPTusage,asweantici-
pate that the more useful ChatGPT is perceived by students for a particular task, the
more likely they will use itfor thistask more frequently.
Recent studies have explored the application and extension of the TAM in higher
education, focusing on various factors influencing students’ acceptance and use of
digitaltoolsandtechnologies.OnestudyexploreshowextendingTAMbyincorporat-
ing factors such as perceived security, relevance, and personal investment can better
explainhighereducationstudents’useofdigitalacademicreadingtools,highlighting
thesignificantcontributionsofpsychologicalandacademicfactors(Lin&Yu,2023).
AsystematicreviewexamineshowTAMhasbeenutilizedduringtheCOVID-19pan-
demic to ensure the sustainability of higher education, identifying key external vari-
ables like self-efficacy, subjective norms, and perceived enjoyment as significant
factors influencing technology acceptance and emphasizing the need for further
research on lecturers and mixed-method studies (Rosli et al., 2022). Another study
focusesontheroleofperceivedenjoymentintheacceptanceofdigitalcommunication

Research Questions
Although the aforementioned studies provide valuable insights into students’ accep-
tance, adoption and use of ChatGPT, there is a significant gap in examining its
usage across a broader spectrum of academic fields and levels. Understanding these
patternswouldallowfortheoptimizationofChatGPT’sbenefitsbytailoringitsappli-
cationstothediverseneedsofdifferentstudentgroups.Toaddressthisresearchgap,
this study aimed to analyze ChatGPT usage patterns across various academic disci-
plinesandgaugetheimpactofthesedifferencesonstudents’perceivedacademicper-
formance. By exploring these differences, our goal is to provide insights and
recommendations for effectively integrating ChatGPT into educational practices to
enhance teaching andlearning in higher education.
Morespecifically,this paper examines the following research questions:
1. WhatarethepatternsofadoptionofChatGPTfunctionsforacademictasksamong
U.S. higher education students across different disciplines?
2. What is the relationship between U.S. higher education students’ ChatGPT usage
and itsperceivedimpact on their academic performance?
Data Collection
DataforthisstudywascollectedthroughanonlineQualtricssurvey(Appendix1)distrib-
uted to students across the United States through email and social media during Spring
2024, and through snowball sampling by asking the authors’ professional networks, to
assist with the distribution of the survey. A total of 1,109 students responded to the
survey.However,NineparticipantswhoindicatedthattheywerenotstudyingatHEinsti-
tutions in the United States and 413 participants who indicated that they had not used
ChatGPT before were excluded from the final data for analysis, resulting in a total of
687 students enrolled at a US Higher Education institution at different degree levels
(rangingfromundergraduatetodoctoral)thatwereanalyzedforthisstudy.

ChatGPT Usage. Questionsinthissectionincludedbothclosed-endedandopen-ended
questions. Eleven closed-ended questions explored the frequency of HE students’
ChatGPT usage on a 5-point Likert Scale focusing on various academic purposes,
such as writing assignments, brainstorming ideas, learning new topics, paraphrasing,
coding-related tasks, among others. In this section, students were also invited to
answer open-ended questions to describe any additional uses of ChatGPT and
provide detailed examples of specific academic tasks or projects where they utilized
ChatGPT. Reliability for these items was high (α=86) ‘indicating students who
usedChatGPTinoneareaarelikleytouseitinotherareas.Theseitemswereanalyzed
separately for investigating the research questions.
Perceived Academic Performance and Overall Experience with ChatGPT. This section
sought to capture HE students’ overall experiences with ChatGPT, again through
closed-ended and open-ended questions. Students rated nine statements regarding
their perceptions on the positive impacts of ChatGPT on completing academic tasks,
enhancing academic performance, motivation, productivity, and confidence.
Cronbach’s Alpha for these items was .93. Additionally, they completed four items
asking them about instances when they’ve had negative experiences using ChatGPT
(α=.79). They were then invited to give details about any challenges or difficulties
encountered while using ChatGPT and the accuracy and usefulness of ChatGPT’s
responses.

Data Analysis
Data analysis procedures were based on the specific research questions that we
exploredforthisstudy.ForRQ1,whichfocusedonidentifyingthepatternsofadoption
ofChatGPTfunctionsforacademictasksandwhetheradifferenceexistsacrossdisci-
plines,weconductedstatisticalanalysesonourquantitativedata,obtainedthroughthe
closed-ended questions, using SPSS version 29.0, namely descriptive statistics from
our data, and multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA). To provide further
detailsonthisquestion,weconductedqualitative contentanalysisontheopen-ended
questionsthataskedfurtherdetailsofstudents’ChatGPTusagepatterns.Theiranswers
werecompiledintoaworddocumentandcategorizeddisciplinebydiscipline.Through
thematic coding and categorizing of this data (Saldaña, 2021), the emergent themes
were grouped according to similarities and differences across disciplines. For our
RQ2, quantitative data, obtained through the closed-ended questions for the
Perceived Academic Performance section of our survey, was analyzed through
Pearson r correlation analysis to find if there is a relationship between ChatGPT
usage and perceived academic performance. In order to do that, a composite was
first created with the survey items to reflect how ChatGPT has aided students in
their academic endeavors (α=.93), and named as perceived academic performance
(PAP). Additionally,thissection included questions about anychallenges ordifficul-
tiesencounteredwhileusingChatGPTandtheaccuracyandusefulnessofChatGPT’s
responses. A composite was created from these items as well to reflect negative aca-
demic experience (NAE) with ChatGPT (α=.79). Moreover, a One-way ANOVA
andTukey’sHSDtestswereruntofindiftherearedifferencesbetweenperceivedaca-
demicperformanceduetoChatGPTamongdifferentdisciplines,andifso,whichones.

Discussion
Thisstudyaimedtoexplorethedifferencesinthefrequencyandpurposeofpatternsof
ChatGPT adoption among U.S. higher education students across various disciplines.
Despite the growing interest in integrating ChatGPT in educational settings, there
remainsagapinunderstandinghowthistechnologyisutilizeddifferentlyacrossaca-
demic disciplines and the implications of these differences on student learning out-
comes. Previous research has primarily focused on general acceptance and perceived
benefits of AI tools, without delving deeply into discipline-specific usage patterns
(Das&Madhusudan,2024;Strzelecki,2024).Thisstudyseekstofillthisgapbypro-
vidingdetailedinsightsintothevariedapplicationsofChatGPTamongstudentsfrom
different academic backgrounds.
OurfindingsfortheRQ1,onthepatternsofChatGPTadoptionamongU.S.HEstu-
dentsacrossdisciplines,alignwithandextendpriorresearchonthistopic.Similarto
previous studies, this study also confirms that students use ChatGPT as an academic
support tool for a variety of academic tasks. Particularly when it comes to academic
tasksinvolvingwriting,studentsreturntoChatGPTinallstagesfromideageneration
and outlining to editing and refining their writing, regardless of their academic disci-
pline. This finding supports previous research on the use of AI tools in academic
writing and research (Khalifa & Albadawy, 2024). The fact that students use
ChatGPT for writing-related tasks regardless of their discipline also reflects the high
perceivedusefulnessofChatGPTinfacilitatingwritingprocesses,afindingsupported
by previous studies which demonstrate that AI tools help streamline the writing
process, providing feedback and suggestions for improvement (Habibi et al., 2023;
Maheshwari, 2024). The consistent use of ChatGPT for similar tasks—across disci-
plines such as Arts and Design, Business, Education, and Engineering—reflects its
