 Introduction

In the fast-paced modern digital age, technology is an integral component of education, transforming
how  students  learn,  interact  with  information,  and  perform  schoolwork.  Among  numerous
technological  advances,  AI-based  platforms  such  as  ChatGPT  have  achieved  great  popularity
among students of all stages. Students use ChatGPT for a whole variety of reasons to quickly get
information for research assignments, to come up with ideas for essays, to proofread and enhance
their own writing, or even to solve problems in courses like math and science (Qazi et al., 2024).

The ease and quickness of access to these tools can make learning more effective, assist
students  in  controlling  their  workload,  and  offer  instant  aid  in  cases  where  they  feel  lost  or  are
unable to grasp tricky concepts. Still, the growing use of AI tools also generates significant concerns
regarding  their  wider  impacts  on  learning  and  intellectual  development.  While  there  are  some
students and teachers who believe that ChatGPT can be a useful friend, providing explanations,
examples, and direction that increase comprehension, others fear repeated usage to be dependent,
stifle critical thinking, or shorten the potential for creative discovery. For example, a student who
repeatedly  employs  ChatGPT  to  write  essays  may  save  time,  but  may  also  lose  out  on  building
creative ideas, honing argumentation techniques, or becoming fully engaged with the subject matter.
Likewise, instructors may find that even though assignments are complete and polished, students'
conceptual knowledge or problem-solving skills may not improve at the same rate. These diverse
viewpoints underscore the need for us to question more critically the place of AI in education today.

more  efficient,  provide  quick  access  to  information,  and  even  help  improve  grades. At  the
same time, concerns have emerged about potential negative effects, such as over-dependence,
reduced  independent  learning,  or  a  decline  in  critical  thinking  skills.  Despite  its  growing
use, there is still uncertainty about whether ChatGPT genuinely enhances students’ learning
experiences or creates unintended challenges in their academic performance. This ambiguity
highlights the need for research that directly investigates students’ experiences and perceptions.

broader implications for learning and academic development. The research aims to examine
how students use ChatGPT for academic purposes, including studying, conducting research,
and completing assignments, while also analyzing its impact on their learning efficiency and
overall academic performance. It further seeks to assess students’ perceptions of ChatGPT as a
learning tool by identifying both its benefits and limitations. In addition, the study will explore
how ChatGPT may reduce student-teacher interaction and investigate its influence on students’
critical thinking skills.

and  efficiency,  and  its  cost-effectiveness,  given  that  it  requires  no  additional  human  labour.
These attributes make it an attractive tool for both students and educators seeking accessible
support in academic work.

Beyond efficiency, ChatGPT has also been associated with improvements in research
skills and academic exploration. Kasneci et al. (2023) argue that ChatGPT can help students
identify  unexplored  aspects  of  a  topic,  offer  resources,  and  introduce  them  to  new  research
areas, ultimately broadening their understanding. Khalil and Er (2023) tested ChatGPT’s role
in academic writing and plagiarism detection, finding that out of 50 essays generated, 40 had a
similarity score of 20% or less. This suggests that ChatGPT can produce largely original work,
allowing students to practice writing and idea development in ways that may not always be
flagged as plagiarism.

Discipline-specific studies further highlight ChatGPT’s benefits. For example, Mike et
al. (2024) report that in business informatics, ChatGPT significantly improved students’ speed
in test completion, showing its value in managing academic workloads. In medical education,
ChatGPT has proven effective in supporting students’ clinical reasoning and producing accurate
exam answers (Kung, 2023). Educators themselves have also adopted ChatGPT as a support
tool. According to Shehri et al. (2023), teachers are increasingly using it to create instructional
content, quizzes, and summaries, which reduces preparation time and allows for greater focus
on student engagement and interactive teaching.

Despite its promise, ChatGPT has also raised significant concerns regarding its effects
on  students’  academic  development. A  recurring  issue  is  the  weakening  of  critical  thinking
and independent learning skills. Essel et al. (2023) argue that over-reliance on AI discourages
students  from  engaging  in  self-directed  inquiry,  as  they  often  accept  ChatGPT’s  responses
without questioning or analyzing them. This aligns with findings by Arhin (2024) and Hasanein
(2023), who stress that higher education institutions must develop strategies to prevent students
from replacing intellectual effort with technological shortcuts. Another major area of concern
is academic dishonesty and plagiarism. Almeida et al. (2023) and Dempere (2023) found that
ChatGPT’s ability to generate polished text makes it easier for students to submit AI-produced
work as their own. This poses serious challenges for teachers and institutions, who struggle to
detect AI-generated content. Although plagiarism-detection tools are improving, Khalil and Er
(2023) demonstrated that many ChatGPT-generated essays escape detection, raising questions
about  fairness  and  assessment  reliability  in  education.  Ferreira  (2024)  extends  this  critique,
warning  that  the  normalization  of  AI-written  assignments  may  erode  the  value  of  original
student work and undermine the integrity of academic institutions.

Concerns  also  exist  regarding  the  depth  and  quality  of  learning  when  ChatGPT  is
used excessively. Ferreira (2024) notes that ChatGPT often produces shallow or incomplete
explanations  that  do  not  foster  deep  understanding.  Muhammad,  Atia,  and  Shoaib  (2022)
further  highlight  that  the  system’s  responses  can  be  inconsistent,  with  accuracy  depending
heavily on how questions are phrased. This variability risks confusing students, who may not
always be equipped to evaluate the reliability of information. Gill et al. (2024) similarly caution
that  ChatGPT  can  generate  false  or  misleading  content  with  high  confidence,  creating  what
scholars describe as “hallucinated knowledge.” Such inaccuracies, when absorbed uncritically,

ChatGPT as a double-edged sword in educational contexts. Empirical studies reflect this dual
nature. Mike et al. (2024) found that while ChatGPT allows students to complete tasks more
quickly, it does not necessarily improve accuracy or comprehension. Shehri et al. (2023) also

student work. Pawar et al. (2023) confirm this finding, noting that although ChatGPT supports
students in academic tasks, it can discourage independent thought when used as a substitute for
personal effort.

and a challenge in education. Its capacity to enhance efficiency, broaden research, and support
educators  is  clear,  yet  the  risks  of  dependency,  plagiarism,  shallow  learning,  and  reduced
creativity  remain  significant. The  literature  collectively  suggests  that  the  value  of  ChatGPT
depends largely on how it is integrated into educational practice: either as a complementary aid
to enhance learning or as a replacement that undermines essential academic skills.

Research Methodology

This study used a quantitative research design to examine the impact of ChatGPT on students’
academic performance and learning habits. A survey approach was adopted because it allowed
the collection of data directly from students in a measurable form and made it possible to analyze
patterns in how ChatGPT was used in academic contexts. The target population consisted of
BS  students  enrolled  at  the  Government  M.A.O  Graduate  College  and  Government  Islamia
Graduate College Civil Lines, Lahore. These colleges were chosen because they were accessible
to the researchers and because it was observed that many students in these institutions actively
used ChatGPT for their academic work.

A  total  of  200  students  between  the  ages  of  18  and  25  participated  in  the  study.
The  sample  included  students  from  a  variety  of  disciplines  to  ensure  diversity  in  academic
backgrounds.  Systematic  sampling  was  employed,  which  meant  that  students  were  selected
at regular intervals from class lists to avoid bias and ensure that the sample represented the

students while still keeping the process structured and efficient.

Data were collected using a structured questionnaire developed by the researchers. The
questionnaire  included  both  closed-ended  questions  and  Likert-scale  items. These  questions
were designed to measure how often students used ChatGPT, how they used it, the benefits
they perceived, and the problems or challenges they encountered. In addition, the questionnaire
gathered  information  on  whether  students  felt  that  their  study  habits,  such  as  note-taking,
independent thinking, and reliance on teachers, had changed after they began using ChatGPT.
The questionnaires were distributed in person to the selected students, and participants were
informed of the purpose of the research before filling them out. The surveys were collected on
the same day to ensure a complete response set and to avoid delays in data analysis. Out of
200 questionnaires, all were returned, giving the study a 100% response rate. In this research,
ChatGPT use was considered the independent variable, while students’ academic performance,
study habits, creativity, and critical thinking served as the dependent variables. These dependent
variables were chosen because the study aimed to investigate how ChatGPT affected not just
academic scores but also broader aspects of the learning process.

For operational purposes, ChatGPT was defined as an artificial intelligence tool that
assisted students by answering questions, providing explanations, suggesting solutions, drafting
assignments, creating summaries, and, in some cases, even generating software code. While
it  clearly  made  academic  work  easier  and  saved  time,  the  study  also  examined  its  potential
negative  consequences.  These  included  students  copying  answers  directly  from  ChatGPT
without  modification,  becoming  less  willing  to  take  notes  in  class,  showing  less  interest  in
listening  to  their  teachers,  and  relying  too  heavily  on  ChatGPT  for  ideas,  which  sometimes
led  to  plagiarism  and  reduced  critical  thinking.  The  study  also  considered  how  much  time
students spent using ChatGPT daily and compared their study habits before and after adopting
this technology. After the questionnaires were collected, the data were coded and entered into
the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) for analysis. Descriptive statistics, such
as frequencies and percentages, were calculated to summarize the responses. The results were
then presented in tables, graphs, and figures to provide a clear and accessible picture of how
students perceived the role of ChatGPT in their academic lives. These statistical results were
later interpreted in relation to the research questions to assess both the positive and negative
impacts of ChatGPT on student learning and performance.

Discussion

The  results  of  this  study  showed  that  ChatGPT  had  both  positive  and  negative  effects  on
students’ academic practices, confirming that its impact depended largely on how it was used. A
majority of students reported using ChatGPT frequently for academic support, particularly for
completing assignments, summarizing content, and preparing for exams. More than 70% of the
respondents agreed that ChatGPT made it easier to understand difficult topics and saved them
time in their studies. These findings support earlier research by Gill et al. (2024) and Kasneci

7

Exploring the Impact of ChatGPT on University Students’ Vol 2, Issue 1 (2025)Journal of Creative Arts & Communicationet al. (2023), who argued that ChatGPT functions as an efficient learning companion, helping
students engage with challenging material in more manageable ways. At the same time, the
statistical results highlighted concerning trends that aligned with the negative themes found in
the literature. Almost half of the students admitted that they often copied and pasted ChatGPT’s
responses without modification, suggesting a decline in independent thinking and originality.
This tendency toward academic dishonesty supports the claims of Almeida et al. (2023) and
Dempere  (2023),  who  emphasized  that AI-generated  text  increases  risks  of  plagiarism  and
undermines  academic  integrity.  Furthermore,  a  significant  portion  of  students  reported  that
they relied on ChatGPT more than on their teachers or textbooks, with over 60% admitting
that  they  preferred  consulting  ChatGPT  instead  of  asking  instructors  for  help.  This  reflects
the concern raised by Shehri et al. (2023), who warned that heavy reliance on AI could reduce
valuable student-teacher interaction and weaken collaborative learning.

Another important finding was that many students reported a decrease in study habits
such as note-taking and memorization. Over one-third of the participants stated that they no
longer felt motivated to take class notes or attempt to remember concepts on their own because
they felt ChatGPT could provide answers instantly when needed. This observation supports
the arguments of Essel et al. (2023) and Ferreira (2024), who cautioned that the convenience
of AI  tools  can  lead  to  shallow  learning  and  reduced  critical  thinking.  In  addition,  the  fact
that some students expressed more trust in ChatGPT’s responses than in their own reasoning
highlights the risk of declining self-confidence in personal intellectual abilities. Taken together,
these findings confirm that ChatGPT acted as a “double-edged sword” in the academic lives
of  students,  echoing  the  conclusions  of  Sok  and  Heng  (2023)  and  Montenegro-Rueda  et  al.
(2023). On one hand, the statistical evidence demonstrated that it improved efficiency, provided
alternative explanations, and supported academic success; on the other, it revealed dependency,
plagiarism,  and  weakened  independent  learning.  The  discussion  suggests  that  the  value  of
ChatGPT depended not on the tool itself but on the way, students chose to engage with it.

The  implications  of  these  findings  are  significant  for  educators  and  institutions.
Students need to be guided toward responsible and critical use of ChatGPT so that it serves
as a supplement to their learning rather than a replacement for their own intellectual effort.
At the same time, teachers and policymakers should develop clear guidelines and training for
students to ensure that AI tools are integrated in ways that promote creativity, critical thinking,
and genuine learning.

Conclusion

This study looked at how students use ChatGPT in their academic life and what effects it has
on their learning and performance. The results show that ChatGPT is both helpful and harmful,
depending on how students use it. On the positive side, many students said ChatGPT makes it
easier for them to understand difficult topics. It saves time, helps with assignments, and gives
quick summaries before exams. A large number of students now prefer using ChatGPT instead of
asking teachers or reading books. They find it more convenient and helpful in completing tasks
faster. However, the study also found some serious concerns. Some students are becoming too
dependent on ChatGPT. Many admitted they copy and paste answers without changing them.
Some feel less motivated to think deeply, take class notes, or remember things on their own. A
few even said they trust ChatGPT more than their own ideas, which shows that their confidence
in themselves is decreasing.

These findings show that while ChatGPT can support learning, using it too much can harm
creativity, memory, and critical thinking. It is important for students to use ChatGPT in a balanced
way, as a tool to support learning, not replace it. Teachers and colleges should guide students on

become an important part of modern education. It has the power to improve academic life if used
responsibly. But overuse or misuse can lead to poor learning habits and reduced personal growth.
Limitations & Recommendations

This study was limited in scope to two government colleges in Lahore, which restricts
the  generalizability  of  the  findings  to  broader  populations  or  different  educational  contexts.
The  use  of  a  self-reported  questionnaire  also  posed  the  risk  of  bias,  as  some  students  may
have exaggerated or minimized their reliance on ChatGPT. In addition, the study employed
only a quantitative approach, which provided useful statistical insights but did not capture the
deeper, qualitative perspectives of students regarding their motivations, emotions, or long-term
experiences with AI use. Finally, the study focused primarily on academic performance, study
habits, and critical thinking, leaving out other dimensions such as ethical concerns, psychological
well-being, and the evolving role of educators in AI-mediated learning environments.

In  light  of  these  findings,  it  is  recommended  that  students  approach  ChatGPT  as
a  supplementary  aid  rather  than  a  replacement  for  independent  study  and  critical  thinking.
Teachers should consider integrating AI tools into learning activities in structured ways that
encourage evaluation and comparison rather than passive acceptance of responses. Institutions
could also develop clear policies on ethical AI use and organize workshops to build students’
digital literacy and awareness of issues such as plagiarism and over-dependence. At a broader
level, policymakers and researchers should promote responsible AI integration into education
by providing guidelines, monitoring practices, and exploring its long-term implications. Future
studies may adopt a mixed-methods approach and expand to other institutions or regions to
provide a more comprehensive understanding of ChatGPT’s impact on learning.
