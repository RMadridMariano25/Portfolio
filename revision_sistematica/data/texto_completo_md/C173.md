FeedbackEvolutioninEducation
Theevolutionoffeedbackineducationalcontextshasundergoneasignificantchange
over the past few decades. Early forms of simple feedback, such as Knowledge of
Results (KR), developed in the 1960s, were usually confined to binary responses,
merely indicating whether a task was correct or incorrect. A pioneering example is
theautomatedcorrectionsystemintroducedbyHollingsworth(Hollingsworth,1960),
whichfocusedmainlyonverifyingthesyntacticcorrectnessofcode.Thesesystems
proved useful for learning basic skills, but they lacked the ability to provide more
elaborate guidance or to promote critical reflection and deep understanding on the
partofthestudent.
Subsequent technological advancements have led to the development of more
sophisticated feedback mechanisms, particularly in programming courses. For
instance, feedback systems employing static and dynamic code analysis techniques
havebecomeincreasinglyprevalent(Keuningetal.,2018).Staticanalysisallowsfor
detectingincorrectsyntacticstructuresorconceptswithoutexecutingthecode,while
dynamicanalysiscomparestheoutputofexecutedcodewiththeexpectedresult.These
approacheshavepavedthewayformoreadvancedtypesoffeedback,suchasKnowl-
edgeofCorrectResponse(KCR),whichprovidesmoreinformativeinsightsthanKR,
though still limited to confirming correctness or revealing the mistake. From these
foundations,ElaboratedFeedback(EF)hasemergedasamoresophisticatedsolution,
capable of providing in-depth explanations regarding students’ correct or incorrect
answers.Forinstance,DiriKoçandÖner(2023)showedthatEFcanbeparticularly
usefulincomplexcontexts,asitallowsstudentstoconnectnewknowledgetoprevious
knowledge,promotingthedevelopmentofadvancedcognitiveskills.
However,EFcanleadtocognitiveoverloadifnotproperlymanaged,especiallyfor
studentswithlimitedbasicskills(Lipnevich&Panadero,2021).Additionaldetailsor
elaborate feedback, such as narrative explanations, may overload students’ working
memory,makingitdifficultforthemtointegrateandapplytheadvicereceived.This
phenomenonisrelatedtotheprinciplesofCognitiveLoadTheory(CLT)andbecomes
particularlyproblematicforstudentswithfewerbasicskillsorthoseunabletohandle
multiple feedback cycles simultaneously (Sweller et al., 2011). Similarly, the study
ofWangetal.(2019)foundthatdetailedexplanationsstimulatedlearners’perception
of feedback, decreased the cognitive load due to ineffective instructional guidance,
andincreasedlearningmotivation.Ontheotherhand,astudybyLangetal.(2022)

Conclusions,LimitationsandFutureWork
ThisstudysummarizedanattempttouseLLMstoenhancefeedbackindatascience
education.Theresearchrevealsthatthecombinedfeedback,i.e.,LLM-generatedfeed-
backcombinedwithtraditionalmethods,ifcomparedwithatraditionalAIfeedback,
didnotyieldclearbenefitsintermsoflearningoutcomes.Conversely,theresultscom-

Thislatterpointneedsfurtherresearch,particularlyinlightofthecontrastingresults

thatstudentswhosolvedmanydifferentexercisesachieved,onaverage,bettergrades
thantheothers,withstatisticallysignificantdifferenceswhenthenumberofexercises

and/orthroughthesystem,e.g.,withremindersorpushnotifications.
In summary, the results suggest that LLM-enhanced feedback, if compared with
traditionalAIfeedback,doesnotseemtoincreasethelearningoutcomes,andrequires
additional cognitive effort for students. On the other hand, students appreciated the
LLM-enhanced feedback (more than expected) and thoroughly used the system for
