Keywords:

While Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools have been used by an increasing number of students, many
teachers  and  researchers  recognized  that  excessive  use  of  these  tools  may  have  adverse  impacts  on  students’
academic  development.  However,  the  underlying  mechanisms  driving  GenAI  reliance  and  its  consequences
remain underexplored. This study aimed to address these gaps by investigating the relationships among academic
self-efficacy,  academic  anxiety,  performance  expectations,  and  GenAI  reliance,  as  well  as  examining  the  con-
sequences of GenAI reliance and coping strategies to mitigate these issues. Data were collected from 337 uni-
versity  students  in  a  Chinese  university  and  analyzed  using  Pearson  correlation,  chain  mediation  effect,  and
thematic  analysis.  The  results  indicated  no  significant  direct  relationship  between  academic  self-efficacy  and
GenAI  reliance.  However,  academic  anxiety  and  performance  expectations  were  found  to  mediate  this  rela-
tionship. The most salient consequences of GenAI reliance were decreased independent thinking ability, reduced
innovative thinking ability, restricted self-reflection, and limited critical thinking ability. Additionally, students’
self-initiated  and  expected  teacher-initiated  strategies  to  alleviate  these  consequences  were  recognized.  This
study contributes to the limited understanding of GenAI reliance and provides valuable insights for educators and
academic institutions in directing students toward the appropriate and critical utilization of GenAI tools.

1. Introduction

Generative AI (GenAI) tools have attracted global attention since the
public release of ChatGPT in November 2022 [1]. As a branch of arti-
ficial intelligence (AI), GenAI is distinct from the traditional forms of AI,
which  relies  on  machine  learning  (ML)  algorithms  and  is  capable  of
sorting out specific types of tasks with pre-programmed responses [2]. In
contrast, powered by deep learning, GenAI could process large amounts
of data and generate grammatically accurate human-like texts and other
types of multi-modal content such as texts, images, and videos according
to the specific requirements described in the users prompts [2–4]. These
tools have been frequently used for generating and summarizing texts,
brainstorming  ideas,  generating  images,  data  analysis,  translation,
paraphrasing texts, and so forth [5].

Due to these powerful affordances, GenAI is anticipated to have the
potential to influence many fields such as education [6], translation [7],
healthcare [8], and so forth. Among these, education is one of the most
profoundly  impacted  areas  by  GenAI  [6].  GenAI  can  function  as  a
teaching assistant, helping to reduce teachers’ workload and save time

by  assisting  them  in  lesson  preparation,  development  of  assessment
items, and feedback generation [2]. GenAI tools can also be employed as
a learning assistant for students to search for information, brainstorm
ideas, proofread, and generate individualized feedback and suggestions
for improvement on their work regardless of time constraints [4,9], and
such  personalized  learning  environments  can  enhance  students’  aca-
demic outcomes [9,10].

However,  even  though  GenAI  tools  have  enormous  potential  to
facilitate the development of education, the limitations and risks caused
by these tools should not be neglected [11]. For example, GenAI tools
have  been  found  many  limitations  such  as  privacy  issues,  confidenti-
ality, and ethical issues [12,13]. In addition, GenAI-generated content
may  contain  misleading,  inaccurate  information,  and  harmful,  biased
content since the content is generated based on the Internet and other
sources [14]. This implies that students should have the awareness to
use these tools appropriately, ethically, and critically [12,2].

Another salient issue arising from the use of GenAI tools is students’
excessive reliance on these tools. GenAI is capable of generating desired
responses based on users’ prompts [15,16], which means students can

use  GenAI  tools  to  generate  answers  and  easily  complete  tasks  the

duces opportunities for them to engage in the critical thinking process.
This  could  gradually  diminish  students’  discernment  and  cognitive
skills, hindering the development of their critical and creative thinking
abilities [17]. The long-term use of GenAI tools for completing assign-

Previous studies have indicated that academic self-efficacy plays a
crucial role in predicting whether students will become overly depen-
dent  on  GenAI  tools  [21,22].  When  students  have  a  high  level  of
self-efficacy  in  their  ability  to  complete  tasks  and  achieve  goals
academically, they are more likely to explore and think independently
rather than immediately relying on digital tools such as GenAI [23,22].
On the contrary, students with lower self-efficacy may feel more stressed
when  facing  learning  challenges,  and  thus  they  tend  to  rely  more  on
GenAI tools to assist them or even replace their learning process [24].
Students  with  relatively  low  self-efficacy  are  more  prone  to  have  a
higher level of academic anxiety, which further prompts them to rely
more on GenAI to alleviate learning pressure [23]. Additionally, if stu-
dents  have  excessively  high  academic  expectations  that  do  not  align
with their actual abilities, they may seek quick solutions, such as relying
on  GenAI  to  meet  the  predetermined  goals  [18].  Zhang  et  al.’s  [22]
study among Korean university students further demonstrated that ac-
ademic stress and performance expectations, as two crucial psycholog-
ical  variables,  not  only  independently  affect  students’  academic
behavior and attitudes, but also mediate the relationship between aca-
demic self-efficacy and GenAI dependence.

Overall, it is well-documented that excessive reliance on GenAI tools
can  have  salient  negative  impacts  on  students  academic  learning
[17–20].  However,  underlying  psychological  factors  influencing  stu-
dents’  GenAI  reliance  remain  underresearched.  Some  factors,  such  as
academic  self-efficacy,  academic  anxiety,  and  performance  expecta-
tions, have been identified as potentially influencing students’ reliance
on GenAI tools [18,22,23]. Therefore, this study investigated the rela-
tionship between these factors and GenAI reliance.

This  study  contributes  to  the  limited  literature  on  the  influencing
factors and possible consequences of GenAI reliance. By scrutinizing the
chain mediating effects of academic anxiety and performance expecta-
tions, the study provides a nuanced perspective on how these variables
interact  with  academic  self-efficacy  to  shape  students’  reliance  on
GenAI. The findings provide valuable insights for teachers to develop
tailored  interventions  to  enhance  students’  academic  self-efficacy,
reduce  anxiety,  and  establish  realistic  performance  expectations,
thereby optimizing the educational benefits of GenAI tools on instead of
over-reliance  on  them.  The  findings  also  inform  teachers  and  educa-
tional institutes to refine their current AI policies so as to guide students
about the appropriate use of GenAI tools to support their learning.

2. Literature review

2.1. Academic self-efficacy and GenAI reliance

Self-efficacy refers to an individual’  s belief in their ability to suc-
cessfully  perform  a  specific  behavior  in  a  given  situation  (Bandura,
[25]). In the context of education, Parmaksız [26] argued that academic
self-efficacy can be considered as students’ confidence or belief in their
capability  to  achieve  learning  goals  and  complete  tasks  during  the
learning  process.  Students  with  high  academic  self-efficacy  are  more

likely to maintain a positive attitude towards learning, persist through
challenges,  work  harder,  and  ultimately  achieve  better  academic
achievements [27].

According  to  Zhang  et  al.  [22],  GenAI  reliance  is  defined  as  the
excessive dependency of individuals or organizations on GenAI tools for
decision-making  or  task  performance,  while  neglecting  their  own
judgment and decision-making agency. This may result in their reduced
ability to adapt and innovate in response to changes [28]. In the current
study, GenAI reliance refers to students’ excessive dependency on con-
tent  generated  by  GenAI  tools  for  learning  purposes,  and  this  could
result in a decrease in independent thinking and creativity.

Current research has highlighted that, while the application of AI in
the field of education can bring convenience and improved efficiency, it
may also have adverse impacts on students’ cognitive development and
social values [29]. Over-reliance on AI tools may result in laziness, the
spread of inaccurate and biased information, reduced creativity, and/or
decreased  independent  thinking  skill.  Similarly,  Zhai  et  al.  [28]  con-
ducted  a  systematic  review  of  students’  excessive  dependence  on  AI
chatbots and investigated how this reliance affects their cognitive abil-
ities,  including  decision-making,  critical  thinking,  and  analytical
reasoning. The findings suggested that excessive reliance on AI chatbots
may  impair  students’  cognitive  abilities,  leading  them  to  favor  quick
solutions rather than more optimal and thoughtful ones.

Students  with  low  academic  self-efficacy  are  prone  to  display
reduced motivation and engagement in learning activities and generally
lack confidence in their ability to complete academic tasks [30]. This
psychological  state  is  usually  manifested  as  avoidance  of  overcoming
learning  challenges,  skepticism  towards  academic  success,  and  a  ten-
dency to give up when faced with difficulties [31]. Previous studies have
suggested that GenAI can provide students with personalized learning
support and assistance such as proofreading, outline generation, instant
feedback, offering suggestions for improvement, and so on, which could
to some extent reduce students’  learning anxiety and help them com-
plete various tasks more efficiently [17]. Therefore, students with lower
self-efficacy are more likely to develop the reliance on GenAI tools.

Previous  studies  have  consistently  demonstrated  a  negative  corre-
lation between academic self-efficacy and addictive behavior [32,33]. In
line with previous research on GenAI reliance, this study proposes the
following hypothesis:

H1. Academic self-efficacy is negatively correlated with GenAI reliance.

2.2. Mediating role of academic anxiety

Anxiety,  as  a  multifaceted  emotional  state,  manifests  as  an  in-
dividual’s emotional response to anticipated dangers or threats, which
includes tension, unease, worry, and distress [34]. As a domain-specific
concept, academic anxiety refers to the anxious feelings and stress that
students experience when dealing with exams, performance evaluations,
and heavy academic pressure within the learning and academic envi-
ronment  [35].  Academic  anxiety  has  profound  impacts  on  students’
learning process and academic performance. A high level of academic
anxiety  may  distract  students’  attention,  impair  their  memory,  and
decrease  their  thinking  ability,  ultimately  impacting  their  academic
performance [36]. Prior studies have identified a negative correlation
between  academic  efficacy  and  academic  anxiety  [36–38].  This  in-
dicates that when individuals have a high level of self-efficacy in their
own abilities and performance in the academic field, their level of aca-
demic  anxiety  tends  to  be  low.  Conversely,  individuals  with  limited
academic self-efficacy may experience higher academic anxiety.

Long-term exposure to high-intensity academic pressure can easily
lead students to experience a state of anxiety, characterized by excessive
anxiety  about  academic  tasks,  self-doubt,  and  emotional  fluctuations
[35]. When confronted with academic anxiety, students often actively
seek external support once they recognize that it is difficult for them to
tackle it independently [39]. GenAI has attracted the attention of many

H2. Academic  anxiety  mediates  the  relationship  between  academic  self-

2.3. Mediating role of performance expectations

Performance expectations typically refer to an individual’s expected
outcomes or goals for a certain behavior or activity [43]. According to
Nikolopoulou  et  al.  [44],  an  individual’s  belief  in  how  much  specific
technologies can improve their performance is known as performance
expectations.  This  is  an  important  cognitive  factor  that  influences  in-
dividuals’  attitudes and sequentially affects their willingness to adopt
digital  technologies  such  as  GenAI.  Thus,  performance  expectations
have been recognized as a crucial factor in predicting individuals’ beliefs
and acceptance of digital tools [45–47].

In light of Brand et al. [48], individuals’ performance expectations
might  influence  the  relationship  between  psychological  factors  and
problematic use of digital tools. Hence, this study suggests that perfor-
mance expectations could mediate the relationship between academic
self-efficacy and GenAI reliance. The hypothesis posits that students who
exhibit lower academic self-efficacy may have higher performance ex-
pectations for technologies, which could consequently result in exces-
sive  use  of  GenAI  as  the  coping  strategy  in  their  academic  studies.
Therefore, this study proposes the following hypothesis:

H3. Performance expectations mediates the relationship between academic
self-efficacy and GenAI reliance.

2.4. Sequential mediation effects of academic anxiety and performance
expectations

To date, academic anxiety is prevalent among many students, which
not  only  heightens  their  fear  of  failure  and  uncertainty  regarding
learning  outcomes  [35],  but  significantly  influences  their  cognitive,
emotional, and behavioral decisions [49]. Students with low academic
self-efficacy are more likely to experience academic anxiety [37], and
may perceive GenAI as tools to compensate for their insufficient abilities
and  help  them  reach  their  desired  performance  expectations,  which
consequently leads to over-reliance on these tools to improve academic
performance [50]. Based on the aforementioned analysis, we propose a
hypothesis that academic anxiety and performance expectations act as
mediating mechanisms to establish a sequential link between academic
self-efficacy and GenAI reliance. Thus, hypothesis 4 was put forward as
follows:

H4. Academic anxiety and performance expectations serially mediate the
effect of academic self-efficacy on GenAI reliance.

2.5. Consequences of excessive use of artificial intelligence tools

Excessive use of GenAI tools can be defined as users’ over-reliance on
GenAI  tools  for  learning  tasks,  rather  than  engaging  deeply  with  the
learning materials or tasks themselves, which can potentially undermine

the development of critical thinking, problem-solving skills, and inde-
pendent learning [22,51]. Researchers have examined the consequences
of excessive use of AI tools from different perspectives. Seo et al.’s [52]
study explored the impacts of AI systems on learner-teacher interactions
by investigating 12 students and 11 teachers from diverse disciplinary
backgrounds  in  South  Korea.  Their  findings  indicated  that  receiving
excessive support from AI may lead to a superficial understanding of the
discipline knowledge and limit students’ ability to learn independently.
Similarly, a study conducted by Jin et al. [53] found that students were
concerned that their reliance on AI may weaken their learning ability
and  creativity.  Existing  research  has  revealed  the  potential  negative
effects of excessive reliance on AI, especially among university students.
Excessive use of AI has been found to potentially have adverse effects on
academic  achievement  and  mental  health  [18,14].  Considering  the
increasingly diverse functionalities and complexities of GenAI tools, it is
crucial  to  delve  into  the  reliance  on  these  tools  and  their  potential
ramifications.  Therefore,  this  study  formulates  the  following  research
question.

RQ. What  are  the  consequences  of  GenAI  reliance  from  students
perspectives?

3. Methods

3.1. Research context and participants

posive sampling, which allowed researchers to choose participants with
specific  characteristics  essential  for  research  purposes,  was  employed
[54].  According  to  the  curriculum  design  at  institution,  the  first-year
and  second-year  students  in  Bachelor  of  Arts  in  English  (B.A.  in  En-
glish) and Bachelor of Arts in Business English (B.A. in Business English)

courses.  Conversely,  the  third-year  and  fourth-year  students  in  these
programs  were  arranged  to  learn  specialized  courses  such  as  Second

and  B.A.  in  Business  English  programs  were  purposefully  selected  to
participate in the current study, and the 1st-year and 2nd-year students

3.2. Measurements

3.2.1. Questionnaire

The  questionnaire  included  the  following  components:  (1)  de-
mographic information, (2) an academic self-efficacy scale (5 items) (3)
an  academic  anxiety  scale  (8  items),  (4)  a  performance  expectations
scale (4 items), (5) a GenAI reliance scale (5 items), and (6) an open-
ended question regarding students perspectives of the consequences of
GenAI reliance (1 item). Please see Appendix I for the detailed items of
the questionnaire.

3.2.1.1. Academic  self-efficacy. Students’  academic  self-efficacy  was
measured through a 5-item general academic self-efficacy scale devel-
oped by van Zyl et al. [55]. Participants rated each item on a five-point
Likert  scale,  from  1  indicating  “strongly  disagree”  to  5  indicating
“strongly agree”. In light of van Zyl et al. [55], this scale demonstrated
good reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.78).

3.2.1.2. Academic  anxiety. Students’  academic  anxiety  was  evaluated
through  an  8-item  academic  anxiety  scale  adapted  from  the  study-
anxiety  scale  developed  by  Casali  et  al.  [56].  Participants  rated  each
item on a five-point Likert scale, from 1 indicating “strongly disagree” to
5  indicating  “strongly  agree”.  In  light  of  Casali  et  al.  [56],  this  scale
demonstrated high reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.87).

3.2.1.3. Performance  expectations. Performance  expectations  were
assessed through a 4-item performance expectation scale adapted from
Rivalldo and Widuri [57]. Participants rated each item on a five-point
Likert  scale,  from  1  indicating  “strongly  disagree”  to  5  indicating
“strongly  agree”.  In  light  of  Rivalldo  and  Widuri  [57],  this  scale
demonstrated high reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.95).

3.2.1.4. Generative  AI  reliance. Students’  reliance on GenAI tools was
measured through a 5-item GenAI reliance scale adapted from Zunaidah
et al. [51]. Participants rated each item on a five-point Likert scale, from
1 indicating “strongly disagree” to 5 indicating “strongly agree”. In light
of Zunaidah et al. [51], this scale demonstrated high reliability (Cron-
bach’s α=0.79).

3.2.1.5. Consequences of generative AI reliance. An open-ended question
“What negative impacts do you think excessive use of GenAI will bring?”
was purposefully developed to explore students perceptions and beliefs
about the consequences of GenAI reliance.

3.2.2. Follow-up semi-structured interviews

Semi-structured  interviews  were  utilized  to  explore  students’  sug-
gestions for mitigating the consequences of excessive reliance on GenAI.
This approach enables researchers to understand participants’ perspec-
tives of certain issues or specific circumstances they experienced [58].
After analyzing the negative consequences of GenAI reliance reported by
questionnaire respondents and  reviewing existing literature on GenAI
dependency (e.g., [28,22]), an interview guide was purposively devel-
oped. It can help researchers gain an in-depth understanding of partic-
ipants’ experiences and perceptions [54]. The guide consisted of three
main  sections:  (1)  general  experiences  of  using  GenAI  to  support
learning; (2) suggestions for avoiding consequences of excessive GenAI
reliance;  and  (3) closing.  The initial  guide  was  reviewed  by  two  pro-
fessors in education and revised according to their feedback to enhance
its  face  validity  and  relevance.  It  was  then  piloted  with  two  un-
dergraduates and further refined for clarity and readability [59]. The
finalized interview guide is included in Appendix II.

Participants were informed that they could use any language during
the  interview,  depending  on  their  preference.  Ultimately,  all  partici-
pants  opted  to  use  their  first  language,  Chinese.  The  interviews  were
conducted  in  person  in  a  reserved  classroom,  audio-recorded,  and
transcribed  into  written  form  for  data  analysis.  “Member  check”  was
applied by sending the transcripts back to the participants to allow them
to check to ensure that their responses accurately reflected their inten-
ded meanings [60], which could enhance the credibility of the findings.

3.3. Data collection and analysis

Ethical approval had been obtained from the Research Ethics Com-
mittee at [university name] before the research was conducted. Infor-
mation  sheet  including  the  research  purposes,  confidentiality,  and
anonymity, the benefits and risks of the research, was provided to the
participants. Informed consent was obtained from the participants prior
to their participation.

Questionnaires were administered to students through Wenjuanxing,
a well-recognized online survey platform in mainland China. After de-
leting  the  incomplete  responses,  there  were  a  total  of  337  valid  re-
sponses.  For  the  quantitative  data  collected  from  the  questionnaire,
mean,  standard  deviation,  and  Pearson  correlation  analysis  were
calculated  utilizing  SPSS  29.0.  Model  6  in  PROCESS  version  3.5  was
used to examine the mediating effect, with the 95 % confidence interval
for parameter estimation derived from 5000 bootstrap samples.

Furthermore,  the  qualitative  data  obtained  from  the  open-ended
question  and  follow-up  semi-structured  interviews  were  analyzed
using Braun and Clarke’ s [61] six-phase framework for thematic anal-
ysis.  Specifically,  the  two  researchers  carefully  read  and  re-read  the
interview  transcripts  multiple  times,  and  Chinese  transcripts  were
manually translated into English. This allowed the researcher to gain a
deep familiarity with the data. Later, the two researchers undertook the
coding of the transcripts, and numerous codes were constructed from the
data. These codes were then inductively organized and categorized into
different  themes.  Regular  group  meetings  were  arranged  among  the
researchers, where the themes and codes were subsequently reviewed
and refined to ensure the consistency between the codes and themes.
Then, the researchers carefully named each theme. Finally, the datasets
were re-evaluated to determine the most typical quotes for each theme
to be illustrated in the findings.

4. Results

4.1. Common method bias test

Harman single-factor method was conducted to examine the items of
academic self-efficacy, academic anxiety, performance expectations and
GenAI  reliance.  The  results  indicated  that  four  factors  had  initial  ei-
genvalues greater than 1, collectively accounting for 63.315 % of the

4.2. Validity and reliability of the scale

Cronbach’s α and KMO measure were employed to assess the internal
consistency of the scales. According to Cronbach [63], a Cronbach’s α
coefficient of 0.9 or higher is considered excellent, between 0.7 and 0.9
is good, between 0.6 and 0.7 is acceptable, between 0.5 and 0.6 is poor,
and below 0.5 is unacceptable. The Cronbach’s α  value of the overall
scale was 0.741, and the Cronbach’s α coefficients of four dimensions in
terms of academic self-efficacy, academic anxiety, performance expec-
tations  and  GenAI  reliance  were  0.883,  0.885,  0.858,  and  0.803,
respectively.  All  of  which  were  higher  than  0.7,  indicating  relatively
high internal consistency reliability. Moreover, the overall KMO value
was 0.911>0.9 and p < 0.01, indicating good validity of the question-
naire  [64].  The  KMO  value  of  four  dimensions  in  terms  of  academic
self-efficacy,  academic  anxiety,  performance  expectations  and  GenAI
reliance were 0.852, 0.891, 0.806, and 0.760, respectively. Therefore,
we could conclude that the reliability and validity of this survey ques-
tionnaire was satisfactory.

5. Discussion

The  current  study  investigated  the  relationship  between  academic
self-efficacy  and  GenAI  reliance  through  the  mediation  of  academic
anxiety and  performance expectations. The  study  also  scrutinized  the
use  of  GenAI  by  university  students  in  English  and  Business  English
programs at the undergraduate level, and the potential consequences of
excessive dependence on GenAI.

The results indicated that there was no significant direct correlation
between academic self-efficacy and GenAI reliance, which contradicted
existing  studies  (e.g.  [68–70]).  The  current  study  revealed  that  in-
dividuals with limited academic self-efficacy might not necessarily have
reliance  on  using  GenAI  tools,  whereas  an  indirect  pathway  between
academic  self-efficacy  and  GenAI  reliance  through  the  mediation  of
academic anxiety was found. This suggests that Students who possess
lower levels of academic self-efficacy tend to experience higher levels of
academic anxiety and consequently rely more on GenAI tools, which is
consistent with the viewpoint of Kwak et al. [71].

Moreover, this study found that academic self-efficacy had no sig-
nificant impact on the performance expectations of GenAI, while there
was  a  significant  correlation  between  performance  expectations  and
GenAI reliance. Almassaad et al.’s [72] and Khlaif et al.’s [73] studies
indicated  that  both  instructors  and  students  generally  believed  that
GenAI tools were helpful for students’ learning progress and academic
performance due to their powerful affordance, so they were willing to
seek support from GenAI tools. This can be supported by the results of
current study, which has shown that over 80 % of the participants used
GenAI tools to assist their regular learning (see Table 1). Further, the
current study found that students with high performance expectations
tended to seek external support to reach their expectations. It was not
difficult  to  see  that  GenAI  had  been  regarded  as  an  effective  tool  for
assisting  students’  learning.  This  finding  aligned  with  the  study  of
Shahzad et  al. [74]. They highlighted the importance of performance
expectations  in  predicting  learners’  learning  behaviors,  e.g.,  the
dependence on GenAI tools.

Further analysis suggested that academic self-efficacy indirectly in-
fluences students’  GenAI reliance through the sequential mediation of
academic anxiety and performance expectations. This mechanism indi-
cated that students with low self-efficacy were more likely to experience
academic  anxiety,  which  in  turn  increased  their  expectations  for

However, even though GenAI can be used to help students with their
learning in various ways, it is imperative for both teachers and students
to be cautious of the potential risks related to excessive reliance on these
tools [12]. According to the feedback from participants in this study on
the open-ended question, it has been identified that excessive reliance
on  GenAI  would  bring  various  negative  consequences,  including  de-
creases in cognitive ability (as shown in independent thinking ability,
innovative thinking ability, and critical thinking ability), inaccurate or
harmful content, and plagiarism. The empirical evidence found in the
current  study aligns  with  previous studies  regarding  the  negative  im-
plications of GenAI reliance (e.g., [18,17]). Therefore, some scholars (e.
g.,  [75,76])  have  emphasized  the  urgent  need  for  teachers  to  guide
students to use GenAI tools reasonably, ethically, and critically, which
needs to be further studied in future research.

While there are potential drawbacks to excessive reliance on GenAI
tools, their benefits in teaching and learning should not be overlooked.
Recent empirical evidence highlights the positive effects of integrating
GenAI  tools  from  various  perspectives  (e.g.,  [77–79]).  For  instance,
Wang and Xue [78] examined the impact of GenAI tools on academic
engagement  among  EFL  students  at  a  Chinese  university.  The  experi-
mental group, which utilized GenAI-assisted instruction, demonstrated
significantly  enhanced  behavioral,  cognitive,  and  emotional  engage-
ment compared to the control group that received traditional instruc-
tion.  This  underscores  the  effective  role  of  GenAI  in  fostering
engagement in EFL contexts. Furthermore, Yin et al. [79] conducted a
longitudinal  quasi-experimental  study  to  assess  the  effects  of
GenAI-generated formative feedback on intrinsic motivation, cognitive
load, and learning performance among 173 Chinese undergraduates in
law, international economics and trade, and economics. Their findings
revealed that students’  intrinsic motivation and learning performance
improved,  while cognitive load  decreased after receiving GenAI  feed-
back on their learning tasks.

Given the growing application of GenAI in education and its positive
impact on teaching and learning discussed above, it is not advisable to
restrict  students  from  using  these  tools.  However,  it  is  crucial  to  be
mindful  of  the  potential  negative  consequences  of  over-reliance.
Therefore,  implementing  strategies  to  guide  students  in  using  GenAI
tools in a more appropriate and critical manner is essential. In response
to the negative consequences and concerns of GenAI reliance, the cur-
rent study revealed that students suggested many coping strategies that
they currently employ or expected their teachers to use in the future to
help them avoid over-reliance on these tools. Students recognized that it
is imperative that they prioritize independent learning while utilizing
GenAI tools as supplementary aids. Independent learning offers learners
the  opportunity  to  actively  engage  with  content,  which  is  vital  for
fostering  students’  critical  thinking,  self-discipline,  and  a  deeper  un-
derstanding of subject knowledge [80]. Meanwhile, GenAI tools serve as
valuable resources that can enhance the self-directed learning journey
by  providing  diverse perspectives,  efficient  information  retrieval, and
personalized support [7]. Therefore, it is crucial that students maintain
agency  over  the  utilization  of  GenAI,  using  AI-generated  content  to
complement rather than dictate their learning processes.

Moreover, students recommended that they should prioritize guid-
ance from peers and teachers over that provided by GenAI. While GenAI
tools  can  serve  as  valuable  supplements  and  offer  quick  access  to  in-
formation  and  alternative  viewpoints,  they  should  not  supplant  the
invaluable  human  guidance,  as  students’  interaction  with  peers  or
teachers can offer nuanced insights and support that technology cannot
fully  replicate  [81].  Teachers,  with  their  expertise  and  experience,
provide contextually rich explanations and mentorship that can inspire

and motivate students in ways that algorithmic responses may lack [81].
Meanwhile, peer collaboration could encourage the exchange of diverse
perspectives and ideas, which is conducive to enhancing their critical
thinking and problem-solving abilities. By prioritizing human guidance,
students can not only develop a deeper understanding of the discipline
knowledge  but  also  enhance  their  ability  to  navigate  complex  social
interactions [82], preparing them for the multifaceted challenges of the
real world.

In addition, it is crucial for teachers to foster students’ critical and
ethical awareness of GenAI use to mitigate the risk of over-reliance on
these tools. As GenAI tools become increasingly integrated into students’
learning process, students should be equipped with the awareness and
ability to critically evaluate the information and guidance these tools
provide, which could prevent students from using inaccurate and biased
information [83]. Teachers are advised to guide students to question and
verify  the  accuracy,  relevance,  and  bias  of  GenAI-generated  content
during the teaching and learning process.

According to the  findings of the current study, some practical im-
plications  are  proposed.  Through  understanding  the  psychological
constructs influencing GenAI reliance, it is suggested that program di-
rectors and teachers should employ some strategies to develop students’
academic self-efficacy, alleviate their academic anxiety, and guide them
to have an appropriate level of performance expectations so as to opti-
mize the use of GenAI and mitigate their over-reliance on these tools. For
example,  program  directors  should  schedule  regular  workshops  to
cultivate  students’  essential  skills  such  as  academic  writing,  critical
thinking,  and  independent  research  [84].  These  workshops  would
empower students to learn autonomously and enhance their academic
self-efficacy, subsequently reducing their dependence on Generative AI
tools. Besides, teachers are advised to design assignments that align with
students’  current  abilities.  This  could  effectively  alleviate  the  anxiety
caused by overly challenging tasks so that students feel more at ease and
confident  in  completing  assignments  independently  [85].  Ultimately,
their tendency to rely excessively on Generative AI tools out of anxiety
can be reduced. Furthermore, teachers are encouraged to organize more
peer learning activities where students can collaborate with their peers
to  tackle  academic  challenges  [86,87].  This  could  effectively  reduce
students’ academic anxiety and boost their self-efficacy in their ability to
address  complex  learning  tasks.  Additionally,  teachers  should  offer
timely positive feedback when students successfully complete learning
tasks on their own [88]. Recognizing their progress and achievements
made  through  personal  effort  can  bolster  students’  academic
self-efficacy  and  decrease  anxiety  [88],  ultimately  diminishing  their
reliance on GenAI. Moreover, teachers should also guide students to set
realistic  and  achievable  academic  goals  that  align  with  their  current
abilities  [89,22].  This  could  allow  students  to  achieve  their  goals
through  personal  efforts  with  less  reliance  on  GenAI  tools  and  help
prevent discouragement from overly challenging objectives [22].

Teachers  are  also  advised  to  guide  students  to  use  GenAI  tools
appropriately and critically during their teaching practices. They should
inform students of the importance and necessity of evaluating the ac-
curacy  of  the  GenAI-generated  texts  by  using  their  own  discipline
knowledge  and  information  from  other  sources  before  utilizing  the
content generated by GenAI for their tasks. In addition, regular GenAI
workshops can be arranged by the school to teach students about the
benefits as well as limitations of GenAI-assisted learning and elaborate
on the school’s policies on GenAI use. This could help students have a
more  comprehensive  understanding  of  GenAI  tools,  facilitating  the
appropriate use of these tools during the learning process.

6. Conclusion and future directions

This  study  scrutinized  the  relationship  between  academic  self-
efficacy, academic anxiety, performance expectations, and GenAI reli-
ance and explored the negative impacts of students’  excessive depen-

Although  GenAI tools  can offer various support  to  facilitate students’

The findings of this study offer substantial theoretical and practical
insights. To the best of our knowledge, it is among the first to investigate
the underlying mechanisms behind GenAI reliance and its consequences.
This research explored how academic anxiety and performance expec-
tations serve as consecutive mediators in the relationship between ac-
ademic self-efficacy and GenAI reliance. By examining these mediating
effects, the study enriches the existing literature by providing a detailed
understanding of how these factors interact with academic self-efficacy
to  influence  students’  dependence  on  GenAI.  Moreover,  the  study
highlights  the  potential  negative  outcomes  of  excessive  reliance  on
GenAI tools for learning, emphasizing the need for educators to guide
students toward more balanced usage. This guidance can help maximize
the benefits of GenAI while minimizing dependency. Accordingly, the
study, in the Discussion section, offers practical strategies for reducing
reliance on GenAI in light of learners’ psychological constructs.

The current study is limited in several aspects. First, the qualitative
analysis of this study is only based on self-reported data from an open-
ended survey question, which may affect the reliability of the results.
Therefore, it is recommended that future studies collect more qualitative
data through conducting follow-up interviews to help researchers have
an  in-depth  understanding  of  students’  GenAI  reliance.  Second,  this
study focuses on students from one Chinese university, which may limit
the generalizability of the findings. Therefore, more participants from
different educational institutions should be included in future studies to
address this issue. In addition to academic self-efficacy, academic anx-
iety, and performance expectations, other factors such as socio-cultural
influences,  individual  cognitive  styles,  motivation,  habits,  and  educa-
tional policies may also impact students’ use of GenAI tools. Therefore,
future research is advised to include more variables to reveal the un-
derlying mechanism of GenAI reliance. Third, in this study, the open-
ended question on the survey only explored the negative consequences
of GenAI use. Therefore, it is advised that future research explore the
positive influences of using GenAI to have a more holistic understanding
of this issue. Besides, the cross-sectional nature of the data collected in
this  study poses a  limitation in  establishing clear  causal relationships
between  the  variables. To  address this,  longitudinal  methods  are  rec-
ommended to be adopted in future studies. This allows for the obser-
vation of changes and developments over time, which could provide a
more comprehensive understanding of the causal relationships between
the  variables.  Another  limitation  of  this  study  is  the  exclusive  use  of
GPT-4.0  as  the  GenAI  tool  among  participants,  since  it  was  recom-
mended by the program directors and the institution’s AI policy. While
this allows for a controlled examination of its effects on academic self-
efficacy,  academic  anxiety,  and  performance  expectations,  it  may
restrict  the  generalizability  of  the  findings  to  other  GenAI  tools.
Different  GenAI  models,  such  as  Gemini,  KIMI,  or  DeepSeek,  might
possess  distinct  features  and  strengths  that  may  influence  these  psy-
chological  constructs  differently.  Therefore,  future  research  should
consider incorporating a variety of GenAI tools to examine how specific
features  and  functionalities  might  variably  affect  students’  learning
experiences and psychological outcomes.
