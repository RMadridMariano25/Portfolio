OPEN Effects of ChatGPT on students’
academic performance in Pakistan
higher education classrooms
Muhammad Azeem Ashraf1, Jan Alam2 & Usama Kalim3
The rapid integration of cutting-edge technology is significantly transforming the higher education
landscape. ChatGPT’s groundbreaking technology has provided numerous advantages for higher
education. This study explored students’ behavioral intentions to utilize ChatGPT as a learning
resource in Pakistani universities and its effect on their academic performance. To accomplish this,
the study employed a quantitative descriptive approach to examine the impact of various factors on
students’ behavioral intentions, actual behavior, and academic performance. Reflective measurement
criteria were adhered to in the development of all indicators and constructs. The study utilized the PLS-
SEM to examine the relationships between variables. The findings indicate that ChatGPT platforms
have been beneficial in enhancing students’ academic performance within the framework of Pakistani
higher education. Behavioral intention serves as the driving force behind students’ actual behaviors,
with significant influences from habit and hedonic motivation. These findings are encouraging for
fostering a positive perception of ChatGPT usage for learning.
Keywords Artificial intelligence, ChatGPT, Higher education, TAM model, Higher education institutions
OpenAI created ChatGPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer), which is a language model based on the GPT-
3 architecture designed for interpretation and generation. ChatGPT can be used for natural language processing
tasks including chatbots, automated assistants, and customer service, allowing it to comprehend and generate
text like humans1. According to Cheung et al.2, modern technology must be integrated into dynamic higher
education environments to foster creative and productive learning for educators, administrators, and students3.
OpenAI’s ChatGPT, an advanced language model, represents a groundbreaking development that can drastically
change our views on academic success and student engagement4. Due to technological advancements, the
distinctions between human and artificial intelligence are rapidly diminishing5. The introduction of AI-driven
chatbots like ChatGPT raises intriguing questions about their transformative effects on learning experiences and
academic outcomes as students navigate the challenges of higher education6,7.
In the past few years, there has been considerable interest in using advanced technology like ChatGPT for
learning and teaching in higher education. It has significant potential to transform how teachers engage with
students and facilitate learning8. Covering a wide range of subject areas, this technology is used to create interactive
classrooms where students receive individualized feedback and support9. ChatGPT enhances communication
in online and hybrid learning environments due to its dynamic nature10, functioning as a virtual assistant
for students by answering questions, providing materials, and assisting with homework11. These attributes of
ChatGPT promote students’ self-directed learning12 and increase their engagement and interaction13. Moreover,
it fosters a more personalized teaching approach by addressing each student’s distinctive learning preferences
and needs14. It has improved students’ critical thinking and problem-solving abilities, leading to higher-order
cognitive skill development13,15.
ChatGPT has influenced higher education teaching and learning styles16, providing prompt support for
assessment and administrative tasks11. Careful consideration must be given to issues such as reluctance to
change, concerns about job security, and the need for training on integrating ChatGPT into the curriculum17.
The incorporation of ChatGPT into university classrooms has significantly changed the educational landscape18.
ChatGPT serves as an innovative tool with tremendous potential to improve the educational process and
tailor teaching to individual student learning styles19. According to Shihab et al.20, ChatGPT fosters a flexible
instructional environment that supports student interaction. Its use makes learning more exploratory and
collaborative, helping students develop deeper interests21.

ChatGPT ensures that students have inclusive accessibility in higher education, supporting communication

students often face hectic schedules and varying time zones. The easy accessibility of ChatGPT makes it an
invaluable resource for students seeking assistance beyond standard university hours24. With ChatGPT, students
can interact at their convenience, fostering a culture of self-directed learning and equipping them with the tools
to take charge of their educational journeys25.
ChatGPT is a helpful addition to free up teachers’ time and energy for higher-order learning objectives
and mentorship, giving prompt feedback11. Teachers can focus on fostering students’ critical thinking and
creativity13. Furthermore, ChatGPT can help with automatic feedback and grading common assignments
through the cooperative synergy between instructors and ChatGPT11. Educating students to meet future
workforce expectations is crucial in an era of fast technology breakthroughs26. Higher education students use
ChatGPT to navigate and utilize AI technology with more excellent proficiency27 to critically assess and use
technology for both academic and professional goals11. Incorporating AI and data-driven insights into various
disciplines is becoming more critical in higher education28. According to Ullah et al.29, using ChatGPT positively
and significantly impacts decision-making, further enhancing financial literacy and promoting market trends.
Given its importance, there is a critical need to develop procedures and policies for training to support the
effective practice of ChatGPT in university settings30.
Limited literature exists on the limitations of ChatGPT, which operates within specific parameters that can
lead to bias. Based on large datasets sourced from the internet, ChatGPT may produce inherent biases in its
results31. Ethically, ChatGPT can pose problems, such as concerns regarding users’ data privacy. It lacks a moral
and legal accountability mechanism when using decision-making tools32. ChatGPT presents inconsistencies
and inaccuracies as it generates knowledge hallucinations with incorrect facts33. Furthermore, according to
Petrič34, excessive use of ChatGPT impacts social communication and socialization due to the lack of common
benchmarks for efficiency and effectiveness. After discussing both the merits and demerits of ChatGPT, there is
a pressing need to examine its impacts on students’ academic performance in university classrooms. ChatGPT
has the capabilities and facilitates students’ access to learning and information11, but a gap still exists regarding
students’ academic performance35. Rasul et al.36 indicated the benefits and risks linked with using ChatGPT
in higher education. Another study37 explained its effectiveness in students’ learning compared to traditional
methods, but the implications of ChatGPT on academic performance remain unresolved38.
In justifying the study, research indicates that higher education institutions in Pakistan encounter challenges
such as resource scarcity, student-faculty ratios, and limited access to quality materials, leading to the reliance
on ChatGPT39. Many students struggle to enhance their learning through traditional rote methods. Instead, they
turn to ChatGPT for inquiry-based learning, as it provides a foundation for easier reading and understanding40.
The current study is designed to fill the gap and evaluate the effects of ChatGPT on the academic performance
of higher education students. The main objective of the study is to examine how students use ChatGPT and its
effect on academic performance in higher education classrooms.

Research methodology
The study adopted a quantitative descriptive approach for collecting information. Online survey using
Google Forms was used to collect the data for the study from 352 higher education students across Pakistan
from November 2023 to January 2024. The questionnaire was divided into two parts. The first part gathered
detailed data about students’ demographic information, while the second part collected scientific data on their
experiences using a five-point Likert Scale that ranged from ‘Strongly Disagree’ to ‘Strongly Agree’. Performance
expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitation conditions, price value, hedonic motivation, habit,
behavioral intention, and actual use of behavior were included as independent variables in the questionnaire.
However, students’ academic performance was identified as a dependent variable. The selection of variables and
indicators from TAM followed the approach of Foroughi et al.81.
The collected data was analyzed statistically using the PLS-SEM (Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling) with SmartPLS4, which is a vital tool for examining key predictors of the variable outcomes. Compared
to CB-SEM, PLS-SEM provides higher item loadings, average variance extraction, and composite reliability.
PLS-SEM consistently employs a confirmatory approach instead of an exploratory one82. For developing the
model, a reflective construct was followed in the format outlined by Ma et al.83. The study presented hypotheses
within a theoretical framework supported by literature, which is echoed in the current study84. For data
analysis, the format utilized by Ashraf et al.85 was followed. In the study, the measurement model assessed factor
loadings, Cronbach’s alpha, Composite Reliability (CR), Average Variance Extraction (AVE), Variance Inflation
Factors (VIF), Mean, and Standard Deviation (SD). The Fornell-Larcker Criterion was employed to determine
discriminant validity. The structural model was implemented to find the effects of ChatGPT on academic
performance, noting differences based on demographic information.
Participants
Table 1 displays the participants’ demographic information. Among the 352 participants, 167 (47.4%) were male,
and 185 (52.6%) were female. The majority of the participants were undergraduate (BS) students, with most
falling within the 18–24 age group (296, 84.1%), while the remaining participants were enrolled in master’s
and doctoral programs. Regarding family residence, 194 participants (55.1%) were from urban areas, while 158
(44.9%) were from rural areas. Participants at both public and private universities took part in the study, with
225 (63.9%) from public institutions and 127 (36.1%) from private institutions. The majority of the participants
were from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (139) and Punjab (136), with the rest belonging to other provinces of Pakistan.

Ethical consideration
Proper ethical standards were maintained throughout the study, in accordance with the guidelines provided by
the National Commission for the Protection of Human Subjects of Biomedical and Behavioral Research (1978).
All participants received comprehensive information regarding the study’s goals, the voluntary nature of their
involvement, and their right to withdraw at any moment without any consequences. A formal consent form was
provided, ensuring that participants had all the necessary information and willingly agreed to participate in the
study. To protect participants’ privacy and confidentiality, pseudonyms were used instead of their real names.
Additionally, both male and female participants were included and treated with consideration for their individual
needs throughout the research process. The Ethical Committee at the University of Wah, Pakistan granted ethical
approval for this study. The research complied with all applicable ethical guidelines and regulations concerning
studies involving human participants86.

usage of ChatGPT positively impacted their academic performance. Behavioral intention serves as the driving
force behind students’ actual behaviors, with habit and hedonic motivation identified as the primary factors
influencing these intentions. The results indicate that habit and hedonic motivation are significant predictors
of students’ intentions in utilizing ChatGPT for learning purposes. Habits, shaped by cognitive and behavioral

Theoretical and practical implications
The study advances theoretical understandings by showing how TAM can be applied to emerging technologies,
such as ChatGPT, in university settings. It links established premises of behavioral intentions to enrich the
understanding of students’ adoption and interaction with generative technologies. The findings provide new
evidence to the growing literature on the connection between emerging technologies and education by exploring
how ChatGPT influences university students’ academic performance. It proposes an understanding of how
technological tools are associated with intellectual and motivational learning theories. The study also advances
theories related to technology-mediated learning, showing how tools like ChatGPT affect knowledge acquisition
and problem-solving skills.

The study also provides practical implications by identifying how ChatGPT can be incorporated into learning
practices to improve academic performance. The findings emphasize the necessity for institutions and teachers
to train students on the effective use of generative technologies like ChatGPT, ensuring they recognize its
abilities, restrictions, and ethical considerations. It could assist as a valuable resource for students who struggle
academically, offering targeted support to close learning gaps. On the other hand, it could also cause unpleasant or
prejudiced tools for students to find easy ways to learn or finish their assigned tasks. Thus, the findings are helpful
to institutions in developing guidelines for the sensible and accountable application of generative technologies
in academics, balancing innovation with concerns regarding academic integrity and excessive dependence on
emerging technologies. In addition, integrating generative technologies like ChatGPT into teaching and learning
requires careful rethinking of curriculum and instruction methods, stressing collaboration between emerging
technological tools and human learning rather than seeing them as separate entities.
Limitations and future directions
The present study is limited in scope as it primarily focuses on students’ perceptions and employs the TAM to
analyse their interactions with ChatGPT. While this approach offers valuable insights into students’ acceptance
and perceived usefulness of the tool, it fails to capture other important dimensions of their learning experiences.
Future research can explore a broader range of factors, such as students’ creativity, satisfaction, and motivation
after engaging with ChatGPT. Investigating how the tool influences creative problem-solving, knowledge
retention, and overall academic engagement could offer a thorough understanding of its impact. Additionally,
examining the potential challenges or drawbacks associated with ChatGPT use, such as excessive reliance on
AI-generated responses or its effect on critical thinking abilities, would further contribute to the literature.
Furthermore, although the TAM is widely utilized for evaluating technology adoption, future research could
explore alternative theoretical models to gain a deeper understanding of students’ behavioral and cognitive
responses to AI-driven learning tools. For instance, models such as the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use
of Technology, the Expectation-Confirmation Model, or the Self-Determination Theory could provide nuanced
perspectives on user engagement, long-term acceptance, and intrinsic motivation. Further research could
also involve longitudinal studies to examine the sustained impact of ChatGPT over time, cross-disciplinary
investigations to explore its effectiveness in different subject areas, and experimental designs comparing AI-
enhanced learning with traditional instructional methods. Additionally, qualitative approaches, such as
interviews and focus groups, could provide richer, in-depth perspectives on students’ experiences, illuminating
the subjective and contextual factors that shape their perceptions and learning outcomes. By focusing on these
areas, future research can enhance our overall understanding of how ChatGPT and similar AI-driven tools
influence educational experiences. This, in turn, will provide valuable insights for educators, policymakers, and
technology developers on best practices for incorporating AI in learning environments.
